## 4. THE MECHANISM OF OUR LOSSES

### 4.1 It is not tempo, and it is not core-defence

* **Not tempo**: our death round in losses (r191) and our kill round in wins (r198)
  are the same clock, and game length is 198 vs 199 median between wins and losses.
* **Not a specific core-kill pattern**: they never melee our core (0/126) and never
  launch. Our core dies to **turret fire from the forward picket**, which is the
  only remaining channel.

### 4.2 It is ECONOMY — but the discriminator is EXPANSION, not attrition

This is the correction that matters, and I got it wrong on the first pass.

**Attrition looks like the story until you compute the wins/losses split:**

```
                     built/g   lost/g   loss rate     (n=126 games)
US   vs HTTP 418      6.37      2.50       39%
US   vs FIELD         6.06      1.21       20%     <- our field control, n=2,165
THEM vs us            9.10      0.13        1%
FIELD opponents       4.62      0.04        1%
```
They **double our harvester loss rate versus the field** (39% vs 20%, median death
round r79 vs r93) while keeping their own at the field-normal 1%. That is real and
it is opponent-induced.

**But it is not what separates our wins from our losses:**

```
                    US harvesters BUILT   US harvesters LOST   loss rate
our WINS   (n=46)          8.13/g               2.43/g           30%
our LOSSES (n=80)          5.36/g               2.54/g           47%
```
**The absolute number we LOSE is flat (2.43 vs 2.54). The number we BUILD collapses
(8.13 → 5.36).** Game length is identical (198 vs 199), so this is not a length
artefact. Tertiles on our early (≤r120) harvester deaths do **not** order our win
rate (38.1% / 28.6% / 42.9%) — losing harvesters early does not predict losing.

⇒ **THE MECHANISM: in the games we lose, we never build the economy in the first
place.** Their forward picket — 5.21 gunners/game planted at d²≈41 of our core from
median r130, plus 5.07 near-core builds/game from median r40 — **suppresses our
EXPANSION**, and the harvesters we already own dying is a smaller, roughly constant
tax on top.

**⭐ INDEPENDENTLY CONFIRMED by a cut that was aimed at something else entirely.**
§3bis.4 set out to test whether their CPU timeouts help us. It found they do not —
and in doing so it identified **our own early entity count as the one variable that
predicts our result at every stratum of their behaviour**:

```
their TLE LOW : our early entities low -> our WR 14.3%   high -> 28.6%
their TLE MID :                           38.1%                  52.4%
their TLE HIGH:                           33.3%                  52.4%
```
**Three strata, same direction, ~+15 to +19pp each** (n=42 per stratum, r0-150 only,
one cell per game). This was computed from `events.tsv` entity counts with no
reference to harvesters, while §4.2 was computed from harvester BUILD events with no
reference to entity counts. **Two independent routes, same answer: our early
build-out is the discriminator.**

The downstream chain is fully measured and every link has its number:

```
their forward picket (5.21 gunners/g in our half, first incursion r40)
  -> our expansion is suppressed  (8.13 -> 5.36 harvesters built/game)
  -> our titanium collapses       (median ti_collected 1650 on wins -> 455 on losses;
                                   theirs 1275 -> 1865, i.e. it moves the OTHER way)
  -> we cannot buy ammunition     (median ammo converted US 78 vs THEM 256)
  -> our sentinels cannot fire    (US 20-22 shots/band vs THEM 72-85)
  -> their picket walks in and our core dies at r191.
```

### 4.3 What the data CANNOT distinguish

**Reverse causation on the expansion gap is not excluded.** "We are winning,
therefore we expand" fits the 8.13-vs-5.36 split as well as "we expand, therefore we
win". The temporal ordering helps a little — our harvesters die at median r79, long
before the median core death at r191 — but ordering is not causation, and the
losses-tertile null (§4.2) actively weakens the attrition half of the story.
**Resolving this needs an intervention, not another cut.** That is the point of §7.

**The seat effect has no mechanism.** §2.1 and §2.3 establish that seat b is where
we lose and that our forward arrival is ~20 rounds late there, but their behaviour
is seat-insensitive on every measure I computed (their arrival r41/r37; their deep
builds 4.11/g in seat-a games vs 5.77/g in seat-b games; their harvester loss rate
1% in both). **The asymmetry is on OUR side and I could not localise it to a
behaviour.** Named here as an open question, not sold as a finding.

## 5. EXPLOITABILITY UNDER THE APPROVED CLASS

### 5.1 Is v103 kidnappable? YES on surface, and they cannot answer it

The requirement is our launcher within **d² ≤ 2 of an enemy builder**. Every
`BUILD` event they emit implies one of their builders stood **orthogonally
adjacent** to that tile on that round. Measured over 126 games:

* **5.07 builds/game land inside our near-core band**, in **84% of games**
  (106/126), starting at **median round 40**.
* **2.35 builds/game land at d² ≤ 8 of our core** — inside our core's own action
  radius. Composition across the set: barrier 145, gunner 82, sentinel 36.
* Their builders' median death position is **d² = 10 from OUR core** — they die
  deep in our base, in ours (median r108–131).
* **They build 0.00 launchers in 126 games** (field opponents: 0.62/game), so a
  launcher we station at home faces **no counter-kidnap and no evidence they have
  ever been on the receiving end of one.**

**A launcher parked in our own near-core band would have an enemy builder in
pickup range in the large majority of games, from roughly round 40.**

**Our incumbent cannot take that shot.** `bots/_v223sealrepair` gates the launcher
at `LAUNCHER_MIN_RND = 160` (`doctrine.py:1536`) — **120 rounds after their median
first incursion, and 30 rounds after their median gunner plant.** We built 0.56
launchers/game at median round **r205**. See the `GREP:` block in §7.

### 5.2 Is v103 CRASHABLE by border-throw? The direct evidence says NO

`corpus/throws.tsv` records **14 throws in these 126 games, all by us** (their
launcher count is zero, so no throw of ours is theirs). Nine are `EXILE` — us
throwing **their** builder. **Two of those landed on a genuine map-border tile**
(`border=1`, and `is_border` has a driven selftest):

```
6d97072b_game_4 (30x30)  r61  EXILE  their bot id 3 -> (20,29)  border=1
6d97072b_game_4 (30x30)  r65  EXILE  their bot id 3 -> (20,29)  border=1
```

**The same entity id was picked up and thrown again four rounds later.** Entity ids
are unique, so **that builder survived the border landing and was still alive,
mobile and adjacent to our launcher at r65.** (`life` reads −1 on these rows and is
**not** read — it is never computed for non-INSERT kinds.) The same bot id 3 was
also exiled three times in `6d97072b_game_2` (r26, r39, r85) to an interior tile and
survived each.

⇒ **v103 guards its off-map queries. Border-throw crash-induction is a NEGATIVE
against this opponent.** n = 2 border throws in 1 game — **thin, and I say so** —
but it is direct, on-engine evidence pointing one way, and it is the only such
evidence there is.

**The kidnap itself still works** (their builder was displaced repeatedly, and they
have no launcher). It is a **denial** lever, not a **crash** lever.

### 5.3 The crash census — a soft positive I am deliberately not selling

`tools/crash_census.py` counts `removeEntity` events on entities that never had an
`updateHp` event, for code-running kinds. **It is `crash_candidate`, not
confirmed-crash**: the wire format has no event for `self_destruct()`, `destroy()`
or `resign()`, so a vanished turret is crash-or-friendly-teardown and the tool says
so itself.

```
HTTP 418 v103 vs us       n=126 games, 32,611 rounds:  329 candidates = 2.61/game
                                                        (76% of games have >=1)
FIELD baseline (seeded random 126 of our other games
  in the same window, 28 opponents)                  :   90 candidates = 0.71/game
                                                        (17% of games)
HTTP 418 v103 vs OTHER league teams  n=112 games     :  354 candidates = 3.16/game
                                                        (77% of games) — REPLICATES
Our own side, both samples                           :    0
```
**3.2–3.7× the field baseline, replicating against opponents that are not us.**

**Kind breakdown (both HTTP 418 fixtures): gunner 655, sentinel 28, launcher 0,
builder_bot 0, core 0.** Only **ammo-spending** turret types ever vanish; the
ammo-free launcher and the never-firing builder/core never do. That is a precise
fit for the self-audit hazard in `CLAUDE.md`: **`can_fire` returns TRUE at 0 ammo,
and the check lives in `finish_firing_turret@0x26eac`, which RAISES — destroying
the turret.** A bot that fires without reading `get_global_ammo()` deletes exactly
its gunners, exactly this way.

**I ran two discriminators and NEITHER confirmed it:**

1. **Teardown-and-advance hypothesis** (they demolish and re-plant a creeping
   picket, which is free and would explain everything innocently): pair each of
   their gunner deaths with the next gunner build within 15 rounds — **171 pairs
   from 482 deaths (35%), median gap 3 rounds, and the replacement is CLOSER to our
   core in only 65/171 = 38%**, i.e. below chance. Their picket's normalised
   distance to our core does not march inward across round bands
   (0.125 / 0.130 / 0.238 / 0.176 / 0.091). **No systematic advance ⇒ the innocent
   explanation is not supported either.**
2. **0-ammo hypothesis**, cut on their end-of-band ammo balance:
   ```
   ammo_end == 0   17 cells   2.29 gunner deaths/cell   3.47 builds/cell
   ammo_end 1-9    98 cells   1.57                      2.50
   ammo_end 10-49 197 cells   1.47                      1.79
   ```
   The raw gradient is there but **the death/build ratio runs the wrong way**
   (0.66 / 0.63 / **0.82**). `ammo_end` is an end-of-band snapshot and a bot can
   hit 0 mid-band and recover, so **this test is underpowered rather than
   refuting** — but it does not corroborate.

⇒ **Status: a real, replicated, gunner-specific anomaly with a mechanism that fits
and two failed confirmations. Open, not banked, not a plank.** Settling it needs
an engine-side read of a single replay (which entity, which round, what its ammo
was) — cheap, and worth a QUEUE row of its own, but it is not the plank below.
