## 5. ⛔ DISCLOSURE — I RAN A BROKEN READ FIRST AND SAW ITS OUTPUT

**Published rather than quietly re-run, because the alternative is a look nobody can audit.**

**The bug was mine, in ad-hoc glue around the certified module, not in the module.**
`meta_join`'s `us_side` column is **lowercase `'a'/'b'`**; my first attempt compared it to
`'A'`. Every row therefore took the else-branch, so:
* "our version" resolved to the **opponent's** version on the 45 `a`-side games — producing a
  nonsense histogram (`49, 13, 58, 226, 21`);
* "opponent" resolved to **`OpenSverige` — ourselves** — on those same games, collapsing them
  into a phantom cell and leaving **two** real cells empty.

**What I saw before the fix:** an invalid per-cell table (`0033` 3/10, `Jython` 3/10,
`Big O` 3/5, `team lazy` 5/5 — all partial `b`-side subsets), an invalid `below 2 / above 1`,
and a **P4 verdict of DOES NOT FIRE**. ⚠ **I also saw the pooled P1 — and P1 is
UNAFFECTED by the bug** (`our_won` summed over all 75 games does not depend on side
resolution): **34/75 = 45.3% in both runs, identical.**

**WHY THIS DOES NOT CONSTITUTE A SECOND LOOK, stated so it can be disputed rather than
assumed:** the fix is forced, not chosen — a case-sensitive string comparison has exactly one
correct form — and the analysis was pinned in a certified function before either run.
**No analytic degree of freedom was exercised between the two runs.** *(That the two P4
verdicts agree is a coincidence and is NOT offered as evidence that the first run was
harmless.)*

⭐ **AND THE STRONGER GROUND, supplied by the side lane's certification and adopted here
because it is better than my own argument.** The paragraph above defends the **FIX**, while
the live hazard is about the **TRIGGER**: **ASYMMETRIC DEBUGGING** — an author who dislikes a
verdict hunts for a bug and one who likes it does not, so *"I found a bug and fixed it"* can
smuggle in outcome-dependent effort **even when every step of the fix is forced.** A
forced-fix argument does not reach that.

**What closes it is a fact already sitting in this disclosure: the bug announced itself
through a DOMAIN VIOLATION, not through an unwelcome number.** The broken run resolved
**`OpenSverige` — ourselves — as our own opponent**, and produced an "our version" histogram
of `49, 13, 58, 226, 21`. **Neither of those is *surprising*; both are ILLEGAL.** A bug hunt
triggered by an illegal value is **outcome-independent by construction — it fires identically
had the broken verdict read FIRES.**

⇒ **the durable rule, which is more useful than this instance: build guards that fail on
IMPOSSIBLE values, not on implausible ones.** An impossible-value guard cannot be
outcome-selective, so it is the only kind whose firing is evidence about the analyst as well
as about the data.

**THE DURABLE FINDING, and it is about the instrument's boundary:** `cal8_read.py` certifies
`accepted_match_ids`, `panel_games`, `v125_reference`, `p4` and `half_width` — **but the
cell-assignment and holder-guard steps were left to hand-written glue at read time, and that
is where the error landed.** The certified core was never wrong. ⇒ **an instrument that stops
short of the final join has not been certified end-to-end**, and the uncertified remainder is
reliably the fiddly part. **Both guards are now written down as code with their PASS/FAIL
driven** (`GUARD0` fails on the previous run's data by construction).
