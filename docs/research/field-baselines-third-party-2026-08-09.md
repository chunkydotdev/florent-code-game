# Five field baselines re-derived with us absent — on 4,356 replays our archiver happened to download

**Research arm, session 25, 2026-08-09.** Every "the field does X" number this project
owns was measured on games we played in (corpus trap 4). `replay_archive/` turns out to
carry a `<match-id>.meta.json` sidecar for 98.1% of archived matches, and **4,356 of the
attributed replay files are clean third-party — the field playing itself, us absent.**
This re-derives five pre-registered numbers on that population.

**REVISION 2 (this version).** The first pass counted `opensverige - plan B` as a field
team. It is a **second registration almost certainly of us**, and 45 of its files sat in
the third-party bucket — i.e. "the field playing itself" with one side being us, which
defeats the point of the sample. `meta_attrib.py` now emits a `related` column; the clean
field is `us_side == "none" AND related == "none"`. **§9 gives old → new for all five.
No verdict flipped.** A separate pre-registered control on item 1 (rating-gap matching,
§3) was added at the same time.

**The sampling limit is in the title and is the honest frame for the whole document.**
Third-party games are not a random sample of the league. They are **the matches our
archiver happened to download**, and the teams in them are not rating-matched to the
teams we play (§8: the third-party field side runs p10 1,088 / p90 1,933 against
1,514 / 1,732 for the sides we face). Every headline below is repeated inside rating
bands and, where a team appears in both populations, **within team** — which needs no
rating control at all and is the strongest form available.

**No direction was pre-registered, deliberately.** Two of the five come back CONFIRMED.

## 1. THE RECONCILIATION GATE — both tests, and the negative controls

The standing rule: a new attribution path must pass the same seat/winner reconciliation
`join.tsv` passes before any verdict consumes it, because `teamXRating` looked correct for
a day and turned out to be a live join.

| test | result |
| --- | --- |
| **CHECK 1** — meta-derived seat + win/loss vs `join.tsv`, on every file in both tables | **1,480 / 1,480 agree = 100.0000%** (0 seat, 0 winner disagreements) |
| **CHECK 2** — sidecar `scoreA`/`scoreB` + `winnerId` vs the **`winner` field decoded out of each replay binary**, per match | **1,268 / 1,268 agree = 100.0000%**, 0 untestable |
| negative control `--selftest=seat` | **PASS** — flipping the seat on every row collapses CHECK 1 from 1,480/1,480 to **0/1,480** |
| negative control `--selftest=winner` | **PASS** — same collapse |
| negative control `--selftest=tally` | **PASS** — guards the third-party leg, which CHECK 1 cannot reach |

**Both required tests pass at 100.0000%, and the guards are observed to fail when fed
corrupted input.** A check that reports 100% proves nothing until it has been shown it
*can* report less. The gate is met; nothing below is qualified on attribution.

### THE POPULATION FILTER — and the 100 files it removes

**A clean gate does not give a clean population.** `meta_attrib.py` marks matches
involving `opensverige - plan B` (`b7cafd9f-…`, a second registration almost certainly of
us) with `related`, deliberately as a separate column so `us_side` semantics did not change
under a running analysis. Filtering `related != "none"` removes **100 files / 20 matches**
in two distinct kinds, and **both are excluded here**:

| kind | files | why it is not an observation of the field |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| `us_side == none`, `related` set | **45** | plan B vs a real field team — "the field playing itself" with **one side being us** |
| `us_side` set, `related` set | **55** | our games **against plan B** — us versus us, not a real opponent game |

**Decision, stated because it was mine to make: the 55 are excluded from the vs-us
reference arm too.** A vs-us baseline built partly on self-play would flatter exactly the
quantities under test — plan B garrisons its collar at 72-76% and heals 187-300 times a
game, i.e. it behaves like us, not like the field. **It moved the vs-us field collar
figure by −1.0pp (51.30% → 50.30%), the largest single movement anywhere in the
correction**, and in the direction that makes our own over-garrisoning look *larger*.
Leaving them in would have been a silent thumb on the scale.

`meta_attrib.py` also skips the **24 keeper `_meta_stub` placeholders**
(`{"id":…, "_meta_stub":true}`, every other field null) which a naive filename join would
have attributed to a nameless team on neither seat.

## 3. Q1 CONTROL — is r211 resilience on our side, or mismatch in the third-party pool?

**Pre-registered by the coordinator before these numbers existed**, because the r296 → r211
result is already being read causally — *"the field's clock against us is slow because we
are hard to reach"* — and there is a live competing explanation. We meet **rating-matched**
opponents on the ladder; the third-party pool spans 70 teams with no such constraint, so
**a weak team dying fast to a strong one pulls that median down with nobody rushing.**

**Matching rule:** third-party games whose `|ratingABefore − ratingBBefore|` gap is at or
below the **75th percentile of the gap in our own clean ladder games (66 Elo)** — the
pairing tightness the ladder actually gives us. The gap distributions differ exactly as the
objection predicts:

| \|ratingBefore gap\| | our ladder games | third-party |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| games | 1,868 | 4,356 |
| median | **33** | **60** |
| p75 | **66** | 130 |
| p90 | 144 | 234 |

### The test

| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| field kills our core | ALL, killer ≥1550 | 628 | **r283** | 8% | 21% |
| field kills our core | gap 0-24 | 290 | r294 | 9% | 21% |
| field kills our core | gap 25-49 | 188 | r306 | 6% | 19% |
| field kills our core | gap 50-99 | 138 | r257 | 11% | 24% |
| **third-party** | ALL | 3,060 | **r200** | 17% | 34% |
| **third-party** | **MATCHED (gap ≤66)** | **1,680** | **r215** | 17% | 31% |
| **third-party** | **MATCHED, killer ≥1550** | **1,181** | **r229** | **13%** | 25% |
| third-party | gap 0-24 | 720 | r214 | 18% | 32% |
| third-party | gap 25-49 | 631 | r206 | 19% | 33% |
| third-party | gap 50-99 | 735 | r209 | 16% | 32% |
| third-party | gap 100-199 | 610 | r189 | 16% | 36% |
| third-party | gap 200-399 | 230 | r184 | 17% | 35% |
| third-party | gap ≥400 | 134 | **r158** | 19% | 43% |

Split by whether the killer was the stronger side, the mismatch effect is visible but
small and not monotone: at gap ≥400 the stronger side kills at r162 (N=123) and the weaker
side at r88 (N=11); at gap 0-24 it is r191 stronger / r237 weaker.

> ### The mismatch explanation is REAL but accounts for at most a third of the gap. The causal reading SURVIVES, weakened.
>
> **There is a genuine gradient**: the third-party median climbs from **r158** at gap ≥400
> to **r214** at gap 0-24, and the fully matched, strength-matched cell reaches **r229**.
> So roughly **18-29 rounds of the 72-round gap (r283 − r211) is mismatch**, exactly as the
> objection predicted.
>
> **It does not close the gap.** Under the tightest control available — gap ≤66 *and*
> killer ≥1550, the same conditioning as the against-us figure, N=1,181 — the field still
> kills its own kind at **r229 against r283 for killing us. 54 rounds, ~24%, survives.**
> The r100 share moves the same way (13% matched vs 8% against us).
>
> **What the builder may say, and what it may not.** *"The field's clock against us is
> slower than its clock against comparable opponents"* is supported at N=1,181 with the
> mismatch confound controlled. *"r296 vs r211"* as a headline pair is **not** the
> controlled comparison and should be quoted as **r283 vs r229**; roughly a third of the
> raw difference is the third-party pool being loosely matched, not us being hard to reach.
> The mechanism itself — that we are a slow target — remains an inference, not a
> measurement; §6 shows we sit at 67.2% collar occupancy against the field's 50-53%, which
> is consistent with it but does not establish it.

## 4. Q2 — "we run 1.11:1 damage-to-repair against the field's 2.79:1"

**Reconstruction** (verified against the published figure before use): from `econ.tsv`,
`HP repaired = heals × 4`, `damage capacity = ammo_converted × 1.8 + attacks × 2`.
`econ.shots` and `econ.deliveries` are **dead all-zero columns** and are not touched.

| population | side-games | heals/g | HP repaired/g | ammo Ti/g | dmg capacity/g | **ratio** |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| **us** (replication; published 1.11) | 1,868 | 424.5 | 1,698 | 745.7 | 1,774 | **1.04 : 1** |
| **field, in our games** (replication; published 2.79) | 1,868 | 237.6 | 950 | 1,410.5 | 2,807 | **2.95 : 1** |
| **field, clean third-party** | **8,712** | 197.7 | 791 | 736.2 | 1,548 | **1.96 : 1** |
| third-party, side rated ≥1550 | 4,812 | 195.0 | 780 | 813.0 | 1,662 | **2.13 : 1** |
| third-party, side rated <1550 | 3,900 | 201.0 | 804 | 641.6 | 1,406 | **1.75 : 1** |

The ratio is scale-invariant, so the length control changes nothing (per 100 rounds:
field-vs-us 48.9 heals / 290.4 ammo Ti, field-third-party 41.0 / 152.8 — same 2.95 and
1.96).

**Within-team, across the 41 teams with ≥5 side-games in each cell: median change
+0.08, and 19 of 41 go *down*.** So the pooled revision is **composition, not behaviour**:
the third-party pool contains teams that heal more, not the same teams behaving
differently.

**Except for the teams that matter.** The six opponents the late-game doctrine was written
against (`late-game-doctrine-2026-08-09.md` §2) move a long way, and it survives the
length control:

| | side-games | rounds | heals/100r | ammo Ti/100r | **ratio** |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| the six, **against us** | 768 | 458,603 | 23.3 | 351.7 | **7.48 : 1** |
| the six, **third-party** | 595 | 203,523 | 33.3 | 227.8 | **3.35 : 1** |

Per round lived, those six buy **1.54× more ammunition** and heal **0.70× as much**
against us as they do against each other.

> ### VERDICT: **REVISED.** N = 8,712 third-party side-games.
>
> **The field's damage-to-repair ratio with us absent is 1.96:1, not 2.79:1** — 2.13:1
> restricted to sides rated ≥1550. Our 1.11:1 (replicating at 1.04:1) is therefore
> **1.9× off the field, not 2.5×.**
>
> **But the mechanism is composition for the broad field and behaviour for the top six.**
> Across 41 paired teams the within-team median barely moves (+0.08); across the six
> opponents the doctrine actually targets it halves (7.48 → 3.35). **The claim "the field
> spends its titanium on damage" is a claim about the teams we play, and against them it
> is if anything stronger than published — they tilt toward damage specifically when
> facing us.**

## 10. NON-COVERAGE AND LIMITS

1. **The sampling limit is the whole frame.** Third-party games are what the archiver
   downloaded. Nothing here makes them a random sample of the league, and the coverage
   is uneven — 42-107 matches for the best-covered five teams, single digits in the tail.
   All within-team pairs with <20 games on either side should be read as suggestive.
2. **25 matches / 125 replay files have no usable sidecar** and are unattributed, plus 224
   local arena diagnostics that have no match id at all. 6,324 of 6,449 league-named
   replays (98.06%) are attributed, of which 6,224 survive the `related` filter.
3. **`related` covers exactly one team id.** If another second registration exists that we
   have not identified, it is still in the pool. The check that found plan B was
   behavioural (collar occupancy and heal rate indistinguishable from ours); no systematic
   sweep for others has been run, and that is the obvious next guard.
4. **Q3's attacker bins are not fully comparable across populations.** "3+ attackers on
   the core" selects a different siege shape against us (builder melee, which we run at
   1.9× the field's rate) than in third-party games. The adjacency *within* a bin is a
   fair comparison; the bin membership is not.
5. **Q2's damage capacity is a capacity, not damage dealt.** `ammo_converted × 1.8` assumes
   sentinel efficiency and counts ammunition that was banked and never fired. That is the
   published definition and was kept deliberately so the numbers are comparable; it is not
   a measurement of damage inflicted. `econ.shots` and `econ.deliveries` are dead all-zero
   columns and were not used; shot counts, where needed, come from `build_agg` `metric ==
   'shot'`.
6. **Not stratified by opponent version**, though `meta_join` now carries both versions at
   100% of our files. Field bots changed under us during the archive window and every
   third-party pooled figure mixes versions. Our own side *was* version-stratified for Q4
   (§6), which is where it changed the reading.
7. **Kill round is the round the core entity was removed**, not the round the attack that
   killed it started. Sieges are long; §2's medians are end-of-siege, not
   start-of-commitment.
8. **§3 controls rating gap, not skill shape.** Two teams 20 Elo apart can still be a fast
   bot and a slow one. The control removes the mismatch explanation as the *sole* cause; it
   does not make the comparison experimental.
9. **No claim here is causal.** "CAD garrisons more without us" is a difference between two
   observed populations that also differ in opponent, map draw and rating.
