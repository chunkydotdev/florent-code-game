## (A) WHAT CONVERTED — five preconditions, and only one of them is "attack earlier"

**1. The commit was a CONDITIONAL FALLBACK, fired by a scouting result, never a
plan.** The Kragle's opening was economic with a rush branch: *"in the case that
the soldiers only found ruins that were 'contested,' they would rush"* — and they
state the theory, *"the best counter to rushing is to capture a ruin as fast as
possible. However, if every nearby ruin is contested, rushes become stronger."*
Result: *"This opening strategy was wildly successful on the ladder."* Liquipedia
gives the same shape for SC2 (*"If scouting reveals a fast teching opponent, the
attacker may decide to move for an All-In attack"*), and a published **abort**
rule for the opposite reading. → `rush-as-fallback-when-the-opening-is-denied.md`

**2. The commit was expressed as a PRICE, not a mode — and BC2020's confused
literally used round 250.** *"we implemented a rush cost: if it's still before
round 250 and the rush isn't over"* … *"then everything non-rush gets an
additional rush cost of 250."* The abort was keyed to an artefact, not a clock:
*"our rush miner signals that the rush is over when our design school is
destroyed."* → `the-rush-cost-budget-gate.md`

**3. The attackers were PRODUCED AT THE TARGET, not marched to it.** Every BC2020
rush that killed bases used Battlegaode's shape: one cheap scout, a factory built
beside the enemy HQ, units spawned on site. Three teams describe it independently.
**This is the precondition we structurally lack** — only our core spawns builders,
≤1/turn, on its own ring. → `spawn-the-attack-at-the-target-not-a-march.md`

**4. The strike LANDED LATE, with a bank, after the defensive reflex had been
priced in.** Java Best Waifu solved the BC2020 rush meta with an early drone and
were still beaten by Kryptonite, who *"rushed relatively late"* and *"stacked a lot
of soup in between."* This is the scheduling reading of `KILL_WINDOW_RND: 250`:
the window is 250 rounds, not an instruction to strike at r20. →
`late-rush-beats-the-anti-rush-reflex.md`

**5. The engagement was chosen so it COULD NOT LOSE.** rooklift's Halite II bot
went **1279-176** in 2-player rush games by only occupying tiles where *"at most
one enemy ship can come into range"*, explicitly *"no prediction at all. We prepare
for what the enemy could do."* Our engine exposes this as a first-class predicate:
`can_fire_from(position, direction, turret_type, target)` is the hypothetical form
and ignores ammo and cooldown. → `no-lose-engagement-geometry.md`

**And the survivability precondition that makes the forward shooter live long
enough:** SC2 cannon-rush doctrine walls the cannons off so workers cannot reach
them, leaving one gap for repair. In our ruleset that is unusually strong — enemy
builders can only attack *buildings* and only from an orthogonally adjacent tile,
and **a sentinel's line ignores obstacles, so our own barrier ring does not blind
it** (a gunner ring would). → `wall-off-the-forward-plant-and-leave-a-rebuild-gap.md`

## WHERE THIS CONTRADICTS OR QUALIFIES THE LIBRARY'S STANDING CONTEXT

**One real qualification, and it matters.** Sweep 2's headline is *"mostly you
don't break it — you win on economy,"* with the corollary that *"every
defence-dominant league swept was rescued by a clock, not a tactic."* **That is
not true across Battlecode seasons.** BC2020 and BC2023 were **offence-dominant
seasons in the same engine family as ours**:

- BC2023 don't @ me: *"We found that launcher rushing, where we sent a large amount
  of launchers early in the game to attack the enemy HQ, was a highly effective
  strategy, often deciding the fate of the game in less than 200 rounds"* and
  *"Spamming launchers the moment an HQ could build a launcher was uncounterable."*
- BC2020: rushes dominated a whole tournament phase, and what ended them was a
  **defensive counter-unit plus organiser map choices** — a tactic and a map pool,
  not a clock.

So a deadline attack *can* be the dominant strategy in a Battlecode-family game.
**The precondition in both seasons was the same and we do not have it: the
attacking damage was a CHEAP, MOBILE, CONTINUOUSLY-PRODUCIBLE unit.** Our only
mobile unit deals 2 damage for 2 Ti and **cannot target enemy builders at all**;
all real damage comes from immobile buildings that must be paid for, placed inside
the enemy's kill zone, and cannot retreat. That is the sharpest statement of why
the forward road closed here and stayed open there — and it does not contradict
`THE FORWARD ROAD IS CLOSED`, it explains it.

**One extension, not a contradiction.** The library prices the sentinel file as
*"N=6 against a maxed 2x2 core is a 5.4:1 exchange AGAINST the attacker."* My
arithmetic from the library's own rates (**inference, mine, not sourced**) adds
that because the heal is a per-**round** tax, total ammo spent on a kill *falls*
as the window shortens: against the field's measured 2.68 healers, N=2 ≈ 680 Ti
of ammo over ~68 rounds, N=3 ≈ 465 Ti over ~31, N=4 ≈ 400 Ti over ~20.
**Concentration is cheaper in ammo, not merely faster** — which strengthens the
standing *"concentration, not more damage"* crack rather than weakening it.

**One point that runs the programme's way** and was not previously stated: the
field's standard, documented-as-sufficient anti-rush is *reactive unit
production*, and our engine **rate-caps it at one builder per turn with +20%
scaling**. Turns are not purchasable. That is a real structural opening — bounded
by the fact that the field's actual counter is healing, which is not rate-capped.

**No contradiction found** on: *"the field does not rush"* (the Battlecode record
explains it — four separate seasons converged away from rushing), the 2.2:1 heal
edge, or the r150 breakage.

**A NEW EXTRACTION TRAP, worth adding to the method.** One genuine quote
(BC2023 don't @ me, the *"less than 200 rounds"* line) failed a literal grep
because a **form-feed page-break character (`\f`) sat inside the sentence** and
survived `tr -s ' \n\t'`. The prescribed flatten command does not remove it.
**The flatten step should be `tr -s ' \n\t\f\r' ' '`** — otherwise any quote
spanning a PDF page boundary returns a false negative.
