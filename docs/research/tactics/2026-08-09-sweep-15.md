# Tactics sweep 15 — WHEN TO MAKE FIRST CONTACT, and whether contact is a cause or a marker

**Research arm, 2026-08-09.** Read-only with respect to `bots/` and `tools/`; no arena, no
submissions. Successor to [sweep 14](2026-08-09-sweep-14.md), which asked *what converted a
deadline attack elsewhere* and answered *a conditional fallback keyed to a scouting trigger*.
**This sweep is the next question down: what was in the trigger.**

---

## THE QUESTION, AND THE MEASURED SURPRISE IT WAS RE-AIMED AT

`docs/research/core-kill-incidence-cut-2026-08-09.md` landed the same day and changed the
shape of the problem:

- **Our build PRODUCTION discriminates nothing.** 17 of 30 of our r0-50 medians are identical
  between games where we destroy the enemy core and games where we do not; *"Not one of those
  production counters is a robust discriminator."* Our builder-bot coefficient of variation is
  **0.09 against opponents' 0.26**.
- **What varies is CONTACT.** `US_shot_w50` (AUC 0.64-0.68, worst Holm p 1.5e-07) is the best
  runtime-readable discriminator, computable by the core alone as
  `cumulative_converted − get_global_ammo()`. With an enemy-economy proxy it spans **13.9% →
  57.1%** core-kill incidence, median kill round **r284 → r117**.
- The cut labels it honestly: *"a marker, not a proven dial"*, and *"Only an arena A/B can turn
  this into a dial"*.
- Separately, `KILL_WINDOW_RND: 250` **is not our binding constraint** — 74.4% of our core-kill
  wins are already inside r250. What is scarce is the kill happening at all.

So, three parts:

- **(A)** In comparable leagues, what governed the decision to initiate the first engagement
  rather than keep developing? What was in the trigger, and what did sources warn against?
- **(B)** Did anyone distinguish *"we engaged early because we were winning"* from *"engaging
  early made us win"*?
- **(C)** Among teams with **fixed openings** (sweep 6 already settled that ours is
  defensible — not re-litigated here), what varied **after** the opening, on what signal, and
  what is the earliest branch point anyone reports?

## (A) WHEN TO MAKE FIRST CONTACT — what was actually in the trigger

Five distinct trigger *contents* were found. They are not variations on one idea; they read
different things.

**1. THE ENEMY'S BANK, INFERRED FROM THEIR OBSERVED SPENDING — and this is the finding.**
BC2020 Java Best Waifu committed their attacking unit only in the window where the counter-unit
was unaffordable:

> *"we decided to spawn Drones only if the enemy has spawned a unit or built a building
> recently (this way we assume they wouldn’t have the soup for a Net Gun)"*

It costs **no scouting** — the evidence is enemy buildings appearing in ordinary vision. And it
converges with the largest effect in our own cut (`THEM_ti_collected_end_w50`, AUC 0.32, *their
economy did not come up*), whose runtime proxy is their conveyor count, **70.1% of which sits
inside one builder's r²=20 of their own core**. Documented counter-strategy: an opponent who
**banks** instead of spending defeats the inference outright.
→ [`infer-their-bank-from-their-spending`](infer-their-bank-from-their-spending.md)

**2. THE FIRST ECONOMIC OBJECTIVE BEING CONTESTED — read by the unit already going there.**
BC2025 The Kragle's soldier pair was sent to capture a ruin for purely economic reasons; the
rush fired on what they found at the destination. *"The first priority of the soldier pair is
always to capture an “uncontested” ruin"* … *"in the case that the soldiers only found ruins
that were “contested,” they would rush"* … *"On 90% of the test maps, the soldier pair would
find an uncontested ruin and capture it before turn 20."* BC2023 Gone Fishin' reached the same
place from the cost side, retiring a dedicated scout — *"having carriers scout is a waste of
their potential to mine resources"* — and folding scouting into travel already being made.
**This is the answer to "scouting costs a build round": the trigger rides on a unit that was
already travelling.**
→ [`the-trigger-rides-on-a-unit-already-going-there`](the-trigger-rides-on-a-unit-already-going-there.md)

**3. A THREE-PART ARMED FLAG WITH A HYSTERESIS BAND.** Halite II Shummie publishes the whole
thing: arm on *"less than 16 turns, the enemy has no docked ships"* and a distance test;
a cohesion precondition — *"we only activate rush mode if all of our ships are within 3 *
MAX_SPEED from each other"*; and a **separately specified disarm**, at a *different* threshold
in the 4-player rule (arm at 7×, disarm at 10×). The middle term — *docked* means committed to
economy — is again an enemy-economy read.
→ [`arm-and-disarm-on-different-thresholds`](arm-and-disarm-on-different-thresholds.md)

**4. LOCAL FORCE COUNT — four leagues, one gate.** TheDuck314 (Halite III):
`(allied within radius 8) − (enemy within radius 8)`, and *"I initial started with a radius much
smaller than 8 but got big self-play improvements by expanding the radius to 8."* reCurse
(Halite II winner) applies the identical form to the *economy* decision with deliberately
asymmetric radii (25 allies / 85 enemies). The Kragle names it in XSquare's micro: *"pushing an
engagement when a robot senses it has many allies"*. mlomb reduces it to one scalar field with
two thresholds. **`transfers: partial`** — counting *bodies* is the wrong denominator here,
because our bodies are not damage.
→ [`local-force-count-is-the-engage-gate`](local-force-count-is-the-engage-gate.md)

**5. SIMULATE BOTH BRANCHES AND TAKE THE BETTER — the winner who could not find a threshold.**
Agade won Ghost in the Cell and reports: *"I play 20 turns of my AI with increasing
enabled/disabled against my AI with increasing disabled. If my production is higher after 20
turns of increasing I play my AI in increasing mode, otherwise I disable increasing."* and
*"This led to a huge gain in win rate."*
→ [`decide-by-simulating-both-branches`](decide-by-simulating-both-branches.md)

**And the field's answer to the *tactical* half of the question is that there is no gate at
all.** The Kragle, on the most-copied combat code in Battlecode: *"decision making for who and
when to attack is usually straightforward (target the lowest hp enemy possible, attack whenever
possible)"* — the entire engagement decision is expressed as **movement**. For us, whose damage
is immobile, that means **placement**.
→ [`initiation-is-a-placement-decision-not-a-fire-decision`](initiation-is-a-placement-decision-not-a-fire-decision.md)

**Supporting machinery found alongside the triggers:**
[`abort-the-scout-on-a-deadline`](abort-the-scout-on-a-deadline.md) (BC2020 confused: *"If after
180 rounds it still doesn't see the enemy HQ, it gives up on the rush and joins the rest of the
miners"*) ·
[`mass-before-contact-by-following-the-first-mover`](mass-before-contact-by-following-the-first-mover.md)
(BC2023 don't @ me's leaderless massing — **deterministic here, given our confirmed id-ascending
turn order**) ·
[`the-scout-that-pays-for-itself`](the-scout-that-pays-for-itself.md) (BC2019 smite: *"At best,
it saved us the game, and at worst, it was almost no loss."*).

**A LATE ADDITION THAT CORROBORATES SWEEP 14 FROM A SECOND FIELD.** RTS theory decomposes the
defender's advantage into **exactly two mechanisms** — reinforcement travel distance, and the
production time that distance buys — and names **one** documented cancellation: *"This close
warp in cancels the notion of the Defender's Advantage (when it comes to reinforcements). The
advantage based on fortifications and structures remains."* That is **production placed at the
target**, which is precisely sweep 14's precondition #3
([`spawn-the-attack-at-the-target-not-a-march`](spawn-the-attack-at-the-target-not-a-march.md)),
arrived at independently of Battlecode. It is also **structurally unavailable to us** — only our
core spawns builders, ≤1/turn, on its own ring — so this raises confidence in the diagnosis
without opening a road. Note too that our own defender's edge is **not** either of their two
mechanisms: it is a heal rate the source game does not have, which is why our advantage survives
a unit cap that would remove theirs.
→ [`defenders-advantage-has-exactly-two-mechanisms`](defenders-advantage-has-exactly-two-mechanisms.md)

**WHAT SOURCES WARNED AGAINST** is filed separately and should be read as counter-evidence, not
buried: Agade flipped a placement sign to be **less** aggressive early and *"This gained a lot
of trueskill points"*; TheDuck314 *"only start ramming late in the game"*; rooklift, author of a
rush theory whose bot went 1279-176, *"never rush[es] in 4 player games"*; and reCurse gates his
whole strategic-panic mode behind *"More than 60 turns have elapsed, to avoid early aggression
being seen as apocalyptic."* **Three of the four are 4-player-format artefacts and largely
evaporate on translation to our 1v1 — that is stated in the file rather than glossed.**
→ [`the-field-warns-against-early-contact`](the-field-warns-against-early-contact.md)

## (B) CAUSE VERSUS MARKER — **nobody did this**

**The answer is a clean negative, and it is measured rather than asserted.** Across all 22
official Battlecode postmortems 2019-2026, these terms appear **zero times in every document**:

`correlat` · `causal` · `confound` · `ablat` · `regression` · `hypothes` · `sample size` ·
`noise` · `misleading` · `coincidence` · `p-value` · `random seed` · `number of games`

`significan` (16 docs) and `luck` (12) are colloquial throughout; `variance` appears twice, both
times about **strategy** variance. The other-leagues subagent reports the same negative from
Halite, Lux, CodinGame and Terminal independently. **No competitor, anywhere in this sweep,
separated "we engaged early because we were already ahead" from "engaging early caused the
win."** The question is not answered badly — it is not asked.
→ [`nobody-separated-cause-from-marker`](nobody-separated-cause-from-marker.md)

**What the field has instead is a literature on the LIMITS OF SELF-PLAY A/B**, which is the
closest live methodological question, and it is unexpectedly rich.

The **BC2025 winner** overrode its own A/B **twice** and won:

> *"When we performed AB testing against our past bots, and using defense towers did not seem to
> be very good… Against our past bots it usually went even in wins, or even slightly lost. But
> our bot wasn’t super aggressive, and we believed that this, in theory, SHOULD be better
> against the teams that we have the worst matchups against."*

The stated defect is exactly ours: **the self-play pool does not contain the opponent behaviour
the feature is designed to read.** Our own pool opens as a near-constant (CV 0.09 vs the field's
0.26) and is dominated 87-90% — so a contact trigger tested against ourselves is asked to
discriminate a distribution our own measurement says is flat.
→ [`self-play-ab-has-the-wrong-population`](self-play-ab-has-the-wrong-population.md)

And **one competitor measured the same feature on both instruments and published both numbers**
— the single most useful calibration found:

> *"Locally, in self-play, amputating this opponent modelling causes a loss of ~30% in
> winrate."* … *"Against other players the impact was not as dramatic but it was there, very
> roughly ~15% seen with CGBench."*

**Roughly 2× inflation, same sign.** The same author names the mechanism (*"in the extreme case
you'll play a bot against itself, minus a mistake, and win all the time"*), names the bias
(*"risks of specialisation. And it's true."*), and publishes a **mitigation** we can use
immediately: amputate, during the self-play leg, the module whose accuracy is inflated by the
opponent being a copy of you. The Halite II winner reaches the same verdict independently —
*"Local testing against previous versions was helpful in the beginning, but the exercise became
increasingly inaccurate and pointless over time."*
→ [`self-play-inflates-the-effect-by-about-2x`](self-play-inflates-the-effect-by-about-2x.md)

**The one controlled experiment anyone ran** is BC2024 cout for clout's *"simple test of 47 vs
50 ducks"* — 90% loss for the smaller army, one variable, everything else fixed. That shape
works because it is a **mechanism** test, not a strategy test.

## (C) THE CONSTANT-OPENING PROBLEM — what varied after the opening

Sweep 6's verdict that *"our constant is DEFENSIBLE"* is not re-opened. The narrower question —
what varied **after** the opening, on what signal — has four answers, and the earliest branch
point is earlier than expected.

**1. MAP GEOMETRY, at round 0, with no scouting — and it is our documented gap.** Two finalists
two seasons apart name the same first branch variable. wololo: *"Thus it was extremely important
to judge the distance and passability between my EC and my opponent’s to determine whether to
rush and bury or to turtle with a defended slanderer economy"*. 4 Musketeers: *"We wait until
we’ve taken a first guess at where the enemy HQ is. If it’s close, then we need to build a lot
of launchers, or else we’ll die."* Note *a first guess* — from symmetry, not from a scout. This
is the mechanism the field used against the failure mode INDEX already names: *"The one
qualification — an opening unconditional on MAP GEOMETRY — is a documented failure mode, and our
own width gradient is it."*
→ [`branch-on-distance-to-the-enemy-core`](branch-on-distance-to-the-enemy-core.md)

**2. AN ACHIEVEMENT OR A COUNT OF YOUR OWN STRUCTURES — never a round number.** BC2025 Just Woke
Up (winner): *"If we had more than 5 towers placed on the map (that the soldier was aware of)
they would start pathfinding towards any empty paint they sensed"* — a **per-unit local**
estimate, deliberately. The Kragle: *"Until the soldiers finished the first ruin, they were in
“opening mode.”"* Both self-pace. Both feed the previous state back in: *"a determineState
function which uses our surroundings, resource levels, and previous states"*.
**The earliest branch point anyone reports is the Kragle's, at turn 20 on 90% of maps**, and
Agade's Code Royale branch is earlier still in build-count terms — off a hardcoded opening,
*"I try to build 2 mines, but will switch to a barrack earlier if the enemy has already started
producing his knights"*.
→ [`branch-on-a-milestone-not-a-round-number`](branch-on-a-milestone-not-a-round-number.md)

**3. A FIXED ORDER THAT COULD NOT BE IMPROVED BY REORDERING — replaced by weights.** BC2021
Malott Fat Cats hit our exact wall and reported it: *"Our build order used for Sprint 2 was
surprisingly hard to improve, in the sense that we couldn’t get a considerable winrate against
our old bot by only changing build order."* Their fix stopped expressing the opening as a list:
*"we substituted our fixed build order by a more flexible one by giving weights to each unit"*,
with weights that *"change over time"*. **Our engine already supplies their accumulator — cost
scaling is exactly it.**
→ [`replace-the-build-order-with-weights`](replace-the-build-order-with-weights.md)

**4. THE MACHINERY THAT MAKES BRANCHING SURVIVABLE.** Two finalists four years apart replaced a
mode variable with a **stack** so an interrupt does not destroy the plan it interrupted (3
Musketeers' `stateStack`; the Kragle's goal stack, fixing *"ruins would be abandoned when robots
went back to get their paint refilled"*). Our own corpus shows exactly that failure in our code:
a builder beside a **damaged** core moves in **15.5% of rounds against 68.3% at full HP**
(n = 143,812, opponent placebo −0.152).
→ [`the-goal-stack-beats-the-mode-flag`](the-goal-stack-beats-the-mode-flag.md) ·
[`relax-the-placement-threshold-when-blocked`](relax-the-placement-threshold-when-blocked.md)
(BC2020 Java Best Waifu's *desperation index* — the third answer to "what if the condition never
materialises", alongside abort and re-aim).

## WHERE THIS TOUCHES THE LIBRARY'S STANDING CONTEXT

**No contradiction found.** Three qualifications, all narrowing rather than reversing:

1. **Sweep 14's "the trigger" is now filled in, and its content is not what the Kragle analogy
   alone suggested.** The corpus cut says our enemy-side signal is *their economy failing*, not
   *their aggression*; Java Best Waifu and Shummie both independently keyed on **enemy economic
   state**. So "commit when their economy has not come up" is doubly sourced — once from our own
   data, once from two leagues — and *"rush when they look aggressive"* is sourced by nobody.
2. **`KILL_WINDOW_RND: 250` is a round number, and no winning bot in this sweep branches on a
   round number.** Every branch found is on an achievement, a structure count, a resource
   threshold, or map geometry. This does not contradict the PROGRAMME — the window is a target,
   not a trigger — but it does say the *implementation* of a commitment should probably not be a
   clock.
3. **The library's own evidence class is now bounded.** Every "what converted a rush" finding in
   sweep 14, and every finding here, rests on winners describing their own winning games. Since
   **nobody in the field ever separated cause from marker**, the library cannot adjudicate the
   question our cut opened. The arena is the only instrument, and that is now a sourced
   conclusion rather than a default.

### Near-misses caught by the mechanical check and corrected before publication — **8**

The library's documented failure mode, sighted eight more times. All eight were **genuine
quotes with a defective transcription**, not fabrications:

1. **A paraphrase smoothed into quotation marks.** A draft read *"On most maps, the soldier pair
   would find an uncontested ruin…"*. The Kragle actually writes **"On 90% of the test maps"**.
   The real sentence is *better* than the draft — it carries a number — and only the literal
   grep found it.
2. **Curly-vs-straight apostrophes, in both directions.** Java Best Waifu and wololo use `’`
   where drafts had `'`; BC2020 confused uses **straight** `"rusher"` where a draft
   "corrected" it to `“rusher”`. There is no house style across the corpus, and assuming one
   produces false negatives either way.
3. **A NEW EXTRACTION TRAP, and it is the same family as the `\f` bug found in sweep 14.**
   `pdftotext` interleaves **footnote markers and page numbers mid-sentence**: BC2025 Just Woke
   Up reads *"hyper aggressive 18 teams"* and The Kragle reads *"an “uncontested” ruin8 ."*.
   **Whitespace flattening does not remove these**, so any quote spanning a footnote marker or a
   page number returns a false negative even with the corrected flatten command. Three of the
