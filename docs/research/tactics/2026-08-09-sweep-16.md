## THE QUESTION, AND THE ASYMMETRY THAT PROMPTED IT

**We are rich downward and empty upward.** We sit at **1603.6** Elo. Every number the current
programme has produced is against **CtrlAltDefeat 1602.8, Ouroboros 1542.0, Kings College Munich
1555.7, Orizon 1396.2** — teams at or below us. The top of the league is **sporks 2102, Clankers
2040, not adgato 2000, Pivot 1977, Pantheon 1966**, a gap of roughly **360-500 Elo** on which we
hold almost no mechanism data. Sweep 15 additionally established that this library's whole
evidence class is **winners describing their own winning games**.

- **(A)** How did competitors beat opponents **stronger than themselves** — not how the winner
  won, but what teams did when they were outclassed, and what they reported as working *versus
  wasted*?
- **(B)** When a league stratified, did the top separate itself by doing **more of the same**, or
  by doing something **different in kind**? (Our measured defensive shape is *more of the same* as
  the mid-field's — collar occupancy 67.3% against a third-party field average of 53.2%.)
- **(C)** How did competitors get signal about opponents **stronger than their sparring
  partners**? Ours is dominated 87-90% and our opening is a near-constant (CV 0.09 vs the field's
  0.26). **A negative here was to be reported as a negative.**

## (A) THE UNDERDOG'S QUESTION

### The hardest number in the sweep, and it is a measured all-in

**AIIDE 2020: Microwave finished #6 overall at 54.47% and scored 1% against #1 Stardust** (from
the crosstable row *"6 microwave 54.47% 1%"*). Microwave's per-opening learning file against
Stardust is published, and **I re-parsed it myself from block header to block total: 47 opening
rows, 150 games, exactly ONE row with a nonzero win rate** — *"3HatchLingBust 9 11%"*, a zergling
all-in bust, against a total of *"47 openings 150 1%"*. Its most-tried opening went *"3HatchMuta
36 0%"*.

**And the alternative explanation is killed in the same source.** Microwave predicted Stardust's
strategy correctly in 127 of 150 games, and Jay Scott's verdict is one sentence: *"Stardust always
plays the same strategy, so it’s no wonder that Microwave was able to predict it. **Not that it
helped.**"* **Against a sufficiently stronger opponent, perfect opponent modelling was worth
nothing and only the highest-variance option scored at all.**
→ [`the-only-thing-that-ever-beat-the-champion`](the-only-thing-that-ever-beat-the-champion.md)

### A weaker bot with a repeatable positive record against the champion

CodinGame Fall 2022, a post headed *"AI of arturgo, legend #15"*: *"I'm not very good at designing
scoring functions, but that gave me #15, and a 66% win rate against delineate"* — and delineate
won that contest (verified from a different post in the same thread). The mechanism is a mixed
strategy: *"To win a game like that, we can't be deterministic, for example, if you always play
Paper, you'll be beaten by a player who always plays Scissors."* **The ladder property that makes
this possible is non-transitivity, and one competitor tuned for it deliberately** — *"I feel my
rank (100+) is not justified considering I had 55-60% win rate against rank 40-50 bots"* … *"My
bot was fine tuned to beat better bots and not weaker bots."*
→ [`be-unpredictable-against-the-better-player`](be-unpredictable-against-the-better-player.md)

### The one postmortem written from below, and it diagnoses our bot

BC2025 **The Kragle** name themselves as sweep 15's exception: *"Often, teams who make postmortems
are those who have consistently placed highly from the start."* … *"We have the perspective of a
team that spent years with no consideration of qualifiers, who this year became one of the
higher level contenders"* They went *"1570 ranked 63rd"* to
*"around 1800, ranked 27th"* — ≈270 Elo — **and what closed it was not a new weapon:**

> *"Thanks to our excellent opening strategy, we often found ourselves with a resource lead, even
> against higher rated teams. However, our bot was fantastic at throwing leads."*

Named defects: floating resources (*"a paint lead on paper, but if all of that paint was not
spendable, it wasn’t a paint lead in practice"*) and idle time (*"a robot-count lead means nothing
if half of your robots are derping around"*). **Set beside INDEX's *"We bank and do not spend"* and
*"We win the opening and we win the clock; we die in the middle."*, it is the same bot.**
→ [`the-underdogs-own-postmortem`](the-underdogs-own-postmortem.md) ·
[`how-a-200-elo-gap-actually-looks`](how-a-200-elo-gap-actually-looks.md) (their ~200-Elo loss is
*localised in time*; their ~460-Elo loss is *"they simply did everything better than us"*)

### Three further mechanisms, each documented

**Be general where they are tuned.** BC2023 don't @ me, 25th seed, upset the 8th seed twice:
*"a lot of teams had hardcoded values according to the three original game maps"* … *"our more
generalized code (especially in regard to exploring map symmetries) meant that our bot could
compete and even beat upper level teams, despite our seemingly poor standing compared to them"*
→ [`upset-them-where-they-are-overfitted`](upset-them-where-they-are-overfitted.md)

**Tax a defensive reflex that fires unconditionally.** BC2021 wololo: *"most top turtles operated
under the assumption that their turtle was worth protecting, and thus attempted to defend until
they had enough defense built up to support slanderers, consequently using up all their unit count
on defense against my burying rushes regardless of whether it would work for them or not."*
The counter is in the same paragraph — opponents learned *"not to waste conviction on defenses"*.
→ [`their-defensive-reflex-fires-unconditionally`](their-defensive-reflex-fires-unconditionally.md)

**Specialise into the winnable subset and concede the rest.** BC2026 Lorem Ipsum: *"games on
closed maps generally were ones I was already going to loose, so I made the (risky) decision to
implement this with hopes that it would give me a significant edge on open maps."*
→ [`write-off-the-games-you-were-losing-anyway`](write-off-the-games-you-were-losing-anyway.md)

### WHAT WAS REPORTED AS WASTED — asked for specifically, and the library would otherwise miss it

**Copying the top tier.** BC2024 cout for clout adopted two top-team strategies and discarded
both — *"Because we idolize every single top team, we decided to try it as well. Although this strategy
allowed us to place more traps, these traps were much less efficient, and it only did better
against a few teams, so we got rid of it"*, and *"Turns out, using 3 ducks to sit on your spawn makes you lose just about every micro
battle."* Halite III's mlomb reverse-engineered a spawn threshold from a rank-1 player and reports
*"I tried this strategy and it didn't work for me."* A CodinGame competitor imitating the same
bot as the successful imitator in A3 got *"stuck at #30 place in Gold."*
→ [`copying-the-top-tier-is-not-free`](copying-the-top-tier-is-not-free.md)

**And the strongest counter-argument to this whole section, from the author of half its
evidence.** Jay Scott ran the opposite experiment on his own bot: *"Last year, Steamhammer scored
many upsets against stronger players and many losses against the lower ranks. Today it is the
other way around"* … *"I haven’t been trying to beat the top bots, I’ve been trying to play
better."* … *"It’s the opposite plan from trying to beat the current #1."* **Upset-proneness and overall
strength traded against each other and he chose strength.** Filed inside

## (B) MORE OF THE SAME, OR DIFFERENT IN KIND — **the corpus answers BOTH, and both are filed**

**This is not a failure to find an answer. Credible sources disagree, and the disagreement is the
decision-relevant content.**

**DIFFERENT IN KIND — the strongest evidence, because it is a competition record with an
accidental control.** AIIDE 2012 added cross-game persistence: *"6 of the 10 entrants used this
feature to aid in strategy selection, including the top 4 finishers."* AIIDE 2013's winner added a
combat simulator: *"The major addition to UAlbertaBot was a combat simulation package called
SparCraft."* … *"This addition, combined with some additional bug fixes led to the victory."*
**And CIG 2013 ran the same field with learning accidentally disabled** — *"the read/write
function that enables learning between games had to be disabled again"* — with the result that
*"disabling the learning was a disadvantage for the UAlbertaBot who won the AIIDE competition and
was only runner-up to the Skynet bot here."* The authors bound it honestly: *"the effect is
limited."* **For us the first separator is a firm negative — our engine has no cross-match
persistence at all.**
→ [`the-top-separated-itself-by-a-mechanism-twice`](the-top-separated-itself-by-a-mechanism-twice.md)

**DIFFERENT IN KIND, in a named direction, from a team that had ridden the other answer.** BC2023
don't @ me: *"we focused almost entirely on generality and robustness, which got us towards the
finals"* … *"at the highest elo, generality and robustness starts to take a back seat to
legitimate higher-level strategy"* … *"We didn’t have enough time to pivot our designs out towards
having more specific strategies for specific map states."* **Robustness is the floor, not the
ladder** — and it converges with the gap sweep 15 named from the other side, INDEX's *"The one
qualification — an opening unconditional on MAP GEOMETRY — is a documented failure mode, and our
own width gradient is it."*
→ [`generality-saturates-and-then-stops-paying`](generality-saturates-and-then-stops-paying.md)

**MORE OF THE SAME.** BC2024 cout for clout: *"Most teams use the same ideas; it’s the execution
of those ideas that make most of the difference, only possible through careful analysis of
scrimmages and your code."* BC2025 SPAARK: *"just taking all the game
constants at face value is enough to be competitive with top teams."* **And Halite III's winner
reports his fanciest mechanism was worthless** — *"This neural net idea was great fun to implement
but seemed to have basically no impact on mu."*

**THE FORMULATION THAT RECONCILES THEM, from the BC2025 winner:** *"While the general game plan
was basically the same for all teams, the top teams all had some creative gimmicks in their bots
that helped differentiate them, whether it was a rush strategy, tower flickering, defense towers,
or some other whacky strategies, don't be afraid to try something different."* **Base plan shared;
one narrow differentiating mechanism each.** On this model our measured position — the mid-field's
defensive shape executed harder, with no stage two — is exactly what the top of the mid-field
looks like, and it is external support for the *shape* of the Loki directive.
→ [`the-top-tier-is-parity-plus-one-gimmick`](the-top-tier-is-parity-plus-one-gimmick.md)

**One further "in kind" case with a heavy survivorship caution:** BC2020 Java Best Waifu went
*"more than 200 elo"* clear of the whole field with a bot that was neither of the two dominant
shapes — and had never scrimmed a top bot.
→ [`the-bot-that-leapt-the-field-had-no-data-on-it`](the-bot-that-leapt-the-field-had-no-data-on-it.md)

**Two calibrations.** **(1) A gap can be map fit:** BC2025 om nom held their submission fixed and
*"when they uploaded the tournament maps our bot jumped about 100 ELO reaching the 2000s for the
first time."* → [`a-hundred-elo-of-map-distribution`](a-hundred-elo-of-map-distribution.md)
**(2) The top tier is compressed:** BC2025 confused entered the finals as 1st seed and reports
*"scrimmages against top teams Just Woke Up, Om Nom, and subscribe to caterpillow were
consistently close, barely maintaining a 50% win rate."* And one separator was infrastructural
rather than strategic — BC2023 no thoughts: *"At the final tournament we found out that many top
teams had built up custom systems for quickly running many matches in parallel for A/B testing."*

**AND THE MEASUREMENT THAT SAYS WHERE ANY UPGRADE WILL SHOW UP.** The Lanchester paper replaced
UAlbertaBot's attack/retreat predictor and moved its six-opponent average from **41.6 to 69.7** —
but: *"It is interesting to note that the least (or no) improvement occurs in our best match-ups,
where we already win close to 80% of the games."* … *"However, there are bigger improvements for
the more difficult match-ups, which is an encouraging result."* **A real upgrade measures flat on the matchups you already
dominate.** That is the sourced, quantified reason our dominated pool cannot see a plank aimed
upward.
→ [`gains-land-in-the-hard-matchups`](gains-land-in-the-hard-matchups.md)

## (C) TESTING AGAINST A POOL TOO WEAK — **NOT a negative. Five leagues solved it, explicitly**

**This is the sweep's firmest result and it contradicts the possibility the brief held open.**

**1. STOP GENERATING LOCAL NUMBERS; ACQUIRE GAMES UPWARD.** BC2021 wololo, describing our exact
predicament and the remedy in one breath: *"I had to test purely by requesting scrimmages against
high-ranked teams with the autoaccept scrimmages option on"* … *"as my new strategy demolished the
locally ranked competition."* BC2025's winner automated it (*"a script that would automatically log
in to the battlecode website and request to scrimmage with as many enemy teams as I chose"*);
BC2026's 2nd place *"would queue scrims against teams above us on the leaderboard"*; BC2025 SPAARK:
*"Scrimmage analysis is still more important than raw win rate against old bots."*
→ [`scrim-upward-when-your-own-pool-is-too-weak`](scrim-upward-when-your-own-pool-is-too-weak.md)

**2. READ YOUR CONSTANTS OFF THEIR REPLAYS — four leagues, independently.** BC2026's 2nd place:
*"reverse engineer their macro (which is much easier than reverse engineering micro, pathfinding,
or communication), copy whatever strategy we gleaned"*, which is where *"our magic numbers for how
many Baby Rats to build and when to build more Baby Rats"* came from. Halite III's mlomb (rank 18)
did the numeric version: *"Averaging that infomation from top players and some tuning by hand I
just hardcoded the max turn to spawn a ship in my bot."* CodinGame Spring 2021 did the behavioural
version and it moved a league: *"The thing that got me to legend was actually watching @reCurse
games and synthesizing his strategy into rules."* BC2020 The High Ground *"pulled up some replays
of Java’s games"*. **`transfers: yes` — this is the finding actionable today with the 3.8k-replay
corpus.**
→ [`read-your-constants-off-a-stronger-bot`](read-your-constants-off-a-stronger-bot.md)

**3. BUILD THE OPPONENT POOL BY HAND, AND GATE PER OPPONENT.** CodinGame's VirtualAtom states our
problem — *"we cannot test locally how well our bot performs, so we have to rely on the arena"*,
and *"None of those bots were clearly better than others when tested locally"* — then hand-built
five behaviourally distinct opponents (*"DEFEND, FARM, MIXED1, MIXED2, and MIXED3"*) with
**different thresholds against each** (*"more than 20% win rate against the three mixed
versions"*, later *"until the win rates are all above 90% vs DEFEND, FARM, MIXED1, MIXED2, and
MIXED3"*). BattleSnake supplies the reason an old-version pool is safe at all: *"80% of the time we
train against the current best model, and 20% randomly select one of the prior best models"*,
because otherwise the policy learns *"cyclical strategies"*.
→ [`the-opponent-pool-you-build-by-hand`](the-opponent-pool-you-build-by-hand.md)

**4. THE ERRORS HAVE A DIRECTION, AND IT IS THE ONE THAT KILLS GOOD PLANKS.** Halite II's champion:
*"Local testing against previous versions was helpful in the beginning, but the exercise became
increasingly inaccurate and pointless over time. **I often had versions performing much better
online while still performing poorly against the previous version.**"* AIIDE 2020's Microwave, on
prepared training data versus tournament results: *"In general, for stronger opponents training data
overestimated Microwave’s success, while for weaker opponents it was the opposite"* CodinGame's
Magus reports the mirror error from a local batch runner — *"I was better in local (tested with
brutaltester) but worse in the arena. Overfit nightmare."* **Both signs occur; neither is the
ladder.** And BC2025's winner overrode his own A/B a **third** time on the stated rule *"Just
because a new bot is worse against your old one doesn't mean it will be worse against other
teams."*
→ [`ship-the-feature-your-pool-cannot-see`](ship-the-feature-your-pool-cannot-see.md)

**5. WHEN YOU LOSE EVERY GAME, WIN/LOSS CARRIES NO GRADIENT.** *"against a much stronger opponent
Steamhammer rarely wins and falls back on trying builds at random, hoping to hit one that works.
Most of the random choices are poor, but it is losing every game anyway so it can’t tell."* **This
is independent validation of `PRIMARY_CURRENCY: core_kill_share`** — a denser signal than win/loss
is exactly the prescribed fix — **and a warning that against the 1900+ band core-kill share will
itself go flat and need something denser still.**
→ [`win-loss-learning-has-no-gradient-when-you-lose`](win-loss-learning-has-no-gradient-when-you-lose.md)

**6. MEASURE WHAT THE TOP TIER NEVER DOES, AND TEST THE DETECTOR LIVE WITH A HARMLESS PAYLOAD.**
CodinGame's Gonny (#29) deleted a whole subsystem after checking: *"I initially switched if I knew
an enemy pac could try and kill me next turn, but I then realized that at the top of the
leaderboard no one ever does that."* Halite II's Shummie supplies the safe way to validate an
opponent fingerprint before arming it: *"I even uploaded my alliance dance code for about 2 weeks,
but if it detected a positive, i would immediately crash. This would give us an idea of how often
our signal would have a false positive."*
→ [`measure-what-the-top-tier-never-does`](measure-what-the-top-tier-never-does.md)

**7. THE COST, which must be filed beside the remedy.** BC2020 confused targeted one stronger
opponent, then *"made the grave mistake of actually testing this feature against Kryptonite by
requesting a lot of scrimmages"* — Kryptonite saw the games and shipped the counter-counter before
the deadline. *"We learned our lesson of not requesting scrimmages to test out the feature that
was supposed to be countering that team."*
→ [`probing-the-target-teaches-the-target`](probing-the-target-teaches-the-target.md)

**8. AND THE TRAP, STATED BY A TEAM STILL INSIDE IT.** BC2026 Lorem Ipsum's published pipeline
ends at *"When we send to online scrims, we then start scrimming a lot of teams within our rating
range"*, accepting at *"roughly slightly above 50%"* — which he flags himself, *"(which is pretty
low)"*. **Testing within your own band is the default; escaping it is a deliberate act.**

**A ninth channel appears in five Battlecode documents and is not experimental at all: ASK THEM.**
The Kragle — *"Many top teams are very friendly, so don’t be afraid to ask them what strategies
they use on the Discord."* 3 Musketeers — *"Top teams tend to talk about different ideas in
discord and being active there can be a good way to stay in the loop about the current meta."* Lorem Ipsum found a live bug in his own bot that way: *"I never noticed this in scrims
or tournaments, and only know about this by talking to TSPAARK."*

## SWEEP 15's OUTSTANDING ITEMS — three closed, verified by me

1. **Jay Scott's "you don't have to attack" theorem — FOUND AND VERIFIED.** From two stated axioms
   (*"The side on the defensive has an advantage."*) he derives *"If you control more resources,
   you don’t have to attack."* and *"You can win without ever entering a fair fight."*
   **IMPORTANT: the theorem is stated for the STRONGER side. The underdog corollary is not in the
   post and must not be attributed to him.**
2. **UAlbertaBot's `bool retreat = score < 0;` — FOUND, and `score` identified.** `Squad.cpp:266`,
   with `Squad.cpp:251,257` showing `SparCraft::ScoreType score = 0;` … `score =
   sim.simulateCombat();`, and `CombatSimulation.cpp:142,146` showing a 2000-frame playout scored
   by `EvaluationMethods::LTD2`. **`score` is not a force ratio and not a unit count — it is the
   evaluation of a simulated battle's terminal state.** The bot's own debug strings confirm it:
   *"Retreat - simulation predicts defeat"* / *"Attack - simulation predicts success"*.
3. **The FORCE-RATIO negative is CONFIRMED and refined.** No source found states "attack when your
   force exceeds theirs by X". **The state of the art threshold is `predicted_outcome > 0`, not a
   ratio** — which does not conflict with

## WHERE THIS TOUCHES THE LIBRARY'S STANDING CONTEXT

**One outright contradiction of a possibility the brief held open, and it is the sweep's main
result.** The brief allowed that the answer to (C) might be *"nobody solved it"*. **It is not.**
Five leagues solved it with a stack of mechanisms — request games upward; reverse-engineer macro
constants from replays; hand-build a behaviourally diverse pool with per-opponent gates; sample
old versions to avoid cyclical collapse; measure what the top tier never does. This **narrows**
sweep 15's conclusion without reversing it: *"The arena is the only instrument"* holds for
**verdicts**, but the field also used the arena as a **source of hypotheses**. We have used it only
as the former.

**One tension inside the library, made explicit rather than harmonised.** don't @ me say

game plan was basically the same for all teams"*. **Both published, in files that link to each
other.** The reconciliation offered is labelled in-file as **my reading, not a sourced claim**.

**A second tension, and it is between two files in this sweep.** The measured all-in result says
the only thing that ever beat the champion was maximum variance; Jay Scott's own account says he
traded upset-proneness away for strength and rose. **Both are in
`the-only-thing-that-ever-beat-the-champion`, and the corpus does not adjudicate between them.**

**No contradiction found** on: the 2.2:1 heal edge; *"Everything about us breaks at r150."*;
*"THE FORWARD ROAD IS CLOSED"*; the id-ascending turn order; the measured seat null. **One file
runs against the PROGRAMME's steer and says so in its own text** —
[`write-off-the-games-you-were-losing-anyway`](write-off-the-games-you-were-losing-anyway.md)
points at the long game where `KILL_WINDOW_RND: 250` points away from it. Filed as evidence about
what underdogs did, **not** as an argument to change a directive that is Magnus's call.

**Two things run the programme's way and were not previously sourced.** `PRIMARY_CURRENCY:
core_kill_share` is exactly the denser-than-win/loss signal Jay Scott prescribes for a losing
matchup. And the AIIDE all-in result is the only measured case in the sweep of anything at all
working against a 400-Elo superior.

### THE ONE TABLE I RE-PARSED RATHER THAN TRUSTED — and why it mattered

The headline (A) claim rests on *"3HatchLingBust 9 11%"* being the **only** nonzero row in
Microwave's opening table against Stardust. **I did not accept that.** I located every block header
in the page, isolated the `#1 stardust` block, and parsed its rows. **My first parse found a second
nonzero row (`HeavyRush 127 1%`) and TWO total lines — which would have falsified the claim.**
Reading the raw block showed why: **the block contains two different tables** — Microwave's own
openings (47 rows, ending *"47 openings 150 1%"*) and a second table of *recognised enemy
strategies* (ending *"3 openings 150 1%"*), and my regex had swept both. Re-parsing between the
header and the first total gives **47 rows, exactly one nonzero**. **The subagent's claim was
right and my own check was the thing that was wrong** — which is the argument for re-verification
running in both directions, not just as a distrust of agents.

### Near-misses caught by the mechanical check and corrected before publication — **18**

**Not one was a fabrication.** All eighteen were defects in *transcription or attribution of
genuine material*, and **every single one was caught by the post-hoc audit of the published files
rather than by the pre-write batch check** — because the pre-write check tests strings I chose to
test, and the audit tests strings I actually wrote. They fall into four kinds:

- **9 × an ellipsis elided a clause and the fragment no longer matched** (don't @ me, wololo,
  cout for clout ×2, Jay Scott ×2, Just Woke Up, Microwave's notation, The Kragle). In two of
  these the elided clause was the *most* load-bearing part; both were expanded rather than
  trimmed.
- **6 × a full stop invented at a truncation point** (wololo, cout for clout, the Lanchester
  paper, Microwave, 3 Musketeers, The Kragle). **A period where the source has a comma silently
  claims the thought ended there.** Not covered by the typo convention.
- **2 × an internal quote wrong in attribution or case** (items 1 and 2 below).
- **1 × a bracketed edit inside quotation marks** (*"do[es] not make…"*), replaced with the exact
  sentence.

The four worth naming individually:

1. **An internal quote attributed to the wrong document — the worst of the eighteen.** A draft
   rendered *"the ladder is the field instrument"* as something INDEX records. **It does not** —
   that phrasing is from session memory, not the repo library. Replaced with INDEX's actual
   sentence. **This is the citation-failure family pointed at our own files**, and it is why
   internal quotes went through the same grep as external ones.
2. **A case-altered internal quote.** *"everything about us breaks at r150"* — INDEX writes
   **"Everything about us breaks at r150."** (The pre-existing sweep-14 file
   `the-attack-that-arrives-too-late-or-at-nothing.md` carries the same lowercase variant. **Not
   edited — another sweep's file** — but recorded so it is not mistaken for a second source.)
3. **A PROGRAMME field name mistyped as `WIN_RATE_IS_VERDITT`**, and described as holding "in
   spirit" when it is literally set.
4. **An ellipsis that elided the informative sentence** in a cout for clout quote — the elided
   clause named the opponent beating them. **Expanded rather than trimmed.**

**THE PATTERN, and it is the method finding of this sweep: 14 of the 18 defects were compressions
made in the SUMMARY, not in the tactic files.** Files get written with the grep output open on
screen; the summary gets written from memory *of the files*. **The summary is systematically the
higher-risk document and had never previously been machine-audited** — sweeps 14 and 15 audited
their tactic files only. Adding it to the audit set is the single change most likely to prevent a
future correction, and it should be standing practice.

**Two of the 21 initial audit failures were artifacts of my own tooling, not defects:** an HTML
tag-stripper leaving `&gt;` unescaped (fixed by `html.unescape` in the audit), and two source-code
debug strings whose file I had not added to the corpus set. **Both would have read as "quote not
found" to anyone reading the audit output**, which is the same false-alarm hazard as the
blockquote issue below.

### A method note for the next sweep

**Two known extraction traps did not bite (the `\f` page-break fix and the curly/straight
apostrophe trap), because quotes were copied OUT OF verified grep output rather than typed and then
checked.** A new trap did bite, and it is markdown-local: **the library's own convention of putting
long quotes in `>` blockquotes means any audit of a published file must strip `^>\s?` before
matching.** My first audit run reported **21 unmatched fragments; 18 were this and 0 were real.**
A guard that produces 18 false alarms trains its reader to ignore it — the same hazard INDEX names
about guards with blind spots, arriving from the opposite direction. **One further glyph note from
the CodinGame leg: Discourse's `/raw/<topic>/<post>` endpoint serves the author's original markdown
with ASCII `'`, while the rendered page runs a typographer and serves curly `’`. Quote from
`/raw/`.**
