# Sweep 17A-Lux — closing the gap 17A declared UNSWEPT

**Why this exists.** [`2026-08-09-sweep-17a.md`](2026-08-09-sweep-17a.md) states its
own gap plainly, in its closing section: its Halite/Lux discovery leg returned a
partial delta, so *"anything from Lux is therefore absent from this sweep and is not a
negative — it is unswept."* That leg's output later reached this session instead of
17A. **This document is the Lux S1/S2 material, re-verified from local raw bytes and
filed.** Nothing here entered on the relaying agent's word — a relay is a claim,
including a relay from our own subagent.

**Aimed at 17A's frame, and the frame survives.** 17A's structural finding is that
**an economically-correct evaluator never finishes**: under our rules a core kill
returns no titanium, no harvester and no tiebreak key, so a bot pricing each action by
its return is *correct* to refuse every step of an assault. **Lux S1 is the control
case** — a league with a kill condition and no kill mechanism, whose games therefore
run to the horizon on score by construction. **Lux S2 is the fix.** And the
counterweight is filed rather than cherry-picked: **Lux S1's organisers moved AWAY from
decisiveness on purpose.**

## The five things worth reading

### 1. Lux S1 is a clean control case, and the negative is machine-countable

The S1 spec contains an elimination clause —
*"A game may end early if a team no longer has any more [Units](#Units) or [CityTiles](#CityTiles). Then the other team wins."*
— confirmed at engine level by `src/logic.ts`, whose guard comment reads
`// over if at least one team has no units left or city tiles` over the test
`if (game.getTeamsUnits(team).size + cityCount[team] === 0) {`.

**And nothing in the rules can drive that count to zero.** Machine-counted over the
whole S1 specification, case-insensitive and whitespace-flattened: `attack` **0**,
`damage` **0**, `combat` **0**, `kill` **0**, `health` **0**, `weapon` **0**,
`shoot` **0**, `hp` **0**. The only unit-on-unit interaction *cancels the move*. So the
field's total convergence on economy in S1 is **not** convergent evidence that economy
is correct — it is the only legal strategy. **Procedural consequence: when a sweep
reports "league X also won on economy", check whether league X could kill at all.**
File: [`lux-s1-a-kill-condition-with-no-kill-mechanism`](lux-s1-a-kill-condition-with-no-kill-mechanism.md).

### 2. Lux S2 is the fix, and the designers wrote down what they were fixing

Same-class collisions changed from a trade into a contest — previously
*"they both were destroyed"*, and at v2.0.0
*"all units with less power are destroyed and the unit with the most power in the collision loses half of the power of the unit with the 2nd most power"*.
The price of aggression moved from a capital loss to an operating expense.

In the same v2.0.0 block, the **scoring** substance was made to pay income —
*"Each tile of lichen owned and connected to a factory gives 1 power to the factory each turn"* —
alongside `Weather is removed` and
*"It takes 20 lichen to grow to new tiles instead of 10 now."*

**But the S2 kill is starvation, not HP**, and this is the discount factor for the
entire S2 corpus: *"If there is no water left, the nuclear reactor that powers the
factory will explode, destroying the factory and leaving behind 50 rubble on each of
the 3x3 tiles."* Our core has 500 HP and no upkeep — machine-checked over
`docs/reference/official-docs.md`: `upkeep` **0**, `starv` **0**, `deplete` **0**,
`exhaust` **0**, `runs out` **0**. **Every S2 competitor's "aggression" is the same
single tactic — interpose units between an enemy factory and its ice — and it is not
four independent convergent data points.**
Files: [`same-class-collisions-changed-from-trade-to-winner`](same-class-collisions-changed-from-trade-to-winner.md),
[`the-scoring-substance-pays-income`](the-scoring-substance-pays-income.md),
[`the-kill-mechanism-was-starvation-not-hp`](the-kill-mechanism-was-starvation-not-hp.md).

### 3. THE COUNTERWEIGHT, filed rather than cherry-picked — designers removed decisiveness on purpose, twice

**Lux S1 v2.0.2 made the contested resource renewable:** *"Wood auto regrows each turn
by 1% of its current valued rounded upwards and is capped at 400."* (their typo,
preserved; later raised to 2.5%). **TIER 2 confirmation from the eventual winner**,
Toad Brigade, who had won a sprint prize with the strategy that was nerfed:
*"However, after the rules change, (where wood regrowth was added and fuel costs were
reduced to weaken swarming scorched earth strategies)"*.

**And Lux S1 v3.0.0 states the organisers' objective function outright:** *"Our goals
with game designs are to always maintain some kind of logarithimc curve on a time+skill
vs bot performance graph, with this simple strategy adding a discontinuity to that."*
(their typo, preserved). **They delete discontinuities on sight.**

**This argues against an assumption our programme makes without stating it.** The whole
incidence re-aim presumes a step change is available. Across five leagues the sourced
picture is one-directional — Lux S1 twice, Battlecode's map pool, Halite II's veto
(*"Survival is part of the meta"*) — with **BC2020's deletion of the score fallback as
the single documented counterexample.** That is not proof no discontinuity exists here;
our organisers are not Lux's. It is a reason the prior should be lower than the
programme currently sets it.
Files: [`they-made-the-resource-renewable-to-weaken-denial`](they-made-the-resource-renewable-to-weaken-denial.md),
[`designers-flattened-the-skill-discontinuity`](designers-flattened-the-skill-discontinuity.md).

### 4. The competitor material is about DENIAL, and denial does not kill here

adamslay (23rd of 637) records the S2 meta moving from collection to denial and then
escalating into escort-the-escort, never exiting. ry_andy_ (**1st of 646**) paid a
permanent positional price for it —
*"purposely choosing an otherwise suboptimal factory spawn position if it meant I was
well-positioned to deny ice to an opponent’s factory"* — and **records his own
conversion failure against an opponent who rerouted flexibly**:
*"This resulted in very few factory kills, and I just had to live with the otherwise
suboptimal factory placements."*

**The failure half transfers better than the success half.** Denial fails against an
opponent who reroutes, and our opponents reroute trivially — conveyors are 3 Ti a tile,
ore clusters are plural, harvesters re-site in a few rounds. **The flg-shaped counter
is our default, not our exception.**

Where denial *does* have purchase here is **the tiebreak table, not the core**: key 2
(`most harvesters`) is a live count of 30 HP buildings a sentinel kills in two shots,
and key 1 (`most titanium collected`) is a rate you can suppress. That is a real road
and it is not the core-kill road.
Files: [`the-lever-was-denial-not-damage`](the-lever-was-denial-not-damage.md),
[`pay-a-positional-price-to-deny`](pay-a-positional-price-to-deny.md).

### 5. Two top-3 authors independently diagnose 17A's structural finding IN THEIR OWN BOTS

**Tigga (2nd) watched his bot release a kill it had already won:** *"For example, I've
seen it stop camping the ice of a factory with very low water where camping it would
have killed the factory."* A per-turn allocator that reprices its units every turn will
withdraw from any assault whose payoff is discontinuous — the marginal round is worth
nothing until the last one is worth everything. **That is 17A's finding, from the
inside, by a top-2 competitor.**

**Philipp Kostuch (6th) held the material and never scheduled the attack:** *"I should
in theory be able to focus fire a single factory overwhelming it with 6+ Heavy before
turn 500 and maybe taking out a factory early"*, under a writeup titled *"lots of focus
on domestic admin, not enough on fighting"*. **The gap he names is not resources and
not unit count — it is that no code path existed whose job was to spend a lead on the
win condition.** Our own numbers are the same shape: we bank and do not spend, the
economy is not our constraint, and **`sporks` at #1 builds 1.99 gunners per side-game
against our 1.95 — we are not under-buying weapons, we are under-directing them.**
Files: [`it-walked-away-from-a-kill-it-had-won`](it-walked-away-from-a-kill-it-had-won.md),

## Audit — and the summary was audited SEPARATELY

Per the s25 method finding (**14 of 18 near-misses were compressions in the *summary*,
which had never been machine-checked**), this document was run through the same literal
checker as the tactic files, as a separate pass, not as a spot-check.

**Result: 119 quoted spans machine-verified verbatim — 99 across the 13 tactic files
and 20 in this summary — 0 unverified, 0 cut.** Upstream of that, **45 candidate
strings** were checked against the raw bytes during discovery: **44 passed on the first
form tried, 1 failed and was corrected** (see below).

**Method used, as the INDEX specifies it:** extract → strip markup → decode HTML
entities → flatten whitespace **including `\f` and `\r`** → grep the **literal**
string. Each candidate was checked against three views of its source (raw-flattened,
entity-decoded-flattened, markup-stripped-flattened) so that literal markdown link
syntax and proxy-normalised entities both have a view that matches them. **No model was
asked whether a string was present.**

### The one failure, and it is the per-string glyph trap in the flesh

Item 10's headline quote from **ry_andy_, 1st of 646** was relayed with an **ASCII**
apostrophe in `opponent's`. The source uses a **CURLY** `’` in that word while other
apostrophes in the same file are ASCII. The ASCII grep returned a clean, confident
FAIL across all three views. **Checking glyphs per *document* rather than per *string*
would have cut a true quote from the competition winner's own writeup** — the exact
false negative the INDEX's method block exists to prevent, and the third time this
library has recorded it. Corrected and filed with the curly form, and the trap is noted
inline in the file so a future re-grep does not re-trip it.

### THREE NEAR-MISSES CAUGHT IN MY OWN DRAFTS BY THE CHECKER, and all three are the same family

The mechanical pass over my own output — not over the sources — flagged three spans in
the first draft of the tactic files. All three are **paraphrase-into-unmarked-quotation**,
the failure the INDEX names as the specific one, arriving in a form that looks harmless:

1. **An ellipsis inside quotation marks.** I rendered `CLAUDE.md`'s titanium rule as
   `moves physically through the map … separate from the global pool…` (specimen of the defect, not a quotation). The elision is
   honest and the sentence is real, but **an elided quote can never be re-verified by a
   literal grep**, which makes it indistinguishable from a fabrication to the next
   auditor. Replaced with the full sentence.
2. **The same defect in a competitor quote** — Philipp Kostuch's `should use … to create
   attackers` (specimen). Replaced with the full unelided span, which is now a blockquote.
3. **My own phrasing wearing quotation marks.** In
   [`the-kill-mechanism-was-starvation-not-hp`](the-kill-mechanism-was-starvation-not-hp.md)
   I set the contrast `how to win` / `how to win the tiebreak` in quote styling.
   Nobody said either phrase. Changed to bold, with an explicit note that they are mine.

**The generalisable one is (1) and (2): an ellipsis is a silent opt-out of the library's
own verification procedure.** Three files in this sweep would have carried spans that no
future re-grep could confirm or refute. **Recommendation for the INDEX method block: quote
in full or split into two quotes — never elide inside quotation marks.**
