# Tactics sweep 17b — CHEAP-CLOSE vs EXPENSIVE-FAR, re-aimed at the inverted kill mix

**Research arm, 2026-08-09.** A re-aim of topics 5 / 5b / 8 / 8b at a measured surprise:
**against ≥1700-rated teams, cores die 53.1% to gunners, 44.4% to sentinels, 2.5% to
builder melee; our own kill mix is 22.7 / 69.2 / 8.1.** They win with the cheap,
short-range, obstacle-blocked turret; we win with the expensive, long-range,
obstacle-piercing one. Their core costs 1,019 damage to kill and ours 1,596.

**21 files. 94 machine-audited quoted spans; 88 verified verbatim against raw source
bytes, 2 are quotations of the sweep brief and labelled as such, 4 were corrected during
the audit (three bracketed completions and one over-long span cut at an inline
hyperlink). 0 unverified claims are presented as quotations.** Legs: 22 Battlecode
postmortems 2019-2026 (read locally), Terminal (shipped config + decompiled engine),
Screeps (engine source + docs + wiki), CodinGame Code Royale / Spring Challenge 2021,
Kaggle Kore, Lux S1/S2, Halite III, and RTS/tower-defence theory.

## THE HEADLINE, and it narrows the search rather than answering it

> **The mix is not a cost decision. On build cost, ammunition cost, HP per titanium and
> the cost-scale externality, our gunner and our sentinel are within roughly 10% of each
> other, and the two largest gaps point in opposite directions. So if the top tier's
> gunner-heavy kill mix is a real mechanism, the mechanism is GEOMETRIC — reach, firing
> line, rotation, or where the turret can be planted — and not economic.**

**Three independent routes reach that conclusion**, which is why I am willing to state it
flatly:

1. **The arithmetic** ([`the-turret-mix-is-not-a-cost-decision`](the-turret-mix-is-not-a-cost-decision.md)).
   Ammunition: 4/7 = **0.5714 Ti/damage** for a gunner against 10/18 = **0.5556** for a
   sentinel — a 2.9% gap. Build: **2.857·s** Ti per point of standing damage for a gunner
   against **3.333·s** — a 14% gap the other way. Break-even for the sentinel's build
   premium: **D > 630·s damage from one turret**, i.e. at a mid-game scale of 2.0 more
   than the entire 1,019 damage it takes to kill a top-tier core. Equal-firepower fleets:
   9 gunners cost 324 Ti and 7 sentinels 336 Ti at scale 1.0.
2. **The falloff route** ([`range-buys-damage-elsewhere-and-buys-nothing-here`](range-buys-damage-elsewhere-and-buys-nothing-here.md)).
   Screeps is the only league that prices range against damage, and it does so steeply —
   `TOWER_POWER_ATTACK: 600` at range ≤ 5 decaying to 150 at ≥ 20, 4:1, at a constant
   10 energy per shot. **We have no falloff at all.** Every "the long-range one must be
   stronger" intuition is imported from a game we are not playing.
3. **The doctrine route** ([`the-case-for-many-cheap-is-earliness-and-area-not-damage`](the-case-for-many-cheap-is-earliness-and-area-not-damage.md)).
   The canonical published case for massing the cheap emplacement names three advantages —
   *"they are often available earlier than more powerful defensive structures. They can be
   much cheaper"* and *"players can build several towers over a wider area for the cost of
   one more powerful defensive structure"*. **All three fail here:** no tech gate, a 3.6%
   price gap at equal firepower, and neither of our turrets covers an *area* — both fire a
   single-tile ray, 3 tiles for a gunner and 5 for a sentinel.

**⚠ TWO CORRECTIONS TO THE BRIEF'S OWN ARITHMETIC, both of which changed the answer.** The
brief gave *"a gunner's shot costs 4 Ti of ammo for 7 dmg (1.75 Ti/dmg) and a sentinel's
costs 10 Ti for 18 dmg (0.556 Ti/dmg)"* — **the two figures are in opposite units** (1.75
is damage per titanium; the comparable prices are 0.571 and 0.556 Ti/damage, a 2.9% gap,
not a 3× one). And the brief called the gunner *higher-DPS-per-round*: our own reference
doc says **"18 every 2 rounds against 7 every round"**, so the sentinel is the higher-DPS
turret, 9/round against 7. A 3× ammo gap and a gunner DPS advantage would have made this a
cost story; neither exists.

## (E) THE MECHANISM-VS-MARKER CALL, STATED PLAINLY

**Mostly MARKER. One narrow mechanism survives, and it is testable.**

**Why marker.** A gunner's line *"stops at the first targetable tile (a builder bot or a
building) in its facing direction"* and its cardinal ray is 3 tiles. **So a gunner can be
credited with a core kill only if it stood within 3 tiles of the core with nothing —
not one enemy barrier, not one enemy conveyor, not one of our own bots — on its axis.** A
sentinel needs neither condition. **A high gunner share of core damage is therefore, in
the first instance, a measurement of how close and how clean the approach was.** It is the
approach reported in a different unit.

**Three external supports, all documented, none of which I had before this sweep:**

- **A top-three finisher's tower-heavy mix was an artefact its author did not choose, and
  the field built counters against the phantom.** Agade (Code Royale, 3rd): *"But very
  often this was overridden by a "Knight danger" criterion, and since my search algorithm
  only builds towers that is mostly what my AI did."* — while Shingy (9th) wrote *"Try not
  to walk into enemy tower range."* explaining *"This was mainly for Agade's strategy of
  covering the map with towers."* Agade's own verdict: *"I'm not very proud of this AI
  (tower spamming, …)"*. **This also narrows one of our own library's readings — see the
  correction note below.**
  [`the-tower-heavy-mix-was-an-artefact-not-a-doctrine`](the-tower-heavy-mix-was-an-artefact-not-a-doctrine.md)
- **The only author in the corpus who ships a bot to a bot ladder says prevalence there
  measures non-punishment, not strength.** Jay Scott: *"But there is a reason that so many
  bots play turtle strategies. Turtling is strong against bots which do not adapt, which
  is most of them."* **We are a bot on a bot ladder measuring bots.**
  [`turtling-persists-because-nobody-punishes-it`](turtling-persists-because-nobody-punishes-it.md)
- **A team documented building the expensive static structure purely as a spending sink.**
  5 Musketeers (BC2022): *"When obese, we wanted to be making more builders. This is
  because the builders can use that excess lead to build watchtowers."* — under a per-turn
  spend cap. **We have a harder rate cap (≤1 builder spawn per turn) and a measured idle
  bank**, so a mix that is really a spending artefact is a live hypothesis for *us* as
  well as for them. [`the-emplacement-as-a-spending-sink`](the-emplacement-as-a-spending-sink.md)

**The one surviving mechanism, and it is narrow:** *once you are close with a clear line,
the gunner is the correct weapon and we may be failing to switch.* At ≤3 tiles against an
unscreened target, the sentinel's entire premium — reach and obstacle-piercing — is
property we are paying for and not using, and the gunner is 14% cheaper per point of
standing damage and is the only turret that can **rotate**. That converts the surprise
into a **siting predicate rather than a build quota**, which is exactly what the garrison
error lacked:

> clear line **and** target within r²=13 → **gunner**;
> blocked line **or** target beyond r²=13 → **sentinel**.

[`a-gunner-kill-is-a-clear-line-not-a-doctrine`](a-gunner-kill-is-a-clear-line-not-a-doctrine.md)

## (A) WHAT STRONG COMPETITORS ACTUALLY DID

**Nobody published a ratio. Four of them published a small integer with a geometry or
threat condition attached.**

| who | rule | form |
|---|---|---|
| **Just Woke Up** (BC2025, **winners**) | *"we have less than 2 defense towers, the tower was in a choke point, determined by the fact that it had at least 1 wall on two opposite sides of the tower, and finally that the ruin was within 50 units squared of the center of the map"* | hard cap + wall-pair choke test + forwardness |
| **robostac** (Code Royale, **1st**) | *"If I had less than 5 towers, more than 2 mines and less towers than mines I added a large negative penalty."* | penalty **band** inside an eval, with an opponent-state escape clause |
| **Azkellas** (Code Royale, 6th) | *"I'd usually ask for 3/4 towers if the enemy has one barracks, 6 if s/he has severals, and 2/0 if I'm confident I am playing against a camper that will not attack me."* | count indexed on **enemy production** — including **zero** |
| **Lostkids** (Terminal, 3rd APAC) | *"Three upgraded turrets in the front line near the edge on each side"* | few, strong, **front**, on the used approach axes |

**The buildable one is the wall-pair choke test** — two `get_tile_env` lookups, and it is
sharper here than where it came from, because a corridor with walls on both flanks is
exactly the geometry in which a gunner's single-tile ray is not a weakness.
[`cap-the-expensive-emplacement-and-gate-it-on-a-choke`](cap-the-expensive-emplacement-and-gate-it-on-a-choke.md) ·
[`set-the-emplacement-count-from-the-enemys-production`](set-the-emplacement-count-from-the-enemys-production.md)

## (B) WHAT DECIDED THE MIX — four answers, and RANGE is not one of them

1. **Target-acquisition rate.** The published theory rules range out by name: *"the squared
   law has nothing to do with range – what is really important is the rate of acquiring
   new targets."* The N² payoff for going numerous is contingent on every structure
   actually firing — which for us is decided at build time by facing, line and ammunition.
   Applying the same paper's default value α = dmg × HP: **gunner 175, sentinel 360 — the
   sentinel is worth 2.06× a gunner, and 1.37× per titanium**, the opposite of where the
   kill mix points, *conditional on both firing*.
   [`the-square-law-is-about-target-acquisition-not-range`](the-square-law-is-about-target-acquisition-not-range.md)
2. **The size of the committed lump under a snowball.** Two independent Battlecode sources:
   5 Musketeers, *"no teams built any labs or sages, and very few teams built watchtowers.
   The games were won and lost with soldiers."* and, after a price cut, *"It still wasn't
   worth it to build anything expensive."*; The Kragle three years later, *"In 2022,
   watchtowers were weak since they were a large investment, which was a death sentence in
   that year's game since cheap units could snowball very quickly."* **Our lumps are 20 and
   30 Ti and our snowball is weak and partly self-cancelling, so this argument binds LESS
   here — which points toward the sentinel, not away.**
   [`the-lump-investment-is-the-thing-that-loses`](the-lump-investment-is-the-thing-that-loses.md)
3. **Relative price against the counter.** BC2020's champions steered the game so the
   opponent had to buy the dearer object: *"If the opponent decides to build a Net Gun to
   counter it, they would have to spend 100 soup more than us"*; confused, independently,
   *"building a net gun was 250 while building a drone factory was 150, leaving us more
   advantageous in economy"*. Run on us, this says almost nothing about gunner-vs-sentinel
   (removal cost tracks build cost for both) and a great deal about **barriers**, whose
   removal-to-build ratio is 10:1.
   [`price-the-emplacement-against-what-counters-it`](price-the-emplacement-against-what-counters-it.md)
4. **Exposure — enemy time-in-range.** The one competitor who states an objective function
   for a static line does not say coverage: *"the structure maximises the exposure of our
   turrets on enemy's units, thereby maximising damage on enemy's units"*, and names the
   counter to a spread line — *"we use one batch of scouts so that turrets do not have time
   to destroy all scouts along the path of traversal."* For ray-shaped turrets this is a
   much better objective than tiles-covered, and it re-prices the barrier as an **exposure
   multiplier** rather than as HP.
   [`maximise-time-in-range-not-tiles-covered`](maximise-time-in-range-not-tiles-covered.md)

## (C) COST SCALING — a clean cross-league NEGATIVE, and one live idea

**No league in the main survey prices the Nth structure higher than the first.** Screeps
uses a per-RCL **count cap** with a flat 5,000 energy tower and has no scaling constant at
all; Terminal is flat (`"cost1": 2.0` for the turret, `1.0` for the wall, and a grep of
the shipped config for every scaling idiom returns zero); Battlecode's rising prices are
per-turn **spend caps** or tiered **upgrade** prices, never per-count.
[`nobody-else-has-a-rising-build-cost`](nobody-else-has-a-rising-build-cost.md)

**Two outside the main survey do have it**, and only one produced doctrine — and that
doctrine is about **order**, not counts. CodinGame Spring Challenge 2021: *"Growing a size
1 tree into a size 2 tree costs 3 sun points + the number of size 2 trees you already
own."* The player rule that fell out: *"impose an order in actions during a day : first
COMPLETE, then GROW…"* because *"It usually make actions cheaper in this order"*.

> **⇒ THE ONE GENUINELY NEW BUILD IDEA IN THIS SWEEP: when a demolition and a build are
> both planned, DEMOLISH FIRST.** Our scale *"decreases again when an entity is
> destroyed"*, so at scale 2.0 a replacement sentinel prices at `floor(1.8 × 30) = 54`
> instead of `floor(2.0 × 30) = 60`, and the discount applies to **every** build in that
> window. **⚠ It rests on one unprobed fact — whether `destroy()` updates the scale within
> the same round or only at end of round. Probe `get_scale_percent()` either side of a
> `destroy()` before building anything on it.**
> [`a-rising-price-makes-the-ORDER-of-actions-matter`](a-rising-price-makes-the-ORDER-of-actions-matter.md)

**And the finding that kills cost-scaling as an explanation of the mix:** our `+20%` is
**identical** for a gunner and a sentinel, and it is one **global team-wide** number
(`docs/game-model.md:393-402`, measured) that multiplies *every* build. It taxes **count**,
not type — so per titanium spent a gunner costs 0.0100 scale against a sentinel's 0.00667.
**Cost scaling mildly favours the EXPENSIVE turret**, which is what we already build.

## (D) FRIENDLY LINE-BLOCKING — negative in every league, positive in two colony sims

**No competitive league surveyed has a player's own units blocking their own ranged
attack.** Screeps' tower attack path computes only Chebyshev range and never consults the
map; Terminal's targeting is a rasterised disc with no intervening-tile term; Code
Royale's referee picks the nearest enemy creep by distance; 22 Battlecode postmortems
mention the mechanic zero times; the RTS canon's "blocking" is always **movement** or
**splash**. Everything the library has imported about turret spacing and massing was
written by people whose turrets could not blind each other.
[`friendly-line-blocking-has-no-rts-precedent`](friendly-line-blocking-has-no-rts-precedent.md)

**The two precedents that do exist are outside competitive play, and they agree:**

- **Dwarf Fortress**, on a massed battery of same-facing engines aimed down one corridor:
  *"Because siege engines are 3x3, they need to be staggered, so each one fires through
  the edge of the one ahead of it."*
- **RimWorld**, which separates the classes — *"In addition, walls block line of fire."*
  versus *"Barricades and sandbags provide 55% cover and do not block line of fire."* —
  and prescribes *"a mix of walls and barricades/sandbags"* with *"The exact ratio will
  depend on what angles the colonist needs to fire at"*.
- **Factorio** supplies the same conclusion from the turret side: its one facing-limited
  turret is the one told *"they have a limited firing arc, and should therefore be placed
  at choke points or behind walls."*

**Translated: two gunners in file on one axis are one gunner and one 20 Ti barrier.
Barriers may go anywhere in front of a SENTINEL and nowhere in front of a GUNNER. The
gunner's ray is a no-build, no-stand corridor for our own side** — the same rule that
sweep 7 already found for healer seats, generalised.
[`stagger-the-file-so-each-gun-clears-the-one-ahead`](stagger-the-file-so-each-gun-clears-the-one-ahead.md)

## A NARROW CORRECTION TO SWEEP 8, made by this sweep and recorded rather than buried

[`2026-08-09-sweep-8.md`](2026-08-09-sweep-8.md) presents Agade's site-scoring formulas —
tower value rising as distance-to-enemy falls, mine value rising as it grows — as the
encoding of his doctrine. **The formulas are genuine and re-verified; what they governed
was narrower than the sweep implies.** They sorted *which sites* to target, and the branch
that used them was, by his own account, *"very often this was overridden"*, with the fallback
search able to build only towers. **So "forward-ness positive" stands as a published
site-choice weight, and must not be upgraded into an explanation of why his bot was
tower-heavy.** Sweep 8's other three sources are untouched.

## METHOD FINDINGS — three new traps for the INDEX's verification block

1. **The glyph trap is PER-STRING, not per-document.** Just Woke Up's BC2025 PDF renders
   *"that's"* with an **ASCII** apostrophe in one sentence and *"wasn't"* with a **curly**
   one elsewhere in the same file. A document-level glyph assumption produces false
   negatives inside documents that otherwise verify.
2. **Markup interleaved inside numerals defeats a literal grep even after whitespace
   flattening.** CodinGame's Spring Challenge 2021 statement writes *size `<const>1</const>`
   tree*, so the rules sentence does not exist as a literal string in the raw bytes and
   must be verified after stripping tags. HTML entities do the same thing — the Screeps
   wiki's *'centered'* is stored as `&#x27;centered&#x27;`.
3. **JSON-escaped punctuation is a third variant of the same trap.** The Wayback capture
   of Kaggle's Kore rules escapes every `+` to its six-character unicode form, so the
   published cost sentence never greps. **The general rule: extract → strip markup →
   decode entities → flatten whitespace → grep.** Two of the four corrections this sweep
   made to its own drafts were caught by exactly this step.

## WHAT I COULD NOT VERIFY, AND WHAT I DID NOT DO

- **The Kaggle Kore 2022 first-place quote *"An adjacent attack is intended to die…"*
  remains UNVERIFIED**, as it was after sweep 8. The discussion page is JS-rendered, the
  Wayback capture contains only the competition description, and Kaggle's discussion API
  needs an authenticated token. **It is quoted nowhere in this sweep.**
- **GemCraft's rising tower price is documented and its doctrine is not.** *"the first
  tower costs 200 mana … and each subsequent one costs an additional 75 mana"* is verified;
  the wiki says nothing about what players did with it.
- **Terminal's official rules page is still unretrievable** — every Terminal claim here
  rests on shipped config or decompiled engine bytecode, and the shipped config and the
  engine's hardcoded fallback carry **two different balance patches**, both live at HEAD.
  All numbers quoted are the shipped-config set.
- **I did not run any corpus cut.** The decisive test of the mechanism-vs-marker question —
  *at the moment of the killing shot, what was the distance and line-clearance of the
  killing turret, ours vs theirs?* — is not run and is not claimed. Everything in the (E)
  section is a reading of the rules plus external analogues.
- **I did not re-probe the cost-scale model.** §5 of the arithmetic file rests on
  `docs/game-model.md` being right that there is a single global additive scale; it is
  marked measured there, and the project CLAUDE.md's *"rises as you build more of that
  category"* contradicts it. **Worth a probe before anything is built on the scale
  numbers.**
- Warcraft III tower doctrine, Halite postmortems, and CodinGame's wider catalogue were
  not swept for these questions.
