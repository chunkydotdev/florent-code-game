# Tactics sweep 3 — wheel topic 12: unit-displacement mechanics

**Research arm, session 23, 2026-08-09.** Opus subagent, model wired explicitly.

**Why this sweep matters more than the wheel position suggests:** our live bot
*is* the launcher bot (v90 "Heimdall 1 (launcher relight)"), and s22 refuted late
offensive insertion for us (raider life 43 → 6 rounds by r150; **2.34% of r200+
forward throws ever land an attack on the enemy core**). The mechanic is unusual
and powerful and we may be pointing it the wrong way.

---

## THE INVERSION — the headline

**The sourced evidence points against our current use, and it comes from the one
league that ran our exact mechanic.**

**Battlecode 2020's Delivery Drone has our Launcher's verb signature almost
exactly**: adjacent pickup, **either team**, place on a legal adjacent tile, and
the held unit is frozen. From the BC2020 spec: *"The picked up unit is frozen; it
cannot execute any code and its remaining cooldown is constant, until it is
dropped by the drone."*

**That field converged on grabbing the ENEMY's unit — not on ferrying their own
into the enemy base.** The High Ground: *"every top team, no matter what their
strategy, now had a drone harass. A drone harass was essential to attempt to
sabotage your opponents' strategy, no matter what it was."* The
ferry-your-own-units-forward use is documented but described as a **terrain
workaround**, never as the win condition.

**And the collapse shape in our own data is explained, not contradicted, by that
literature.** Team Smite (BC2020, 2nd place): attackers needed a *"Drone :
Opponent Net Gun ratio of 10:1 or more … to guarantee a successful attack."* The
High Ground: once a defence was set up *"it was almost impossible to break
through… The only way to stop these teams was to harass them enough that they
couldn't get their cookie up."* **Our 43 → 6 round raider lifetime and 2.34%
post-r200 core-touch is that curve, measured.** The prescription in the
literature is *earlier, or not at all* — and since the field builds ~2
turrets/game to our 0.2, we are on the wrong side of that race in both directions.

**This is independent corroboration of the builder's forward-road closure
(09:05), arrived at from a completely different evidence path.**

**What the evidence supports positively, in descending confidence:** defensive
interception → displace-don't-kill → imprisonment (unverified, cheap to falsify)
→ logistics throwing.

**Where I refuse to manufacture a recommendation:** I found **no sourced case of
a team throwing an enemy unit purely to waste its turns and measuring that
against killing it.** The closest is CodinGame's *"wind heroes away from the sweet
spot"* — a defensive counter in a game with no kills at all — and BC2020's
harass, where the throw *was* the kill. **Sub-question 2 rests on strong analogy
plus our own cost-scale rule, not on a direct measured precedent. Treat the
arithmetic as the evidence, not the literature.**

**Counter-play warning, both ways:** an enemy launcher costs 20 Ti and can pick up
**our** builder bots. Our insertion strategy is single-building-counterable, and
the BC2020 record says opponents adopt the counter fast once the mechanic is
visible on the ladder.

---

## Tactics

```
---
tactic: Defensive interception — grab the incoming attacker as it arrives
source: https://battlecode.org/assets/files/postmortem-2020-the-high-ground.pdf
origin: Battlecode 2020 / The High Ground (4th place)
evidence: documented
transfers: yes
---
WHAT IT IS — In their round-of-16 against top rush team Kryptonite: "We won the
games where they either failed to path to us or we happened to pick up and drown
their rushing miner right as it was reaching our base." The displacement verb as
GOALKEEPER, not spear. They note the reverse cost: "Unlike many other teams, we
didn't keep a drone back to defend rushes, which made us weaker vs rush teams."
WHY IT MIGHT TRANSFER — This is the inversion, and OUR RULESET MAKES IT STRONGER
THAN THEIRS. An enemy builder inside our base can be answered in exactly two ways:
turret fire (24-30 Ti of converted ammo to kill 40 HP) or a launcher throw (0
ammo, 0 titanium, facing-independent). Our own builder bots CANNOT HELP AT ALL --
builder attack cannot hit builder bots. And the adjacency comes FREE: an enemy
builder attacking our building MUST be orthogonally adjacent to it, which is
exactly the geometry the launcher needs.
WHAT WOULD KILL IT — If launch has a long action cooldown, one launcher cannot
keep up with several raiders. If raiders hit perimeter buildings rather than tiles
near the launcher, it never gets a grab.
BUILDER HOOK — SMALLEST TEST IN THIS SWEEP: one launcher orthogonally adjacent to
the core footprint, one rule -- if any enemy builder is adjacent, throw it to the
farthest passable tile from our core. Measure enemy damage dealt to our core per
game vs current. No other change.
```

```
---
tactic: Displace, don't kill — killing refunds their cost scale
source: https://forum.codingame.com/t/spring-challenge-2022-feedbacks-strategies/195736
origin: CodinGame Spring Challenge 2022 "Spider Attack", Legend-league defensive play
evidence: documented
transfers: yes
---
WHAT IT IS — The answer to the wind cannon was not to kill the cannoneer (heroes
are unkillable there) but to MOVE it. Dapps: "I had an approach that would try to
intercept spiders as much as possible and if they couldn't, wind heroes away from
the sweet spot."
WHY IT MIGHT TRANSFER — Purest "displace, don't kill" evidence in the sweep, and it
comes from a game where killing was IMPOSSIBLE, so the whole field was forced to
discover the value of wasted enemy turns. In our ruleset killing is possible but
actively bad: destroying an enemy builder REMOVES ITS CONTRIBUTION to their
+20%-per-builder cost scale -- i.e. killing their builder makes their next builder
CHEAPER and frees one of their 50 unit slots. A living, permanently-displaced enemy
builder is a standing tax on their entire build order.
WHAT WOULD KILL IT — Dscarts' caveat transfers: "a persistent canoneer would drain
my mana like this very quickly and most of the time succeed anyway." A persistent
attacker beats a reactive defender on tempo even at zero titanium cost.
BUILDER HOOK — Corpus query first: what IS our opponents' builder-cost scale at
r100/200/400? If they run 5+ builders, every kill we score hands them a discount,
and that number is the argument for switching from kill-priority to throw-priority.
```

```
---
tactic: Imprisonment — throw them into a prebuilt cell and let the heal arithmetic hold it
source: https://superflux.dev/blog/battlesnake-2018
origin: Battlesnake 2018 (emergent, not designed); arithmetic is ours
evidence: documented (for the trap-and-starve principle) / inference (for our implementation)
transfers: partial — GATED ON ONE UNVERIFIED ENGINE FACT
---
WHAT IT IS — Emergent winning behaviour: "my snake would wrap around it, trapping
it. When the 'chicken-snake' decided it had been spinning for long enough and it
was time to eat, there was nowhere to go." Author reports it "worked very well" 1v1.
WHY IT MIGHT TRANSFER — This is where OUR ruleset is BETTER than the source game.
An imprisoned enemy builder costs us almost nothing to hold: they break out by
attacking a barrier at 2 dmg for 2 Ti per turn; we repair at +4 HP for 1 Ti per
turn. HEAL BEATS ATTACK 2x ON RATE AND 2x ON COST SIMULTANEOUSLY, so a maintained
barrier is mathematically unbreakable by a SINGLE enemy builder. Four barriers
(12 Ti before scale) seal a tile completely, because builder bots move CARDINALLY
ONLY -- no diagonal escape. And the prisoner keeps taxing their +20% builder scale
and occupying one of their 50 unit slots for the rest of the match.
WHAT WOULD KILL IT — Two engine facts that decide it: (a) whether can_launch
accepts a target tile fully enclosed by buildings -- if the engine requires
connectivity or a path, the prebuilt-cell play is dead and we must seal manually
after the throw; (b) TWO enemy builders adjacent to the cell deal 4 dmg/turn
against one healer's 4 HP/turn -- an exact tie, so a two-builder rescue breaks it.
**IMPORTANT SCOPE NOTE (research arm):** the builder's 08:52 probe refuted
building a barrier ON TOP OF a standing bot (can_build_barrier=False even though
is_tile_empty=True). THIS IS A DIFFERENT PROPOSITION -- a prebuilt empty cell that
a bot is thrown INTO -- and it is NOT refuted by that probe. Per s22 §4, "refuted
alone is not refuted."
BUILDER HOOK — Highest-value cheap experiment in this sweep: prebuild a 1-tile
cell (4 barriers around an empty tile) inside our territory within r²=26 of a
launcher, then test whether can_launch(enemy_bot_pos, cell_tile) returns True.
That one boolean decides whether this is a strategy or a fantasy. ~12 Ti, one match.
```

```
---
tactic: Early displacement harass on enemy workers (economy denial without kills)
source: https://stonet2000.github.io/battlecode/2020/
origin: Battlecode 2020 — NP-ez pioneered; Java Best Waifu (1st place) industrialised it
evidence: documented
transfers: yes
---
WHAT IT IS — NP-ez "placed fulfillment centers near enemy units near soup locations
that would then make drones to pick up enemy units and drop them into the water."
The effect was not attrition -- enemy "miners and landscapers [were rendered]
useless" and it was "hard for them to lattice, build a wall or mine." The cost
asymmetry is stated explicitly: "the only way to defeat them is to build a costly
250 soup net gun, in comparison to the 150 soup cost drone."
WHY IT MIGHT TRANSFER — Directly. A builder repeatedly thrown off its work tile
cannot build, because our builders cannot act on a turn they move, and a r²=26
throw costs ~5 walk-back turns. Their harvester never gets built; our tiebreak
(delivered → harvesters alive) improves without spending a single ammo.
WHAT WOULD KILL IT — THE BIGGEST STRUCTURAL GAP IN THIS SWEEP: BC2020's drone was
MOBILE and could fly to the victim. Our launcher is a BUILDING. So the harass
version only works where their builders are forced to come to us -- our ore tiles,
our conveyor line, our core.
BUILDER HOOK — Instrument first: from decoded replays, count how often an enemy
builder ends a round orthogonally adjacent to one of our buildings inside our half.
Large => a defensive launcher ring has targets. Near zero => dead on arrival.
```

```
---
tactic: Self-displacement as logistics — throw your own unit for tempo, not combat
source: https://dmtrung.com/blogs/battlecode_writeups.html
origin: Battlecode 2024 (zhugeduck writeup); mechanic also core to BC2020 drones
evidence: documented
transfers: partial
---
WHAT IT IS — Two leagues used throwing purely as transport. BC2024: ducks would
"quickly overcome obstacles by 'throwing the flag' to the other side of the
pond/wall." BC2020 drones "can transport land units across otherwise impassable
barriers… to move to soup locations" and were "the only units allowed to move to
tiles of arbitrary elevation."
WHY IT MIGHT TRANSFER — Our builders move 1 cardinal tile/turn and cannot act on a
turn they move; a throw is ~5 tiles instantly. Getting a builder to a distant ore
patch 5 rounds sooner is pure economy, and it carries NONE of the risk our own data
refuted, because the destination is inside our OWN territory. Note BC2020's caveat
that drones "move twice every three turns" -- transport was SLOWER per tile than
walking and was still worth it purely for reach.
WHAT WOULD KILL IT — If being thrown consumes the thrown bot's action or move
cooldown, the net turn saving shrinks or vanishes. On an 8x8 map a 5-tile hop is
most of the map; on 30x30 it is significant.
BUILDER HOOK — Check whether a thrown bot's get_move_cooldown()/
get_action_cooldown() are altered by the throw. If not: one launcher next to the
core, throwing each newly spawned builder toward the nearest unworked ore. Metric:
round-of-first-harvester and total titanium delivered -- both tiebreak-relevant.
```

```
---
tactic: The wind cannon — mass offensive displacement into the enemy base
source: https://forum.codingame.com/t/spring-challenge-2022-feedbacks-strategies/195736
origin: CodinGame Spring Challenge 2022, Legend league meta
evidence: documented
transfers: partial
---
WHAT IT IS — Heroes at the edge of the enemy base chained WIND casts to shove
neutral monsters into the enemy core faster than defenders could react. BlitzProg:
"The base idea consists of stacking two WINDS on a appropriately placed spider to
blow it into the ennemy base from afar." Dscarts: "It seems maybe a *little* too
lethal for something so simple."
WHY IT MIGHT TRANSFER — It is the ONLY sourced case where OFFENSIVE displacement
was a dominant meta, so it is the fair steelman of what we currently do. But read
what made it work: the thing being thrown was a FREE NEUTRAL UNIT the enemy had to
deal with anyway. The attacker risked nothing.
WHAT WOULD KILL IT — We have no neutral units. Every forward throw spends a 30 Ti
builder, scaled +20% per builder we own, into a place where our own measurement
says it survives 6 rounds by r150 and lands an attack 2.34% of the time after
r200. THE WIND CANNON THREW AMMUNITION IT DID NOT PAY FOR; WE ARE THROWING OUR
ECONOMY.
BUILDER HOOK — None. This block marks that the strongest pro-offence evidence in
the literature depends on a free projectile we do not have.
```

```
---
tactic: Static anti-displacement defence, and the 10:1 rule
source: https://github.com/mvpatel2000/Battlecode2020
origin: Battlecode 2020 / Team Smite (2nd place)
evidence: documented
transfers: yes
---
WHAT IT IS — The counter to drones was the Net Gun, a cheap static building.
Smite's README: "as soon as a drone sees the gun, its dead," and "Attackers needed
a Drone : Opponent Net Gun ratio of 10:1 or more (if the defense has additional
support) to guarantee a successful attack."
WHY IT MIGHT TRANSFER — THIS IS THE LITERATURE EXPLAINING THE SHAPE OF OUR OWN
COLLAPSE. Offensive insertion is a race against static-defence construction, and
once the defence exists the attacker needs an order of magnitude. The sourced
remedy is NOT "insert better later" -- it is "harass early enough that the defence
never gets built."
WHAT WOULD KILL IT — Nothing; it is corroboration. The one caveat makes it WORSE
for us: BC2020 net guns were single-purpose anti-drone, whereas our
gunners/sentinels are general-purpose, so the enemy's anti-launcher defence is
something they are ALREADY BUYING for other reasons. Our insertion window is
narrower than BC2020's, not wider.
BUILDER HOOK — Bound the window empirically: from the corpus, find the earliest
round at which each opponent's first turret exists. Any insertion after that round
is a 10:1 proposition. If the median is under 150, forward insertion should be
TIME-GATED OFF, not tuned.
```

```
---
tactic: Kidnap into a kill zone (hook / force-push into friendly fire)
source: https://www.kjcesports.com/guide/why-you-should-buy-the-force-staff-in-dota-2/
origin: RTS/MOBA theory — Dota 2 Force Staff and Pudge's Meat Hook
evidence: anecdotal
transfers: partial
---
WHAT IT IS — The canonical framing: "you can push enemies into your team… few can
react to a sudden enemy Force cast." Pudge guides describe hooking a target out of
safety into tower range so the tower does the killing.
WHY IT MIGHT TRANSFER — Mechanically it fits: our Launcher is facing-independent at
r²=26, and our Gunner (r²=13) and Sentinel (r²=32) CAN hit enemy builder bots --
only our builder's melee cannot. "Throw the enemy builder onto a tile inside our
sentinel line" is legal and cheap.
WHAT WOULD KILL IT — Our own cost-scale rule argues against finishing the kill. A
sentinel needs 3 shots (30 ammo = 30 Ti) to kill a 40 HP builder, and the reward is
REMOVING that builder's +20% scale contribution -- i.e. we pay 30 Ti to give them a
discount and free a unit slot. Kidnap-into-kill-zone is real but strictly worse
than kidnap-into-jail in OUR ruleset.
BUILDER HOOK — Only if the imprisonment test fails. Even then, prefer throwing into
gunner range and NOT firing -- the threat pins them without triggering the refund.
```

```
---
tactic: Body-block wall made of units ("drone wall")
source: https://battlecode.org/assets/files/postmortem-2020-the-high-ground.pdf
origin: Battlecode 2020 / Steam Locomotive and other turtle teams
evidence: documented
transfers: partial
---
WHAT IT IS — Turtling teams ringed their base with drones as a physical barrier:
"If a full drone wall was formed around your turtle… it was impossible to crunch on
as drones could not move through other drones." The only counter found was exotic
(Bowl of Chowder's self-destruct islands).
WHY IT MIGHT TRANSFER — For us the wall material is Barriers at 3 Ti / 30 HP with
only +1% scale -- far cheaper than any unit -- and cardinal-only movement means a
barrier ring has no diagonal leaks.
WHAT WOULD KILL IT — Barriers are buildings, so enemy builders can attack them, and
ours must be orthogonally adjacent to place each one, which is slow under fire.
Unlike drones, barriers cannot reposition.
BUILDER HOOK — Covered by the imprisonment hook. This block mainly establishes that
"wall of stuff" beat "attack" in a comparable league and needed a special-case
answer to break.
```

```
---
tactic: Mazing — spend the enemy's turns instead of their HP
source: https://forum.c1games.com/t/regarding-maze-algos/472
origin: Terminal (Correlation One), CodeBullet challenge era
evidence: anecdotal
transfers: partial
---
WHAT IT IS — Terminal attackers path automatically around firewalls, so defenders
built mazes lengthening the route and keeping attackers under fire longer. 4 of the
top 8 algos in the CodeBullet challenge were maze algos. Reported weakness: static
mazes "constantly failed on opponents rushing specific corners."
WHY IT MIGHT TRANSFER — Barriers are 3 Ti at +1% scale. Enemy builders move 1
cardinal tile/turn and cannot act on a moving turn. A barrier line adding 6 tiles to
their approach costs us ~3 Ti/tile ONCE and costs them 6 turns per builder, per
trip, forever. Same currency as displacement -- enemy turns -- bought with
construction, and it needs no adjacency.
WHAT WOULD KILL IT — Terminal's attackers were FORCED to path; our enemy builders
are hand-coded and may attack through a barrier (15 turns and 30 Ti per barrier,
arguably a win for us) or route around. Plus the documented corner-rush failure.
BUILDER HOOK — Independent of the launcher question: place a barrier line across the
approach corridor our corpus shows raiders actually using; measure change in
enemy-builder arrival round.
```

```
---
tactic: Forced movement of an enemy unit (Screeps creep pull)
source: https://screeps.com/forum/topic/2437/ptr-changelog-2018-11-01-creep-pull
origin: Screeps, Creep.pull() introduced 2018-11-01
evidence: documented
transfers: no
---
WHAT IT IS — Screeps is the one persistent league that shipped an explicit
unit-moving verb, and the designers made it NON-COERCIVE. The changelog says
"Creeps may belong to different players," but the mechanic requires the target's
cooperation: creep1.pull(creep2); creep2.move(creep1) -- "second creep accepts it."
WHY IT DOES NOT TRANSFER — Consent requirement. Filed so nobody re-searches Screeps
for enemy-displacement precedent. Screeps solves the same problem with TERRAIN
(walls and ramparts), not forced movement.
BUILDER HOOK — None. Stop looking here.
```

```
---
tactic: Collision / ramming as displacement
source: https://www.lux-ai.org/specs-s2
origin: Lux AI Season 2; Halite II collisions
evidence: documented
transfers: no
---
WHAT IT IS — Lux S2 resolves same-tile moves by mass: "heavies destroy lights and
among same units of the same type the one with more power wins." Halite II had
mutually destructive ship collisions.
WHY IT DOES NOT TRANSFER — We have no collision resolution and no ramming verb; two
builder bots simply cannot occupy a tile.
BUILDER HOOK — None. Residue worth keeping (inference, not sourced): standing our
own builder on a chokepoint tile denies it, free, no launcher needed.
```

```
---
tactic: Drop-the-enemy-unit-into-the-hazard (BC2020 drone + water)
source: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/battlecode/battlecode20/master/specs/specs.md
origin: Battlecode 2020 core mechanic; used by NP-ez, Java Best Waifu (1st), The High Ground (4th)
evidence: documented
transfers: partial
---
WHAT IT IS — BC2020's Delivery Drone could pickUpUnit() from an adjacent tile and
dropUnit() on an adjacent empty OR FLOODED tile: "If the unit is placed on a flooded
tile and is not a drone, it is destroyed as usual." Drones could not pick up
buildings or other drones. THE CLOSEST PUBLISHED ANALOGUE TO OUR LAUNCHER THAT
EXISTS.
WHY IT MIGHT TRANSFER — The verb is spec-identical in shape. The lesson that matters
is that THE SAME VERB SERVED TWO COMPLETELY DIFFERENT STRATEGIES -- carry-your-own
forward, and grab-theirs-and-delete-it -- and the competitive field converged on the
second.
WHAT WOULD KILL IT — We have NO HAZARD TERRAIN. Environment is only EMPTY / WALL /
ORE_TITANIUM and a thrown bot lands at full HP on a passable tile. The KILL half does
not transfer at all; only the displacement half does.
BUILDER HOOK — Verify in the engine whether can_launch adjacency is 4-way or 8-way,
and whether the throw target must be REACHABLE or merely is_tile_passable. Both
answers gate every tactic in this file.
```
