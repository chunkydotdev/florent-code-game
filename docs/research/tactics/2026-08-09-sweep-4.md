# Tactics sweep 4 — wheel topic 8: economy, saturation, expansion timing

**Research arm, session 23, 2026-08-09.** Opus subagent, model wired explicitly.
Sources: Screeps official docs, Halite III postmortems, Battlecode 2020/2022/2023/2025
postmortem PDFs, CodinGame Spring 2021 referee source, StarCraft II mining measurements.

**Read with `../middle-game-hazard-and-economy-2026-08-09.md`**, which runs this
sweep's corpus hooks and reaches a conclusion the sweep could not: **the economy is
not our binding constraint — middle-game survival is.**

## The arithmetic (the sweep's headline)

Harvester: 20 Ti base, 2.5 Ti/round → **8-round payback**. "+5% each" computed both
additively and multiplicatively; "destroying removes its contribution" means N is the
**live** count.

| N live | additive | payback | multiplicative | payback |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 20 | 8 | 20 | 8 |
| 20 | 39 | 16 | 50 | 20 |
| 50 | 69 | 28 | 218 | 87 |
| 100 | 119 | 48 | 2504 | 1002 |

**Cost scaling never binds on harvesters.** Break-even is ~2,482 (additive) or ~100
(multiplicative, harsh reading) simultaneous harvesters — beyond any map's ore supply.
**It binds hard on the +20% categories**, which produce no revenue: the 20th builder
bot costs 958 Ti multiplicatively. **`MAX_TEAM_UNITS = 50` is not our ceiling; the
cost curve is.**

## Tactics

```
---
tactic: Never float resources
source: https://battlecode.org/assets/files/postmortem-2025-the-kragle.pdf
origin: Battlecode 2025 / The Kragle, sect 2.3.1 "Floating Resources"
evidence: documented
transfers: yes
---
WHAT IT IS — A top team's postmortem of our exact failure mode: "we would float tons
of paint in all stages of the game... we could have a paint lead on paper, but if all
of that paint was not spendable, it wasn't a paint lead in practice." Their footnote:
"floating resources is bad."
WHY IT TRANSFERS — We end r200-300 holding MORE titanium than Ouroboros having bought
a twelfth the ammunition. And it is worse for us than for them: titanium stored is only
the THIRD tiebreak. VERIFIED IN OUR CORPUS: ti_collected is non-decreasing in 6,453 of
6,454 team-games while ti_end falls in 56.9% -- they are different counters, so
SPENDING CANNOT COST US TIEBREAK KEY #1. convert_ammo is 1:1, same-turn, and does not
consume the core's action cooldown: a free, always-available sink we are not using.
WHAT WOULD KILL IT — If bought ammo is systematically wasted (turrets firing at
nothing, or dying before firing), banking beats converting. Our own 2.2:1 heal-vs-damage
finding is a real argument against converting blindly.
BUILDER HOOK — Ammo floor sized to ONE VOLLEY: reserve = 10*sentinels + 4*gunners,
convert everything above it. One conditional in the core turn.
```

```
---
tactic: Buy the second tiebreak late — harvesters alive is a SNAPSHOT, not a stream
source: https://battlecode.org/assets/files/postmortem-2022-5-musketeers.pdf
origin: Battlecode 2022 / 5 Musketeers (semifinalists) — the "transmute 1 gold" tiebreak module
evidence: documented (the principle) / inference (our application)
transfers: yes
---
WHAT IT IS — 5 Musketeers reached semis on a module whose only job was to nudge the
tiebreak metric: "in a game that was dead even, we could win solely off of having like,
1 gold."
WHY IT TRANSFERS — Our tiebreak chain is delivered -> HARVESTERS ALIVE -> stored. Key 1
is an integral over time and is therefore won in the first 300 rounds. KEY 2 IS A
SNAPSHOT AT r1000. A harvester built at r990 delivers ~12 Ti (worthless on key 1) but
counts fully on key 2 -- AND NEEDS NO CONVEYOR CONNECTION. 353 of our 1,230 archived
games reach r1000.
WHAT WOULD KILL IT — Needs enough free reachable ore late, and a builder alive to place
it. Only pays in games that reach the clock AND are tied on key 1.
BUILDER HOOK — At ~r940, if no core kill is plausible, redirect surplus titanium and
idle builders to harvesters on any reachable ore. Measure on games currently reaching
r1000.
```

```
---
tactic: Cap defence at your economy count (an explicit econ:military ratio)
source: https://battlecode.org/assets/files/postmortem-2020-the-high-ground.pdf
origin: Battlecode 2020 / The High Ground (4th place)
evidence: documented
transfers: partial
---
WHAT IT IS — The only hard stated econ:military ratio found in any postmortem swept:
"we kept at least as many vaporators as we had net guns until we stopped building
vaporators", plus a spacing rule ("at least 8 distance squared away from each other").
WHY IT MIGHT TRANSFER — Cheap to encode in the 16-slot store, and the spacing half is
independently useful so one push cannot trade into several turrets.
WHAT WOULD KILL IT — AND IT DOES, IN THIS DIRECTION: we measure as the team that
ALREADY out-builds economy and UNDER-builds turrets (paired: conveyors +13, turrets -3,
we lead turrets in only 20.1% of games). A cap on turrets binds on exactly the wrong
side. Useful only inverted -- as a FLOOR on harvesters, or as the spacing rule alone.
BUILDER HOOK — Take the spacing rule, drop the cap.
```

```
---
tactic: Saturation is set by the transport ceiling, not the node
source: https://docs.screeps.com/api/#Source
origin: Screeps official docs + community remote-mining article
evidence: documented (Screeps math) / inference (our ceiling)
transfers: partial
---
WHAT IT IS — Screeps saturation is pure arithmetic: 10 energy/tick per source,
HARVEST_POWER 2 per WORK part, so exactly 5 WORK parts saturate one source. Haulers are
sized by ROUND-TRIP DISTANCE, not by the source.
WHY IT MIGHT TRANSFER — Our ore never depletes, so node saturation is meaningless, but
TRANSPORT is a ceiling we have not costed. INFERENCE: a harvester emits 0.25 stacks/round;
a conveyor holds at most one stack (get_stored_resource_id is singular) and advances once
per end-of-round distribution -> a trunk carries ~1 stack/round = 10 Ti/round, so ONE
TRUNK SATURATES AT ~4 HARVESTERS. The core's 2x2 has 8 orthogonal perimeter tiles -> ~80
Ti/round absolute ceiling.
WHAT WOULD KILL IT — If a conveyor can accept and emit in the same distribution step, or
stacks can stack, the 1-stack/round figure is wrong. THE SWEEP FLAGS THIS AS ITS MOST
LOAD-BEARING UNVERIFIED ASSUMPTION.
BUILDER HOOK — From the corpus, find the max per-round delivery ever observed. If it
never exceeds 10x(core-adjacent conveyors), the ceiling is confirmed and "never funnel
more than 4 harvesters into one trunk" becomes doctrine.
```

```
---
tactic: Live-count cost scaling makes losses self-correcting
source: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CodinGame/SpringChallenge2021/main/src/main/java/com/codingame/game/Game.java
origin: CodinGame Spring Challenge 2021 (Photosynthesis), referee source
evidence: documented
transfers: yes
---
WHAT IT IS — The closest structural match to our cost rule found anywhere:
getCostFor(size, owner) = BASE[size] + count(owner's LIVE trees of that size). Cost
inflates per category and counts LIVE entities, so removing one immediately cheapens the
next.
WHY IT MIGHT TRANSFER — Our "+5% each... destroying an entity removes its contribution"
is the same construction. Two consequences we may not have priced: (a) when the enemy
kills our harvester our NEXT harvester gets cheaper, so losses are partially
self-correcting -- which weakens the case for expensive defence of any single harvester;
(b) at equal budget, act in the category whose scale you are about to relieve.
WHAT WOULD KILL IT — If scale is driven by cumulative-ever builds instead, the
self-correcting property evaporates.
BUILDER HOOK — One match: log get_harvester_cost() immediately before and after a
friendly harvester is destroyed. If it drops, the live-count reading is confirmed.
```

```
---
tactic: Stop expanding at a fixed fraction of the clock
source: https://mlomb.dev/blog/halite-iii-postmortem
origin: Halite III (2018), mlomb (rank 18) and Stone Tao
evidence: documented
transfers: partial
---
WHAT IT IS — Nobody solved this analytically; every top Halite III bot used a tuned clock
fraction. mlomb: hard "turn >= 0.8 * MAX_TURNS" stop, mode-specific cut-offs 0.70/0.65/
0.55/0.36, plus "stop if average halite per cell < 60". Stone Tao: turn < 0.65*MAX_TURNS.
mlomb states the "magic numbers were extracted analyzing games from top players".
WHY IT MIGHT TRANSFER — Same shape: fixed clock, economy unit with a payback period. The
transferable FORM is "clock fraction + map-richness term, tuned empirically".
WHAT WOULD KILL IT — The direction of transfer is wrong. In Halite III the ship's cost is
SUBTRACTED FROM THE SCORE. Our primary tiebreak is delivered, a separate counter that
spending does not decrement (VERIFIED, see the companion doc). So our stop-expanding round
is much LATER than Halite's 65-80% -- roughly "stop when transit time exceeds rounds
remaining".
BUILDER HOOK — Covered by the tiebreak-2 hook above.
```

```
---
tactic: Payback-period gate on an economic building
source: https://battlecode.org/assets/files/postmortem-2020-the-high-ground.pdf
origin: Battlecode 2020 / The High Ground
evidence: documented
transfers: yes
---
WHAT IT IS — BC2020's Vaporator produced 2 soup/turn for 500 soup. When it was nerfed
from 1000/7 to 500/2, a 4th-place team's entire strategic verdict hung on one division:
it "still paid for itself within 250 rounds". 500/2 = 250 against a ~500-round clock.
WHY IT TRANSFERS — Same shape of building, and OUR NUMBERS ARE THIRTY TIMES BETTER: 20 Ti
for 2.5 Ti/round = 8 rounds. Note also that our +10 passive per 4 rounds is EXACTLY one
harvester's output -- the first harvester doubles total income.
WHAT WOULD KILL IT — If delivered stacks do not credit the global pool, harvesters buy
tiebreak position but not spending power.
BUILDER HOOK — Log get_global_resources() deltas on rounds a stack reaches the core;
confirm +10. Two lines, settles it permanently.
```

```
---
tactic: Harass the economy — kill the stream, not the stock
source: https://battlecode.org/assets/files/postmortem-2023-dont-at-me.pdf
origin: Battlecode 2020 (drone harass, universal) and Battlecode 2023 / Don't @ Me
evidence: anecdotal
transfers: partial
---
WHAT IT IS — BC2020: a drone harass was "essential... no matter what the strategy" and
"effective at limiting the opponent's economic capabilities". BC2023: "Carriers are almost
always grouped around wells, so a single well-aimed destabilizer could swiftly cause
significant disruption."
WHY IT MIGHT TRANSFER — Our economy is MORE exposed: harvesters are immobile 30 HP
buildings on fixed ore tiles, conveyors 20 HP, both on a path inferable from map symmetry.
Under a cumulative tiebreak the leverage looks extreme -- a harvester killed at r300 denies
~1,750 Ti of cumulative delivery.
WHAT WOULD KILL IT — Heal is 4 HP/Ti against our 1.80 HP/Ti best damage: ONE healer beats
ONE attacker outright on a harvester (+4 vs 2 per round). You need three attackers, at
6 Ti/round, three exposed 40 HP bots deep in enemy vision. And this is the offensive arm,
refuted on three instruments.
BUILDER HOOK — DO NOT BUILD IT. Measure first: how often are enemy harvesters actually
HEALED when attacked? If the field does not defend them the leverage stands; if it does,
drop this permanently.
```

```
---
tactic: Shorten the haul rather than lengthen the line (dropoffs / refineries)
source: https://github.com/TheDuck314/halite2018
origin: Halite III (2018) TheDuck314 (6th); Battlecode 2020 refineries
evidence: documented
transfers: no
---
WHAT IT IS — In both leagues the dominant logistics answer was to move the DROP-OFF POINT
to the resource. TheDuck314 targeted "A + B * (# of ships)" dropoffs, scored by halite in a
15x15 square minus a distance penalty.
WHY IT DOES NOT TRANSFER — WE HAVE EXACTLY ONE CORE AND CANNOT BUILD ANOTHER DEPOSIT POINT.
No dropoff, no refinery, no second drain. The bulk of the Halite III logistics literature is
therefore inapplicable. Filed explicitly so the next session does not re-derive it.
WHAT SURVIVES — Only the ranking consequence: with a fixed drain, develop ore strictly in
order of PATH LENGTH to the core, because line length is capex (3 Ti/tile), latency,
attack surface (20 HP/tile), and above all builder-bot labour -- a bot laying a line of
length d spends ~2d rounds, since moving and acting are mutually exclusive.
BUILDER HOOK — Sort candidate ore by BFS distance to core, not Euclidean; never start a
line longer than the rounds remaining.
```

```
---
tactic: Farm your own resource nodes / don't mine a tile to zero
source: https://battlecode.org/assets/files/postmortem-2022-5-musketeers.pdf
origin: Battlecode 2022 / 5 Musketeers
evidence: documented
transfers: no
---
WHAT IT IS — In BC2022 dead units left lead where they died and deposits regenerated, so
teams manufactured lead farms next to their base: "Many teams used this strategy, and some
beat us with it."
WHY IT DOES NOT TRANSFER — Our ore is TERRAIN (Environment.ORE_TITANIUM), not a deposit.
Nothing creates, moves or depletes it; self_destruct() explicitly deals no damage and drops
nothing. Harvesters never deplete their tile either, so "leave 1 behind" has no analogue.
FILED AS A DEFINITE NO so it is not chased again.
BUILDER HOOK — None. Do not look for this.
```

```
---
tactic: Destroy-and-rebuild to convert the abundant currency into the scarce one
source: https://battlecode.org/assets/files/postmortem-2025-om-nom.pdf
origin: Battlecode 2025 / Gone Whalin, adopted by Om Nom
evidence: documented
transfers: partial
---
WHAT IT IS — "Tower flickering": towers spawned with 500 paint, so Gone Whalin destroyed
and rebuilt their own towers "to convert 1000 chips to 500 paint". Om Nom designated "about
a third" of their money towers as farms. Om Nom's general lesson: "it didn't matter if we
built too many Money Towers, as long as we had a way to convert it to paint."
WHY IT MIGHT TRANSFER — We have the primitives: destroy() on an allied adjacent building is
free, no cooldown, unlimited per turn, and removes the scale contribution.
WHAT WOULD KILL IT — Our conversion sink is already free and better: convert_ammo is 1:1
and costs no cooldown, so no flicker trick is needed. And cycling gains nothing since scale
tracks the LIVE count. Rebuilding a damaged harvester (20+ Ti) is also worse than healing it
(7.5 Ti for a full 30 HP).
BUILDER HOOK — None as a mechanic. Its value is as the diagnosis: a top team's answer to
"we have too much money" was to INVENT A SINK, not to build more economy.
```
