# Tactics sweep 5 — wheel topic 7: coordinating many units through a tiny shared channel

**Research arm, session 23, 2026-08-09.** Opus subagent, model wired explicitly.
Sources: 15 Battlecode team postmortems 2019-2026 (all verified live at
`battlecode.org/assets/files/`), the BC2020/2024/2026 specs, Halite III,
AI-Challenge Ants, Screeps docs, and the multi-agent literature.

**Read with `../store-semantics-2026-08-09.md`**, which probes the four assumptions
this sweep flagged. **All four came back measured, and one of them is a live latent
bug in our bot.**

## What the probe settled (so the sweep's caveats can be dropped)

| question | answer |
|---|---|
| write visible same turn? | **No — buffered**, as documented |
| two writers, one slot, one round | **LAST WRITER WINS** |
| read-increment-write ticket idiom under collision | **BROKEN, silently** — 5 writers, counter advanced by 1, all five believed they were unit #0 |
| slot value range | **unsigned 32-bit `[0, 2**32-1]`; negatives RAISE OverflowError** |

**32 bits per slot × 16 slots = 512 bits — wider than Battlecode's own arrays
(10-16 bits/entry across the years surveyed), so every packing scheme below fits.**

## The single most useful source

**Gone Fishin' 2023 (2nd place) publishes its complete bit-level shared-array
schema** — the closest thing to a reference design that exists in this literature.
Its enemy block: *"140 bits: a total of 10 enemies, each containing 12 bits for
location, and 2 bits for the round this enemy is reported."*

## Tactics

```
---
tactic: Round-stamped enemy records (report + expiry in the same field)
source: https://battlecode.org/assets/files/postmortem-2023-gone-fishin.pdf
origin: Battlecode 2023 / Gone Fishin' (2nd place)
evidence: documented
transfers: yes
---
WHAT IT IS — Every threat record carries its own coarse timestamp, so a reader tells
a fresh sighting from a dead one with no extra bookkeeping and no clearing pass.
WHY IT MIGHT TRANSFER — Our writes are visible only next round, so every threat we
read is ALREADY >=1 round old and we have no way to know how much older. Vision is
per-unit (builder r^2=20) with no shared map, so a threat record is the only way a
sighting reaches the core or a distant defender. A stamp packed into the same int
costs zero extra slots. CONFIRMED CHEAP: 32 bits per slot, and a 30x30 position
needs 10.
WHAT WOULD KILL IT — Nothing in our ruleset.
BUILDER HOOK — Repack SLOT_THREAT from bare pack_pos to (round_mod_64 << 10) |
packed_pos; readers ignore records older than N rounds. Merging SLOT_UNDER +
SLOT_ATK_RND by the same move frees a slot outright.
```

```
---
tactic: Deterministic index dictionary — send an index, not a coordinate
source: https://battlecode.org/assets/files/postmortem-2019-smite.pdf
origin: bc19 2019 / smite (tournament winner); same trick in Java's Best Waifu 2020
evidence: documented
transfers: yes
---
WHAT IT IS — smite ran the same BFS clustering in every unit: "Every unit that runs
this BFS gets the exact same array of clusters in the same order, so we can safely
communicate just the index of the cluster of interest" -- 12 bits collapsed to 5.
WHY IT MIGHT TRANSFER — Every one of our units runs the same Player class; the map
is symmetric, fully queryable, and TERRAIN NEVER CHANGES. So an ore enumeration or a
ring of defence posts can be recomputed identically by every unit for free, and the
store carries only a small index. HIGHEST-LEVERAGE COMPRESSION AVAILABLE TO US,
because it converts store bandwidth into CPU -- and our 10 ms budget is PER-UNIT
while the store is team-wide.
WHAT WOULD KILL IT — The 10 ms per-unit budget: the enumeration must be cheap or
cached on the instance (the Player object persists per unit, so compute once on the
first turn). An enumeration depending on MUTABLE state is NOT safe -- different units
see different neighbourhoods and would derive different orderings.
BUILDER HOOK — ORE_INDEX = ore tiles sorted by (d^2 to own core, x, y), computed once
per unit. A harvester-site claim then costs ~5 bits instead of 10.
```

```
---
tactic: Distress slot that steers SPAWN LOCATION, not just unit behaviour
source: https://battlecode.org/assets/files/postmortem-2024-cout-for-clout.pdf
origin: Battlecode 2024 / Cout for Clout
evidence: documented
transfers: yes
---
WHAT IT IS — "A crude distress system was put in, which made ducks mark the closest
spawn to where the flag was being stolen. Jailed ducks would then spawn there."
The channel's primary consumer was THE SPAWNER, not the field units. Their squadron
summon cites Lanchester: "adding one duck increases the power of a large army by a
lot... we try to send ducks to combat locations so they are more close together."
WHY IT MIGHT TRANSFER — Best match in the sweep to our measured situation. Our core
spawns <=1 builder/turn and CHOOSES the tile. A distress record lets it spawn on the
THREATENED side, saving 2-6 rounds of walking for a unit that moves one cardinal tile
per turn and cannot move and act in the same turn. Given the conditional probability
a core kill is ours rises to 76% by r601-999, rounds saved on the defensive response
are worth more than anything the store could say about offence.
WHAT WOULD KILL IT — The core's own vision is r^2=36 and it may already see close
threats itself; the slot only pays when the threat is spotted by a distant builder or
a sentinel (r^2=32) outside core vision. Measure before spending a slot.
BUILDER HOOK — In _core_turn, order the can_spawn candidate tiles by distance to the
reported threat instead of a fixed direction list. A reordering of an existing loop:
no slots, no new code paths.
```

```
---
tactic: Ticket counter — a unit learns its index by incrementing a shared int
source: https://battlecode.org/assets/files/postmortem-2022-5-musketeers.pdf
origin: Battlecode 2022 / 5 Musketeers; independently Gone Fishin' 2023
evidence: documented
transfers: PARTIAL -- AND IT IS A LATENT BUG IN OUR BOT, NOW DEMONSTRATED
---
WHAT IT IS — "When Archon #2 goes, how does it know it's #2?... Counting them
manually was useful for things like this."
WHY IT ONLY PARTLY TRANSFERS — It works in Battlecode because robots take turns in
spawn order WITHIN a round and a write is seen by later robots the SAME round. OURS
ARE BUFFERED. PROBED 2026-08-09: five builders ran the idiom in one round; all five
read 0, all five wrote 1, THE COUNTER ADVANCED BY ONE, and all five believed they
were unit #0 -- with no error of any kind.
WHAT WOULD KILL IT — Our SLOT_ROLE_N (main.py:902) is this idiom. It is safe ONLY
because the core spawns at most one builder per turn, i.e. a game rule is holding up
a bot invariant by luck. A launcher-thrown bot re-initialising, a second spawner, or
turrets taking tickets all break it silently into role collapse.
BUILDER HOOK — Two lines: the CORE owns the counter, incrementing on a successful
spawn_builder(); the newborn reads it on its first turn. Single writer, provably
collision-free, survives any future change to who initialises when.
```

```
---
tactic: Type-tag header bits — one slot multiplexed across message kinds
source: https://battlecode.org/assets/files/postmortem-2021-wololo.pdf
origin: BC2021 Wololo; same idiom in SPAARK 2025, Just Woke Up 2025, Strokes Theorem 2026
evidence: documented
transfers: partial
---
WHAT IT IS — "I used the first few bits of the flag to indicate how the rest of the
bits should have been interpreted." FOUR independent top teams across six years
converged on a 3-bit header.
WHY IT MIGHT TRANSFER — The standard way to make a scarce channel carry rare message
types without permanently reserving a slot each.
WHAT WOULD KILL IT — Under buffered last-writer-wins a multiplexed slot is STRICTLY
WORSE than a dedicated one: two units writing different types in the same round lose
one message entirely and the reader cannot tell. SAFE ONLY ON A SINGLE-WRITER SLOT.
BUILDER HOOK — Collapse SLOT_LAUNCH_ID / SLOT_LAUNCH_RND / SLOT_LAUNCHED_ID into one
launcher-owned tagged int. Launchers are few and the handshake is inherently
one-writer; recovers two slots at no semantic cost.
```

```
---
tactic: Stigmergy — coordinate through the world; keep the store for what the world cannot say
source: https://battlecode.org/assets/files/postmortem-2025-om-nom.pdf
origin: BC2025 Om Nom (3rd); corroborated by Confused 2025 and Strokes Theorem 2026
evidence: documented
transfers: partial
---
WHAT IT IS — Om Nom, 3rd in the world: "We didn't use the actual communications much.
The only thing we used them for is symmetry checking, JUST 3 BITS." Confused, also a
finalist: "By the end of the competition I barely used communication, which was the
case for most top teams."
WHY IT MIGHT TRANSFER — The NEGATIVE rule is the valuable half: anything a unit can
observe locally must NOT occupy a slot. Terrain, ore, emptiness, passability, our own
HP, cost scale, global titanium and ammo all have direct getters and are identical for
every unit that asks.
WHAT WOULD KILL IT — Writing a NEW world marker costs titanium and one of the
builder's single actions (build competes with move), so a marker costs ~a full
builder-turn plus 3 Ti against a store write costing nothing. Markers are also visible
to the enemy; the store is private.
BUILDER HOOK — Audit, not build: delete any slot whose value is derivable from a
getter.
```

```
---
tactic: Anti-crowding by observation, with a timeout to stop oscillation
source: https://battlecode.org/assets/files/postmortem-2023-4-musketeers.pdf
origin: BC2023 4 Musketeers; mechanism corroborated by AI Challenge Ants diffusion bots
evidence: documented
transfers: yes
---
WHAT IT IS — "If a carrier reaches a well and it's crowded, they find another well...
To avoid an oscillation problem where launchers see that an HQ is crowded, step away,
see that it's no longer crowded, and step back, we added timeouts."
WHY IT MIGHT TRANSFER — ZERO-BANDWIDTH task allocation, and we have the sensors
(get_nearby_units, get_tile_builder_bot_id). It matters most for our heal screen: a
damaged building has exactly FOUR orthogonal heal posts, so the fifth healer to arrive
is pure waste -- and healing at 4.00 HP/Ti is the mechanism behind our one measured
advantage.
WHAT WOULD KILL IT — Vision limits (builder r^2=20 sees ~4 tiles) mean crowding is
detected late. And the oscillation failure is real and SILENT: a bot flipping between
two targets every round, with move and act mutually exclusive, does literally nothing
all game.
BUILDER HOOK — Heal-post assignment: defender takes post index (role_n mod 4) around
the target, falls back to any free orthogonal tile, and LATCHES for K rounds before
reconsidering. Zero slots, one instance variable, directly serves r150-r1000 survival.
```

```
---
tactic: Locally accumulated danger fields from sparse reports
source: https://battlecode.org/assets/files/postmortem-2020-java-best-waifu.pdf
origin: Battlecode 2020 / Java's Best Waifu (finalist)
evidence: documented
transfers: partial
---
WHAT IT IS — Each unit kept a private map-sized array; "every time a Net Gun is
reported they would add +1 to all cells at distance 15 or less... A Drone considers
all locations with value > 0 as obstacles." The store carried the sparse fact; the
unit's own memory carried the dense consequence.
WHY IT MIGHT TRANSFER — One reported enemy SENTINEL (r^2=32, and its line ignores
friendly entities -- probed) implies a large no-go region every builder can expand
locally from a single 10-bit coordinate. Our per-unit Player instance persists across
turns, so the field is built once and updated.
WHAT WOULD KILL IT — Explicit RETRACTION does not transfer: it needs a second write
from a unit that revisited the spot, which under a one-round buffer can race the
original. Prefer TTL expiry. And watch the 10 ms budget -- a full 30x30 rebuild every
turn for 40 units is not free.
BUILDER HOOK — On reading a SENTINEL-class threat, mark tiles within r^2=32 as costly
in the builder's own pathing scores while the record is fresh.
```

```
---
tactic: Read-all / modify-local / flush-dirty buffer pool
source: https://battlecode.org/assets/files/postmortem-2023-4-musketeers.pdf
origin: Battlecode 2023 / 4 Musketeers
evidence: documented
transfers: no
---
WHAT IT IS — To beat write amplification they "first read the entire global array to
a local copy... then for each dirty index, flush it to the global array." Cout for
Clout 2024 built a message QUEUE on the same framework.
WHY IT DOES NOT TRANSFER — Both depend on Battlecode's same-round visibility. OURS
ARE BUFFERED (probed). A flush of a whole int read a round ago SILENTLY CLOBBERS every
other unit's write to that slot from the intervening round. A queue with a head
pointer is worse: every unit in a round reads the same head and writes the same
position, so all but one message vanishes.
WHAT SURVIVES — The opposite rule, and it is the governing one: SINGLE-WRITER
DISCIPLINE. Give every slot exactly one owner class, or make its content idempotent
enough that last-writer-wins is harmless. An alarm slot where all writers report
roughly the same fact loses nothing to a dropped duplicate; A COUNTER LOSES
EVERYTHING (probed -- see sweep note above).
BUILDER HOOK — none; the probe already ran.
```

```
---
tactic: Auction / contract-net task allocation
source: https://cse-robotics.engr.tamu.edu/dshell/cs689/papers/dias06market.pdf
origin: multi-agent theory (Smith 1980 Contract Net; Dias/Zlot/Kalra/Stentz survey)
evidence: documented
transfers: no
---
WHAT IT IS — Manager announces, contractors bid, manager awards, contractor accepts,
executes: five message stages per task.
WHY IT DOES NOT TRANSFER — Arithmetic. Five stages at one round of latency each is
five rounds per assignment, and with up to 50 units and 16 ints there is no room for
per-robot bids -- bids are exactly the many-writers-one-slot traffic our buffered
store destroys. Every documented Battlecode team solved allocation with counters,
indices and claim flags instead.
WHAT SURVIVES — Deterministic assignment from a shared snapshot costs ZERO messages:
all units see the SAME snapshot for a whole round (our buffering guarantees it), so
identical code on identical inputs reaches identical conclusions about who does what.
BUILDER HOOK — none. Recorded so it is not re-proposed.
```

```
---
tactic: Comms jamming and spoofing (adversarial channel)
source: https://battlecode.org/assets/files/postmortem-2020-the-high-ground.pdf
origin: Battlecode 2020 (blockchain year) / The High Ground, Prasici
evidence: documented
transfers: no
---
WHAT IT IS — BC2020's channel was a PUBLIC auction, unlabelled by sender or team.
"Prasici successfully tricked Steam Locomotive's communications by broadcasting the
first message their seeding bot had sent. This hilariously caused Steam Locomotive's
landscapers to run towards the edge of the map."
WHY IT DOES NOT TRANSFER — Our store is PRIVATE PER TEAM with no read path for the
opponent, so there is no spoofing surface and no reason to spend a bit on
obfuscation. Worth stating because "play the players" makes channel attacks a natural
thing to reach for, and the door is closed.
WHAT SURVIVES — That channel also had ONE-ROUND LATENCY by construction and top teams
still coordinated a rush response through it ("we had our HQ signal when rushed").
One round of delay is not a blocker for defensive alarms; only for tight tactical
micro.
BUILDER HOOK — none.
```

## The sweep's proposed budget, and the one thing to weigh against it

The sweep proposes spending **11 slots and leaving 5 empty**, on the grounds that
**every top team surveyed under-used its array — Om Nom took 3rd in the world on 3
bits** — and that being at 16/16 is a smell rather than an achievement. Its
governing rule is the probed one: **every slot must have exactly one writer class,
or contents for which last-writer-wins is harmless.**

Concrete moves it identifies, each freeing capacity at no semantic cost:
- merge `SLOT_UNDER` + `SLOT_ATK_RND` into one stamped alarm int (**−1 slot**);
- collapse the three launcher-handshake slots into one launcher-owned tagged int
  (**−2 slots**);
- move `SLOT_DEFEND_BEAT` and `SLOT_DROPPED` onto the unit's own persistent
  instance — *a slot only one unit ever reads is not communication*;
- add a **defence rally / spawn-bias** slot, which is the one genuinely new item and
  the one aimed at the r150-r1000 survival target.

**Research caveat on the budget, stated because the sweep does not:** this is a
*design proposal*, not a measured improvement. None of it has been shown to win
anything, and slot pressure is not currently a named problem in our tape. The two
items I would separate out as standing on their own are (a) **the ticket fix**,
because that is a demonstrated latent bug rather than a refactor, and (b) **the
spawn-bias reordering**, because it costs no slots at all.
