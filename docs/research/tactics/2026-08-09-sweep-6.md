# Tactics sweep 6 — wheel topic 9: opening theory and build-order steering

**Research arm, session 23, 2026-08-09.** Opus subagent, model wired explicitly.
Sources: Battlecode postmortems 2019-2026, Terminal, Halite II/III, Lux, CodinGame,
plus two properly controlled academic results (Tavares AIIDE 2016; Churchill & Buro
IEEE CoG 2019).

**Posed because our opening is measurably a CONSTANT** — r0-150 build medians are
identical in wins and losses (`../middle-game-hazard-and-economy-2026-08-09.md` §3).
The brief explicitly allowed *"the opening is fine, don't touch it"* as an outcome,
so the sweep was not free to manufacture a change.

---

## IS OUR CONSTANT DEFENSIBLE? — yes, with one qualification

**The literature does not condemn fixed openings. It condemns fixed openings under
ADAPTIVE opponents.** The strongest anti-constant result — Tavares et al., AIIDE
2016, 1000 matches per pairing, ANOVA + Tukey HSD — puts *"single choice"* dead last
at **28.1%** against *"reply-last"* at **60.9%**, and single-choice was configured to
play the pool's **strongest** pure strategy. But **every meta-strategy that beat it
requires cross-game memory of a named opponent**, which Battlecode's engine forbids
outright. Consequently **no Battlecode postmortem in seven years describes an
opponent-indexed opening book** — not because it is a bad idea, but because it is
illegal.

**The pro-constant evidence comes from winners.** A fully static algo won a $2000
Terminal challenge with 4 of the top 8 in its family. The **Halite III winner
deliberately removed degrees of freedom** — *"spawning adds some variance… before
implementing this system, I was winning and losing some games because of spawning
decisions, and eliminating that helped my winrate overall."* Five straight Battlecode
years show teams hard-coding their first N units, and **Gone Fishin' (2nd) measured
2/3 win rate from spawn ORDERING alone**, with no strategic change.

**THE QUALIFICATION, and it is the one our own data independently confirms: an
opening unconditional on MAP GEOMETRY is a documented failure mode, at the level
just above us.** Malott Fat Cats held #1 for half of BC2021 and finished 4th — they
knew the eco/offence sweet spot *"was heavily map dependent (economy being better
the larger the map)"*, fixed one point, and lost the Finals when the map
distribution shifted. **"food" (2026) lost the small-map tail to a single +4 constant
validated in aggregate**, diagnosed only after elimination.

> **Our own s23 measurement is that failure mode, already visible.** Win rate by core
> separation, n=2,525 games: **narrow d²≤81 64.2% → mid 53.6% → wide 47.5% → hive
> 33.6%.** And our live turret count is **flat across map width (1.55/1.90/1.64/1.14
> at r150) while the field's climbs (2.16/2.80/3.57)**. We do not condition on
> geometry; the field effectively does. See
> `../home-turret-production-gate-2026-08-09.md`.
>
> Note the two winners who *did* condition used almost no machinery: Just Woke Up
> (BC2025, 1st) computed **one threshold at round 0** from map size; Baby Ducks
> (BC2021, 1st) read **one live counter**.

**BUT — and our data settles this, not the literature — the opening is not where we
are losing.** We win the opening exchange (29% of r0-150 core kills are ours; the
field does not rush). Our collapse is r300-999, where the core-kill share against us
runs 55% → 72% → 76%. Gone Fishin' quantified the general case: *"A slightly improved
macro strategy might increase our win rate by 5%, but micro can do a 30%-50%
increase."* **So the highest-value reading of this sweep is that opening work is the
wrong queue item, and the map-conditional finding should be filed with a tripwire
rather than built now.**

## Tactics worth carrying

```
---
tactic: Score build orders by the INTEGRAL of your strength curve, not its terminal value
source: https://ieee-cog.org/2019/papers/paper_85.pdf
origin: Churchill & Buro, IEEE CoG 2019
evidence: documented
transfers: yes
---
WHAT IT IS — Optimising army value at a fixed horizon T is formally exploitable:
"for each fixed time T there may be an earlier time for the opponent to optimize AV
for, attack, and win." BOSS-IM integrates strength over time instead. Measured
against the 2017 AIIDE top-10 over 50 round-robin rounds: 27.66% -> 37.19%,
+9.53 points.
WHY IT MIGHT TRANSFER — THE ONLY RESULT IN THIS SWEEP WITH A MECHANISM, A FORMAL
ARGUMENT AND A MEASURED DELTA. It also supplies an objective function we lack: today
an opening change is judged by a snapshot (units at r150) or by pooled Elo.
Integrating titanium + HP on board + ammo over r0-400 is computable straight from
the corpus with NO bot changes.
WHAT WOULD KILL IT — Our failure profile is not the classic exploitability signature:
we WIN the opening, so nobody is punishing us with an earlier timing. What transfers
is the SCORING RULE -- and our late collapse is consistent with a strength curve that
sags through the middle, which an integral penalises and a terminal objective does not.
BUILDER HOOK — Compute the strength integral over r0-400 for our games and the top
teams', and plot. If ours sits below theirs specifically in r150-350, that localises
the deficit better than any build-count table.
```

```
---
tactic: Hard-code the first N units — and the ORDERING inside it is underexploited
source: https://battlecode.org/assets/files/postmortem-2023-gone-fishin.pdf
origin: BC2021 3 Musketeers; BC2023 Gone Fishin' (2nd); CodinGame Agade, robostac, pb4
evidence: documented
transfers: yes
---
WHAT IT IS — 3 Musketeers: "a lot of teams were hardcoding their initial moves for
the first ~30 robots, so we decided to do that too... hardcoding our initial strategy
greatly improved our bot's performance." Gone Fishin' measured the ORDERING alone:
spawning the first four launchers in a specific outward order "wins about 2/3 of the
game when we play against a version of our bots without it."
WHY IT MIGHT TRANSFER — It validates the EXISTENCE of our constant across five BC
years and two other leagues. Our core spawns <=1 builder/turn onto a 12-tile ring, so
the first ~10 spawns are a genuinely scripted sequence with heavy downstream leverage.
2/3 from ordering is free win rate with no strategic change at all.
WHAT WOULD KILL IT — Nothing. This is the strongest evidence FOR our constant and the
reason "fixed opening = failure mode" is the wrong framing.
BUILDER HOOK — CHEAPEST EXPERIMENT IN THE SWEEP: same 6 builders, same 36 conveyors,
different first-4 spawn TILES (toward enemy / toward ore / toward centre). Documented
2/3 precedent.
```

```
---
tactic: Gate the greedy/safe dial on map geometry, computed once at round 0
source: https://battlecode.org/assets/files/postmortem-2025-just-woke-up.pdf
origin: BC2025 Just Woke Up (1st); Halite III teccles/TheDuck314/reCurse; BC2021 Baby Ducks (1st)
evidence: documented
transfers: yes
---
WHAT IT IS — Just Woke Up computed ONE threshold at round 0: "on small maps, we want
to prioritize towers, since wasting time on SRPs often means we'll get rushed... but
on big maps the benefits of the SRPs will have more time to compound." Halite III made
per-map-size constant tables standard. Baby Ducks read a live signal instead.
WHY IT MIGHT TRANSFER — Our conveyor pipeline is the compounding-economy analogue (36
of them in r0-150, on EVERY map, at every core separation), and the stated reason --
compounding pays only when the map buys time -- is exactly the axis our maps span 20x
on. OUR OWN DATA ALREADY SHOWS THE UNCONDITIONED VERSION FAILING (win rate 64% narrow
-> 34% hive).
WHAT WOULD KILL IT — If core separation did not predict time-to-contact. It should
predict it BETTER than in their games: builder bots are our only mobile unit, move one
cardinal tile per turn, and cannot act on a turn they move, so travel time is close to
deterministic.
BUILDER HOOK — Split the corpus by d2 tercile and recompute the r0-150 build table per
tercile. Measured before any bot code is written.
```

```
---
tactic: Copy the leader's opening straight off the replays
source: https://battlecode.org/assets/files/postmortem-2020-the-high-ground.pdf
origin: BC2020 The High Ground (4th); xSquare's guide; Halite III mlomb
evidence: documented
transfers: yes
---
WHAT IT IS — "we decided to shamelessly copy Java's strategy... we pulled up some
replays of Java's games and worked on a new bot for several days until we imitated it
as closely as possible", and in their lessons: "The most tried and true strategy in
Battlecode is to copy whatever better teams are doing... in all our years we have
[invented an original winning strategy] exactly 0 times." xSquare's guide: "All top
teams in Battlecode copy from one another and, if they tell you they're not, they're
lying!"
WHY IT MIGHT TRANSFER — WE ALREADY HOLD THE INSTRUMENT: ~4,300 decoded replays plus
league-wide match lists. The same r0-150 extraction that produced our own table runs
over the top teams' games. We already know the paired delta: ~13 more conveyors and
~3 fewer turrets than the field.
WHAT WOULD KILL IT — Field medians are pooled across map classes, so a naive copy
imports their averaging problem too. Split by d2 tercile or inherit the failure above.
BUILDER HOOK — Run the r0-150 extraction over the top-10 teams, split by d2 tercile,
diff against ours. Needs no bot changes.
```

```
---
tactic: Isolate the opening in its own code path, exit on enemy state, and ALWAYS ship a return timer
source: http://web.archive.org/web/20250912062821/https://recursive.cc/blog/halite-ii-post-mortem.html
origin: reCurse (Halite II winner); BC2020 Java Best Waifu (1st) and The High Ground; BC2019 smite (1st)
evidence: documented
transfers: yes
---
WHAT IT IS — reCurse isolated the early game "in its own code path" with explicit
exit conditions. Four independent sources say the trigger should read ENEMY STATE, not
the round counter. And The High Ground shipped the antidote to a trigger that gets
stuck: "return to normal build order at round 400 whether we are being rushed or not.
While hacky, this solution allowed us to win some games against them."
WHY IT MIGHT TRANSFER — A deviation branch WITHOUT a return timer is a hostage: an
opponent parking cheap pressure near us holds us in defensive mode and wins on the
r1000 tiebreakers. Given we already lose the middle and late game (55% -> 72% -> 76%
core-kill share against us), a mode we can be LOCKED INTO is a direct route to the
failure we already have.
WHAT WOULD KILL IT — smite's "at worst almost no loss" scouting argument does NOT hold
for us: a scouting builder cannot act on a turn it moves and cannot attack enemy
builder bots, so it gives zero suppression on arrival. On our largest maps the walk
costs ~26 rounds.
BUILDER HOOK — Before building any of it, measure whether an enemy build seen before
r100 predicts the winner at all. And whenever a deviation mode ships, ITS EXPIRY ROUND
SHIPS IN THE SAME COMMIT.
```

```
---
tactic: Opponent-class detection and mid-game opening switch
source: https://github.com/davechurchill/ualbertabot/wiki/Artificial-Intelligence
origin: Terminal (KauffK, zigzagninja); UAlbertaBot; AIIDE 2017 Iron
evidence: documented
transfers: no
---
WHAT IT IS — It works where it is legal. Terminal's zigzagninja: "I now make a maze
(adaptive) to counter mazes... but it was very weak to ping rushes, so I made a system
that detects ping cannons and then it goes back to my other strategy."
WHY IT DOES NOT TRANSFER — Battlecode's engine forbids the cross-match persistence
every one of these methods needs, and does not expose opponent identity. Absent that,
the entire 60.9% "reply-last" column is unavailable and only within-game class
detection remains. The false-positive cost is also brutal: one Terminal player reports
a low-level algo tripping his ping-cannon detector and "completely destroy[ing] me in
5 turns."
BUILDER HOOK — ONE THING ONLY: run a cross-match persistence probe (module-level
counter + a file in the bot zip's directory, read back across two consecutive
matches). If negative, CLOSE THIS LINE PERMANENTLY and stop spending sweep budget on
it.
```

```
---
tactic: Validate against the field with a confidence interval — and know your resolution limit
source: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Agade09/Agade-Ghost-in-the-Cell-Postmortem/master/Agade_GitC_Postmortem.md
origin: BC2026 3MiceWalkIntoABar; BC2025 Just Woke Up (1st); Agade (CodinGame GitC winner)
evidence: documented
transfers: yes
---
WHAT IT IS — 3MiceWalkIntoABar gated merges on "a 95% confidence interval on the win
rates", at 300-500 games per test, because "there was often enough local variation to
make a bad change look as if it improved the bot." Agade, a winner: "Trying to guess
that an AI is slightly better by looking at a few games in the IDE and/or doing some
submits, which only have ~100 games, is an attack on my sanity." Two named
variance-reduction techniques: reCurse's PAIRED MIRRORED GAMES ("it runs the same game
twice, replacing the first player") and xSquare's targeted map design.
WHY IT MIGHT TRANSFER — This is our standing "benchmark vs field, not self" rule,
independently derived by six teams across four leagues, now with SAMPLE SIZES
attached: distinguishing a 55% opening from 50% needs roughly 500-660 games at 80-90%
power. Anything cheaper is not a measurement.
WHAT WOULD KILL IT — Only our throughput. If we cannot run hundreds of games per
variant, the honest move is to prefer changes large enough to show in tens of games
and treat everything smaller as UNMEASURABLE rather than measured.
BUILDER HOOK — Put the number on the arena harness: games/hour, therefore the smallest
effect resolvable in one session. That number decides which opening experiments are
worth attempting at all.
```

## Honest limits (the sweep's own)

Every Battlecode/Terminal/Halite citation is **retrospective self-diagnosis by a
strong competitor, not a controlled experiment**. Only Tavares and Churchill & Buro
are properly controlled, and both are StarCraft. **No source in any league reported a
significance test for accepting an opening change** — paired-seed mirrored games and
95% CIs on a few hundred games are the ceiling of rigour anyone reached. The
mechanisms are documented; the magnitudes, for our ruleset, are not.

**Two corrections the sweep flagged against commonly-repeated claims:** UAlbertaBot
does *not* ship UCB1-over-strategies per race by default (that survey sentence
describes CherryPi; UAlbertaBot disables it). And **AlphaStar did not play a sampled
human build order** — the build-order statistic `z` conditioned training only, and
Battle.net evaluation used *"the unconditional policy"*.
