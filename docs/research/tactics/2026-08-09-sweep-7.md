## 0. THE SWEEP'S LEADING HYPOTHESIS, AND WHY I AM NOT ADOPTING IT

The sweep's best explanation for the field's rising turret survival was **avoidance**:
*"the counter to a static defence is to not fight it, so turret survival is an
avoidance rate, and avoidance gets cheaper as the map gets bigger."* It is well
sourced — The Kragle (BC2025) states the doctrine, Java Best Waifu (BC2020
**champions**) implemented a literal per-drone danger array marking every known net
gun's footprint impassable, and Screeps' own docs call walls *"a delay … than as
impenetrable defenses."*

**It makes a sharp prediction, and the sweep named the test itself: if the field's
wide-map turrets survive because nobody fights them, their firing rate should
collapse with width.** Adapted to what the corpus can answer (shots per turret
built, since per-turret firing is not decoded):

| band | US shots/turret | **THEM shots/turret** | THEM survival |
|---|---|---|---|
| narrow ≤81 | 26.5 | **26.7** | 40.8% |
| mid 128-144 | 21.9 | 24.4 | 49.0% |
| wide 288-392 | 19.1 | **22.5** | **63.2%** |
| hive 650 | 22.1 | 23.7 | 57.3% |

**The field's survival rises 40.8% → 63.2% (+22pp) while its firing rate falls only
26.7 → 22.5 (−16%).** Their turrets are still fighting and still surviving.
**Avoidance is not the explanation, or not the main one.**

*Limitation, stated: the sweep asked for the **never-fired fraction**, and a flat
mean can hide a skewed distribution. Per-turret firing is not in the corpus, so this
is the closest available test and it weakens the hypothesis rather than killing it.*

## 1. What the sweep got right, and it is the buildable part

```
---
tactic: Ablative screen — a cheap wall in front of the turret (SENTINEL ONLY)
source: https://github.com/correlation-one/C1GamesStarterKit/blob/master/python-algo/algo_strategy.py
origin: Terminal (Correlation One), official starter algo `build_defences`
evidence: documented
transfers: partial -- and the arithmetic here is extreme
---
WHAT IT IS — Verbatim from the official starter: "Place walls in front of turrets to
soak up damage for them."
WHY IT MIGHT TRANSFER — Barrier 30 HP for 3 Ti = 10 HP/Ti. Gunner 25 HP for 20 Ti =
1.25. Sentinel 40 HP for 30 Ti = 1.33. A BARRIER IS ~8x MORE HIT POINTS PER TITANIUM
THAN THE TURRET IT SCREENS. An attacker chewing a 30 HP barrier at 2 dmg for 2 Ti
spends 30 Ti and 15 builder-turns to remove a 3 Ti object -- 10:1 before healing.
Add ONE healer (+4 HP/round vs 2 dmg/round): A BARRIER WITH ONE HEALER IS LITERALLY
UNBREAKABLE BY ONE BUILDER BOT, and two attackers only stalemate it.
WHAT WOULD KILL IT — IT BLINDS A GUNNER. Our gunner's line is blocked by any
building including our own (probe-verified), so a screen turns a 20 Ti gunner into a
20 Ti barrier. It does NOT blind a sentinel (probe-verified: 18 damage landed
through a friendly bot AND a friendly barrier). SENTINEL-ONLY -- and this is the
concrete answer to "choose turret type by how built-up the defended area is."
BUILDER HOOK — one sentinel facing a lane, a barrier on the tile directly in front,
and a heal-priority rule ranking the barrier ABOVE the sentinel.
```

```
---
tactic: Site defences inside the REPAIR envelope, not by field of fire
source: https://docs.screeps.com/defense.html
origin: Screeps official defence docs; corroborated at wiki.screepspl.us/Combat/
evidence: documented
transfers: yes
---
WHAT IT IS — "Always place towers as close to their potential targets as possible",
and "develop your defense system in such a way that any attacked square adjoins a
creep defender covered by a rampart."
WHY IT MIGHT TRANSFER — Sharper for us than for Screeps. Our heal needs a builder on
an ORTHOGONALLY ADJACENT tile. A turret no builder ever visits is unhealable -- and
unhealed, a 25 HP gunner is 13 enemy builder-turns from death. Healed, the same
gunner cannot be killed by fewer than three attackers. HEALER REACHABILITY IS WORTH
MORE THAN FIELD OF FIRE, because it changes the outcome from "dies in 13 turns" to
"cannot be killed."
WHAT WOULD KILL IT — The 4-healer adjacency cap (~16 HP/round) means a big enough
push still wins; and a healer standing beside a GUNNER may be standing on its firing
line and blinding it.
BUILDER HOOK — before siting, require an orthogonally adjacent tile that is passable,
NOT on the turret's facing axis, and near a tile our builders already traverse. Then
measure: WHAT FRACTION OF OUR TURRETS EVER RECEIVED A HEAL? If near zero,
siting-by-fire-arc is the whole problem.
```

```
---
tactic: Approach off the firing axis — what opponents should be doing to our gunners
source: https://battlecode.org/assets/files/postmortem-2025-just-woke-up.pdf
origin: Battlecode 2025, Just Woke Up (winners)
evidence: documented
transfers: partial
---
WHAT IT IS — JWU kite towers: "if you are out of range, you enter range and attack
them. If you are in range, you attack them and then move out of range."
WHAT WOULD KILL IT — LITERAL KITING IS IMPOSSIBLE HERE: our builders cannot move and
act in the same round.
WHY THE PRINCIPLE STILL TRANSFERS — A gunner fires along ONE straight line. A builder
standing orthogonally adjacent but OFF the facing axis takes zero damage while
attacking at 2 dmg/2 Ti: a 25 HP gunner falls in 13 turns for 26 Ti and can never
answer. Rotating costs 10 Ti + a cooldown; a sentinel cannot rotate at all. THIS IS
WHAT WE SHOULD EXPECT OPPONENTS TO DO TO OUR GUNNERS, AND IT IS FREE FOR THEM.
BUILDER HOOK — pair every gunner with something covering its blind quadrants (a
launcher is the natural partner: r^2=26 and facing-independent), or stop building
gunners where the approach axis is unknown.
```

```
---
tactic: Turret spacing floor (d^2 >= 8) -- AND WHY IT SHOULD PROBABLY BE INVERTED HERE
source: https://battlecode.org/assets/files/postmortem-2020-the-high-ground.pdf
origin: Battlecode 2020, The High Ground (4th)
evidence: documented
transfers: partial -- inverted
---
WHAT IT IS — "we would usually build net guns at least 8 distance squared away from
each other."
WHY IT DOES NOT TRANSFER AS WRITTEN — OUR GAME HAS NO SPLASH, NO AoE, NO MULTI-TARGET
ATTACK ANYWHERE. Builder attack is 2 damage on exactly one orthogonally adjacent
tile. The universal reason to spread static defence -- don't lose two to one attack
-- DOES NOT EXIST HERE.
WHY THE INVERSE MAY BE RIGHT — A single builder parked between two diagonally
adjacent turrets (d^2=2) heals either on alternate turns without moving, and a
sentinel's line passes through friendly entities, so sentinels can be stacked in
file.
BUILDER HOOK — from builds.tsv, compute nearest-neighbour d^2 between same-game
turret builds, ours vs field, by band. If the field clusters and we scatter, that is
a free lever.
```

```
---
tactic: Ship static defence on the FIELD verdict even when self-play says no
source: https://battlecode.org/assets/files/postmortem-2025-just-woke-up.pdf
origin: Battlecode 2025, Just Woke Up (winners); confirmed from the other side by confused (2nd)
evidence: documented
transfers: yes
---
WHAT IT IS — "When we performed AB testing against our past bots, using defense
towers did not seem to be very good... But our bot wasn't super aggressive, and we
believed that this SHOULD be better against the teams that we have the worst
matchups against. So we sent it, and the results were unbelievable."
WHY IT MIGHT TRANSFER — An external, documented instance of our own standing
directive (benchmark vs field, not self). Static defence is the archetypal change
SELF-PLAY CANNOT SCORE, because its value depends entirely on opponent aggression --
the one thing held constant in a self-leg.
WHAT WOULD KILL IT — Procedural risk: it licenses shipping on a hunch. Note JWU had
a NAMED hypothesis and a named field to test it on, not a general licence.
BUILDER HOOK — none in code. Procedural: any turret-siting change gets a
class-weighted vs-field battery; the self-leg is attribution only, never a gate.
```

```
---
tactic: Demolish static defence that has not seen an enemy
source: https://battlecode.org/assets/files/postmortem-2025-spaark.pdf
origin: Battlecode 2025, SPAARK
evidence: documented
transfers: partial -- AND IT IS A WARNING LABEL ON OUR OWN MEASUREMENT
---
WHAT IT IS — "if we haven't seen an enemy robot or paint in the last 30 turns, then
we can probably disintegrate."
WHY IT MIGHT TRANSFER — Our destroy() is free, cooldown-free, unlimited, and removes
the entity's +20% cost-scale contribution -- cheaper for us than for SPAARK, who paid
full price to rebuild.
THE WARNING — If our bot (or the field's) demolishes turrets, "fraction alive at
r150" IS NOT A SURVIVAL RATE. Our decoder's removeEntity does not distinguish
owner-demolition from an enemy kill. THIS IS A LIVE CAVEAT ON EVERY SURVIVAL NUMBER
IN THE s23 DOCS, including my own.
BUILDER HOOK — split death attribution: was the removeEntity preceded by an enemy
builderAttack (unum 13, already decoded with attacker and target) or enemy turret
damage on that entity id? If neither, it was our own demolition.
```

## 2. The sweep's own ranked candidates for our flat curve

Offered honestly as unconfirmed, and the first is the one it would bet on:

1. **Our siting is indexed to our ECONOMY; the field's to its CORE.** We build 59
   conveyors/game to the field's 34. A conveyor *line* lengthens with core
   separation; a *ring* around a core does not. **This is the only candidate that
   predicts a flat curve for us against a rising one for them.**
2. **Some of our "deaths" are our own `destroy()`** — see the warning label above.
3. **Denominator fragility** — 4.5 turrets/game, median 2 live; a ±3pp band across
   four cells may be an absence of signal rather than a flat line.
4. *Rejected:* thinner line per tile on a big map — predicts the field's survival
   should fall too. It doesn't.
5. *Rejected:* the field fights us better on wide maps — predicts our survival should
   fall. It's flat, and it says nothing about their rise.

## 3. The closing observation, which I think is the sweep's best paragraph

> *10.7 turrets built per game, ~24 shots each, 63.2% still standing at r150 on wide
> maps is **also** the signature of a field that over-invests in idle static defence
> on big maps — precisely the weakness The Kragle names. Our wide-map problem is a
> **win rate of 47.5% wide and 33.6% hive**, and the closest documented match to that
> curve is wololo's: a rush-shaped bot meeting a fortify-shaped field, fixed by a
> map-size mode switch. **The turret-survival gap is more likely a symptom of that
> than its cause, and "catch up to the field's turret survival" may be the wrong goal
> entirely.***

wololo (BC2021) is the match: *"monky began to fortify… to the point that they could
defeat my rush every time on medium to large size maps"*, fixed by an explicit dual
mode — *"overwhelm the opponent with my rush on small maps, and yet turtle harder
than my opponent on larger maps."* **Our curve is his curve.**
