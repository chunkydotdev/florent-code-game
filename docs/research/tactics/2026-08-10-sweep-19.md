## 1. (A) BUILD-TIME INVARIANTS — answered, four ways, and they are not the same idea

| pattern | who | shape |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **dry-run the whole route, then commit** | AdmiralAI (OpenTTD) | `AITestMode()` + `TestBuildPath` before `BuildPath` |
| **incomplete plan commits NOTHING** | Overmind (Screeps) | `if (ret.incomplete) { return; }`, enforced by the data flow, not the check |
| **build, then RE-WALK and patch** | NoNoCAB (OpenTTD) | `CheckPath` re-asserts adjacency tile by tile |
| **delete hand-routing entirely** | Mindustry, Factorio Learning Environment | name the endpoints, a pathfinder lays the line |

**The one that fits us best is the third, and the reason is our store.** AdmiralAI's
transaction is one function call; ours would be tens of rounds long in a unit whose
only shared memory is 16 buffered unsigned ints. **NoNoCAB's re-walk needs no
persistent plan at all** — and its stated reason for existing is our situation
exactly: the API has no global reachability query, only adjacency
(*"'tile_from' and 'tile_to' are directly neighbouring tiles."*). **Our `Controller`
has the identical gap: `get_direction` and `get_tile_building_id` and nothing that
answers "does this reach the core".**

**The strongest single transferable item in the sweep is not a check, it is a
scalar.** Overmind keeps `roadCoverage = roadCount / pathLength` as a persisted
number and a **completely different subsystem branches on it** — hauler bodies
switch at `ROAD_COVERAGE_THRESHOLD = 0.75`. **It compresses to one small
non-negative integer, which is the one thing our 16-slot store is safe for**, and we
currently have no way to express "the network is in bad shape" at all.
[`route-completeness-as-a-live-scalar`](route-completeness-as-a-live-scalar.md)

**And the Screeps community has a NAME for what we look like.** *"massive road
networks that are never used, or as seen from a user's view that look like airport
runways"* — the named cause is placing infrastructure opportunistically where a unit
happens to walk. **We run 116 conveyors to Viktor5776's 34 and collect less
titanium.**
[`laying-road-where-a-unit-happens-to-walk-is-a-named-trap`](laying-road-where-a-unit-happens-to-walk-is-a-named-trap.md)

**One clean negative inside (A), with an inversion:** **nobody rolls back a partial
route.** Seven OpenTTD AIs, zero matches for rollback vocabulary; abandoned road is
left on the map. **That is safe in their game and expensive in ours** — a leftover
conveyor is a cork at ~1:78 against its visible cost — **and our engine has the
primitive they lacked**: `destroy()` is free, uses no cooldown, is unlimited per
turn, and per the organisers' doc *"returns any resources currently in transit on
that tile to your team's balance."*
[`nobody-rolls-back-a-partial-route-and-we-can`](nobody-rolls-back-a-partial-route-and-we-can.md)

## 2. (C) DETECTION — the answer, in eleven lines, and its three challengers

**THE ANSWER: CluelessPlus never asks whether its road is connected. It asks whether
the route is still earning.**

> *"mean_income < 30 ||"*
> *"(long_time_mean_income > 0 && income < (long_time_mean_income / 2)) ||"*

against an EMA maintained on the same route, with a three-month rate limiter and a
deliberate **damping of the baseline after a successful repair** so the alarm cannot
re-fire against a memory of the healthy past. **Corroborated from the opposite
direction by the Factorio wiki, which states that inspecting the line is not a
test:** *"This is especially deceiving when the item isn't moving and all belts have
filled up as these belts can't carry the amount they lead one to believe they can."*
**A full belt and a working belt look identical.**
[`detect-the-break-at-the-till-not-on-the-line`](detect-the-break-at-the-till-not-on-the-line.md)

**Three other detectors exist and each is cheaper or sharper on one axis:**

1. **The stuck counter — four implementations across two leagues, all eight lines.**
   Compare state to last tick, increment on identical, **reset on any change**, latch
   after N. Flatland's round-1 winner keys on `(position, direction)` and latches at
   `>100`; Screeps' Traveler and Overmind both use `DEFAULT_STUCK_VALUE = 2` with a
   **randomised** repath. **Our observable is `get_stored_resource_id` and the
   reset-on-change rule is exactly what discriminates a cork from real saturation.**
   [`the-stuck-counter-is-the-universal-primitive`](the-stuck-counter-is-the-universal-primitive.md)
2. **The precondition timeout, and it is the only one with published numbers.**
   RCLL: *"Execution monitoring also aborted 422 actions that were stuck, because
   preconditions were not met until a timeout threshold was reached. 83 % of those
   stuck actions concern machine assembly steps"* — **and the distribution of the
   aborts localised the fault**, which is the only route in this sweep from "something
   is wrong" to "this is what is wrong" that a bot could run itself.
   [`retry-in-place-worked-22-percent-of-the-time`](retry-in-place-worked-22-percent-of-the-time.md)
3. **Model-based monitoring — predict the effect, compare to the observation.** The
   robotics literature's dominant method, and the shape our ruleset makes trivial
   because the rules are arithmetic.
   [`detect-the-break-by-predicting-the-effect`](detect-the-break-by-predicting-the-effect.md)

**⚠ AND THE FINDING THAT SHOULD SET EXPECTATIONS, because it is three independent
codebases doing the same thing.** In Overmind, in ChooChoo, and in the Factorio
Learning Environment, **the exact check we want was written, priced, and commented
out.** Overmind's is still in the file: *"// // verify creep is in the location it
thinks it should be in"* under a live *"// TODO: repath if you are not on expected
next position"*. ChooChoo's author disabled a self-crossing check and wrote down why:
*"Is it bad if it happens? Not sure. For ChooChoo, worst case the route might jam and
we sell the trains. I'd rather have the (very significant!) speed boost to
pathfinding."* **Nobody failed to think of these checks. Three people measured them
and removed them.**
[`the-strongest-detector-was-written-and-then-disabled`](the-strongest-detector-was-written-and-then-disabled.md)

**⇒ The single cheapest useful action from this whole sweep: run the terminus walk
inside a builder's turn, do nothing with the result, and log `get_cpu_time_elapsed()`
against the 10,000 µs budget.** That one line settles the objection three authors
raised, before any plank is built.

**And the honest counterweight, from the winners of the league whose entire problem
is deadlock:** *"Deadlocks are essentially impossible to reason about locally."*
*"The only way to prevent deadlocks is to reason globally about the trains."* Their
claim is about **prevention**, not detection — but anyone proposing a local rule that
claims to *prevent* head-to-head conveyors should read it first.

## 3. (B) REPAIR VS REBUILD — measured twice, and both measurements come with a catch

**Somebody did the arithmetic.** A Screeps community author priced decay repair
against rebuild: *"Rebuilding is always more expensive than repair."* — 0.5 energy
per tick against 1. **⚠ It is an UNMERGED pull request to the official docs repo,
open since 2017-08-23 and never accepted** (verified via the API). And the community's
own constants table shows the answer is a table, not a number: **plain road 0.001
e/tick, tunnel road 0.15 — 150x.** *Policy per structure class, not global.*
[`repair-beats-rebuild-and-somebody-did-the-arithmetic`](repair-beats-rebuild-and-somebody-did-the-arithmetic.md)

**The cheapest implementation in the sweep is CluelessPlus's: repair and reroute are
the SAME pathfinder call with one constant changed** —
`pathfinder.cost.no_existing_road = repair_existing? 300 : 60; // default = 40` —
run as a two-rung ladder, repair mode first, then the penalty dropped.
[`repair-versus-reroute-is-one-pathfinder-constant`](repair-versus-reroute-is-one-pathfinder-constant.md)

**Both Flatland winners gate expensive replanning behind an accumulated-damage
counter rather than behind the disruption**, and one published the **net** rather
than the gross: *"partial replanning consumed 1,965 seconds on these instances and
improved their accumulated reward by 4.812. Eventually, we solved 2 fewer instances
within 8 hours but improved the accumulated reward by 3.629."* **Repair won and then
gave back a quarter of it in runtime.** Our runtime currency is 10 ms per unit per
turn and an overrun turn is silently discarded.
[`gate-the-expensive-replan-behind-a-damage-counter`](gate-the-expensive-replan-behind-a-damage-counter.md)

**⚠ THE NEGATIVE INSIDE (B), and it should be stated plainly: nobody ran repair
against rebuild head to head.** Leg 1 grepped all seven OpenTTD codebases for
measurement vocabulary and found **no A/B, no cost accounting, and no recorded
rationale for any tuned constant.** The only measured recovery number anywhere is
RCLL's **22% of 134 plan executions containing a retry** — a success rate for
retrying, **with no alternative arm.** And the AI-planning literature's answer to the
same question is about compute, not resources, and is a negative
([`plan-repair-versus-rebuild-was-measured-and-it-was-the-wrong-question`](plan-repair-versus-rebuild-was-measured-and-it-was-the-wrong-question.md)).

**⚠ AND THE PROCESS POINT THE BUILDER NEEDS: every repair plank in this sweep is in
the INDEX's gated class.** The arena cannot measure it — the probe family fires
54,264 shots with 99.83% at our core, and `razer_probe` needs 26 attack events per
building destroyed against a league median near 10. **A repair line will look
fantastic against a fixture that cannot break anything.** The ladder can see it; the
arena cannot.

## 4. (D) SELF-INTERFERENCE — documented, and the remedy is unanimous

**Yes, and the answer to "what stopped it" is: prophylaxis at placement, never
detection afterwards.** Five implementations — Overmind plans the road **around the
planned buildings** before either exists; NoNoCAB separates the two ends of a
bidirectional route so that, in its own comment, *"we don't hinder ourselves"*;
CluelessPlus refuses to
demolish road with its own vehicle on it and *"don't demolish our own depot!"*;
AdmiralAI strips **every company-owned tile** from its demolition candidate set; Kore
bots treat their **own** shipyards and fleets as interceptors by default.
**Nobody in this sweep detects self-interference from its consequences.**
[`guard-self-interference-before-the-build-never-after`](guard-self-interference-before-the-build-never-after.md)

**This validates the SHAPE of the LOKI-10 refusal and names the half it misses.**
The binding-tile cut's §8.5 measured that the refusal catches **70% of v102's events
and 52% of the Eir archive** — it stops a turret being built onto a faced tile, but
not a **conveyor built later, aimed at an existing friendly turret** (23 and 607
events). **Overmind's ordering catches that direction and ours does not.** The
symmetric second refusal — *a conveyor may not face a friendly turret, barrier or
harvester* — also subsumes `INTO_HARVESTER` (5.64%).

**On our 6x head-to-head rate, two candidate mechanisms and a decisive cheap test.**
Flatland's official deadlock-avoidance baseline documents the blind spot of every
occupancy-based check: *"neither currently occupies that (still empty) cell, so the
opposition check finds no conflict for either of them"*. **But our unit turn order is
global-id ascending with 0 inversions over 1.8M pairs, so our two builders do NOT act
simultaneously** — which means either the second builder never checks the destination
tile's facing, or the pairs form across many rounds. **The corpus can separate these
with a histogram of one number (the build rounds of the two conveyors) and has not
been asked.**
[`the-symmetric-claim-is-the-blind-spot`](the-symmetric-claim-is-the-blind-spot.md)

**And the only mechanism that makes head-to-head structurally impossible has a
published price tag.** Flatland's CPR reserves the *direction* of each cell so no
future agent can claim the opposite, *"and thus guarantees deadlock-freeness"* — and
the winners benchmarked it at **96% success against CPR's 66% on a 1,006-train
fixture** and wrote *"We therefore did not pursue CPR."* **Three arguments say our
version escapes that cost — our reservation is already stored in the world as the
conveyor's facing, our tiles are genuinely single-purpose, and `destroy()` makes the
reservation revocable at zero cost — but they are arguments, not measurements.**
[`forbid-the-opposing-claim-and-pay-for-it`](forbid-the-opposing-claim-and-pay-for-it.md)

## 5. THE THING THAT SHOULD MAKE US UNCOMFORTABLE

The Factorio Learning Environment paper, describing six frontier LLM agents in a
factory-building benchmark:

> *"For instance, when debugging non-working structures or factories where the throughput was not at expected levels, agents often focused on whether all singular entities were working but did not investigate whether the topology of the whole structure was correct."*

**That is our binding-tile cut, written by someone who has never seen our bot.** Each
of our conveyors works. `can_build_conveyor` returns `True` for all of them. **The
topology is wrong.** The authors label the passage *"Anecdotally"* themselves and it
must not be quoted as a statistic — but the same paragraph carries the two hazards
that most threaten the planks above: *"the agents broke existing working structures
due to incorrectly identifying the root-cause of problems"* and *"Agents often fell
into a loop of greedily repeating the same fix"*. **Our `destroy()` is free, uncapped
and instant.**
[`agents-check-entities-and-not-topology`](agents-check-entities-and-not-topology.md)

**And the grounding fact: in Mindustry — the game our ruleset is mechanically
modelled on — a conveyor facing nothing is silent by design.** `pass()` returns false
forever, `clogHeat` ramps to 1 over 60 ticks, and its only three consumers are the
animation frame, unit-pushing, and the ambient sound. **The complete 174,760-byte
localisation bundle contains no warning string for it.** Factorio, in the same genre,
ships **17 named alert types including `train_no_path` and `pipeline_overextended`
and none for belts.** **The silence is structural to the genre, not an oversight in
our engine.**
[`the-game-our-ruleset-copies-ships-this-bug-silently`](the-game-our-ruleset-copies-ships-this-bug-silently.md)

## 8. WHAT I THINK IS WRONG IN THE BRIEF I WAS GIVEN

Magnus asked for this explicitly. Four items, in descending order of how much they
would change a decision.

**1. ⚠ THE SUB-QUESTION WEIGHTINGS COME FROM A TABLE THAT DOES NOT RECONCILE, AND
THE ERROR INFLATES EXACTLY THE FOUR ROWS THE BRIEF IS BUILT ON.** The binding-tile
cut's §1 class table is internally perfect — it sums to **100.000%** and **7,767
Ti/game**, matching its own stated total. Its *regrouped* table sums to **120.5%** and
**9,353 Ti**. The note says the overlap is *"slight"* and that *"the first three are
sub-classes of the two `DEAD_END_*` rows"*. **It cannot be so:** those three rows are
`33.4 + 23.9 + 11.1 = 68.4%` against `DEAD_END_GROUND + DEAD_END_BUILDING = 52.76%`.
Three rows reconcile **exactly** — fix facings `15.8` = `9.94 + 5.64 + 0.20`, no output
`15.9`, saturation `14.3` — and **all four over-counted rows are the ones the brief's
sub-questions quote**: finish 33.4 (A), repair 23.9 (B), own turrets 11.1 (D), enemy
6.1. Together they claim **74.5pp where their parent classes hold 53.50pp**, a factor
of **1.393**. **The qualitative finding (broken ≫ saturated) is untouched. The
relative priority of "finish" vs "repair" vs "stop self-blocking" is not established
at the precision the brief quotes**, and I cannot resolve it because the scripts are
session-scratch and gone.
**Consequence for this sweep: I have not sized any recommendation on 33.4 / 23.9 /
11.1.**

**2. The brief calls the 39.6% "a build-time validity failure, not a strategy error"
— but the source document explicitly splits that class into never-built and
destroyed** into a row where *"it ends on ground where nothing was ever built"* and a
row where *"a carrier once stood on that tile and was destroyed"*. **So (A)'s
mass is at most 33.4% and probably less, and (B)'s 23.9% cannot be a subset of the
39.6% at that size** (see item 1). Attributing the whole `DEAD_END_GROUND` class to
build-time validity over-states (A) and under-states (B).

**3. The 74.3% opponent figure is quoted without the 55.2% that sits next to it, and
that flips the story.** §4's table labels that column
*"our opponents *in our games* (not a field baseline)"* — the inner emphasis is the
source document's — and gives third-party at **55.2% against our 58.8%**. **We are above
the third-party average and below the specific opponents we played to round 1000** —
which is a selection statement, not a skill statement. Reaching r1000 against someone
already selects. **That table does not say the field connects its network and we do not.**

**4. Two of the brief's figures are not in the document it points at, and one uses an
instrument that document supersedes.** The brief's 116-conveyors-to-Viktor5776's-34
figure and its 41%-of-harvester-emission figure are from the parent stalled-stack
cut, not from `binding-tile-cut-2026-08-10.md`. And the binding-tile cut states in its
appendix that *"The parent cut's "the harvester's output tile" is a mode, not a property"* — 652 of 1,509 harvesters used more than one output tile — **which is
precisely the instrument the 41% figure rests on.** Both may still be right; **they
are not evidenced by the document the sweep was aimed at.**

**5. A smaller one, on scope rather than fact.** The brief says the delivered-titanium
tiebreak decides about 94% of round-1000 games (the brief's figure), which is true — but **round-1000 games
are 2,271 of 8,519 decoded, and v102 reaches round 1000 in 8 of 115 attributed
games.** The document's own §7.6 says *"Nothing here describes economies in games
decided by a core kill"*. **So the entire value of this row is capped by how often we
play to the clock**, and that is a much smaller share for our current build than the
94% suggests. It is still worth fixing — a corked network also starves the ammunition
that decides the other games — **but the sweep should not be sold on the 94%.**
