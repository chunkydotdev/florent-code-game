## ⭐ THE HEADLINE — THE POOLING BIAS HAS A SIGN, A MAGNITUDE, AND THREE CONTROLS

**Blocked league-wide with our own bot frozen inside each block — block = `(team, that
team's own version, opponent)` — a rival's later versions cost a team 8.00 percentage points
of game share against that rival's earlier ones.** 1,970 blocks, 27,847 games,
−0.0800 ± 0.0051, **t = −15.81**. At K = 32 that is **2.6 rating points per match**, and it
is invisible to every cut we currently run.

| control | result | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| coinflip split inside the block (null by construction), 3 seeds | +0.0022 / +0.0024 / −0.0014 | all \|t\| < 1 ✅ |
| opponent version debut order shuffled within opponent | +0.0063 ± 0.0050, t = +1.25 | null ✅ |
| **opponent version CONSTANT, split by median TIME** (1,867 blocks, 18,981 games) | −0.0023 ± 0.0038, **t = −0.61** | **null — kills the censoring story** ✅ |

The third control is the one that matters: a block *ends* when the team ships, and teams ship
after a bad run, so a pure time trend plus that censoring would have faked the entire effect.
With the opponent's version held constant, later-in-block is not worse. **The effect is the
opponent's version, not the clock.**

**It is a STEP, not a rate.** Dose-response by separation between halves: −6.19pp under 3h,
−6.96 at 3–8h, −7.72 at 8–16h, −9.79 at 16–36h, −11.80 beyond 36h. **An earlier draft
computed "−16pp per day" off the 11.9-hour median and that extrapolation is not licensed —
it is cut.**

**Two independent sources of the same bias, both pointing the same way.** Games against a
rival's *rolled-back* versions make them look **8.23pp weaker** than they are (407
excursions, t = −6.61). Stale retired versions and rejected prototypes both inflate our
per-opponent cells. **Our numbers read high, twice.**

**Direct exposure, measured:** across our top-15 opponents by archive volume, **72.6% of our
archived games were played against a version that opponent no longer runs**, and for five of
them the share on their current version is **0.0%**.

## THE FOUR ARMS

### (A) MEASUREMENT — how did anyone detect that the pool moved?

**The field re-versions far faster than any of our statistics assume.** Median opponent
version lifetime **1.17 h**; 50.0% replaced within an hour, 82.3% within six, 97.1% within a
day. Median team runs 20 distinct versions and 23 version changes across 10 days; only 4 of
72 active teams never changed. Hazard that an opponent's version changes inside a 20-minute
window (given ≥2 matches in it): **6.52%**; inside an hour, **14.11%**.

Three external techniques, all analysis-side:
* **CIAO matrix** (Cliff & Miller 1995, via Cartlidge & Bullock) — score current-vs-*ancestral*
  opponents as a matrix, because a number against a moving population is not comparable over
  time. **Our per-opponent statistic is the column mean of exactly that matrix.** Ships with
  a warning label: 10 of 22 published plots are "tartan" and cannot separate cycling from
  drift.
* **Glicko's Rating Deviation** — uncertainty that grows with elapsed time, by construction,
  plus a documented procedure for calibrating the growth constant. **We do not have to assume
  our constant; we measured it at 1.17 h.** But Glicko diffuses and bot strength *steps*, so
  key the staleness on observed ship events, not on a smooth clock.
* **Steamhammer's recency-discounted opponent model** — the canonical BW answer, and note
  *why*: its file is keyed by NAME and it is blind to versions, so it approximates version
  identity with exponential decay. **We can see versions, so we should do the strictly
  stronger thing and segment — hierarchically, since a hard segment starves every cell.**

### (B) EXPERIMENT DESIGN — what did anyone block on?

**The design we need was never run against a live ladder by anyone in any of these
corpora.** What exists:
* **Pollack & Blair 1998** — champion and mutant both play *the same foil* under *the same
  dice streams*, with a strict acceptance rule. Three parts, two available to us: the fixed
  third-party foil (our panel) and the strict acceptance rule (matches
  `MARGIN_IS_THE_CURRENCY`). **Common random numbers are unavailable — we cannot seed the
  engine or choose the map — and that is the single biggest variance-reduction tool in the
  paper. Say so rather than pretending the panel does the same job.**
* **CGBenchmark / CG Spunk** converged independently on blocking `seed × opponent × seat`,
  with pairing deterministic across arms and win rate reported **separately per seat**.
* **Jay Scott's anchor/equating design** — pin the opponents who are provably identical
  across both windows and re-solve everyone else against them. He states the name-vs-version
  rule explicitly: *"If some participants which are not identical have the same names,
  distinguish them somehow."* **We can execute this better than he could — he hand-assembled
  26 unchanged bots; we get version strings free on every match.**
* **Fishtest** — every patch tested against ONE frozen released reference, with the *test
  conditions themselves* versioned by date range. Plus the self-audit: **"Elo's estimates of
  passing patches are biased"**, detectable as the gap between summed patch gains and the
  end-to-end regression delta. **We have both halves and have never run it.**

**The binding constraint on all of it, stated plainly: our two arms cannot be fired
simultaneously.** `fcode match unrated` plays the *active* submission, so arms are separated
in time by at least an activation — and at a 1.17-hour median version lifetime, **a paired
design over a serial instrument is not paired.** Interleave at the finest granularity the
rate limit allows and discard any pair straddling a ship.

### (C) THE POOLING TRAP — documented wrong conclusions elsewhere

**BananaBrain, AIIDE 2020, is our incident with the arithmetic visible and a worse
outcome.** 3,000 pre-trained games against "Stardust" where the entity behind the name was an
older build or possibly a different bot entirely. Pre-training 51%, tournament 38% — **and
the per-opening ranking inverted: the best real counter scored 0% in 56 pooled games and 65%
in the tournament, while the pooled favourite with n = 1110 collapsed from 66% to 24%.**
*"It looks as though BananaBrain might have won the tournament if it had played against the
same versions of opponents that it trained against."* **A large n on the wrong subject is
worse than no data, because it buys confidence.**

Supporting cases: **Balduzzi's redundancy-invariance** states our bug as a theorem —
duplicating an agent in the evaluation population flips the ranking with no agent changed
(*"falsely suggesting agent B is superior"*); **sethorizer's 17.49%** of pairwise orderings
reversed for one unchanged bot judged by two populations; **Agade rising 23 places with
"no resubmit, no changes"**; and **CodinGame's Bo5 case**, where the leaderboard order was
traceable to *which* opponent kept playing whom — caught only because a third-party tool
stored win rate **split by opponent**.

**And the best decision in the whole sweep is a refusal.** Jay Scott, AIIDE 2021: *"there
was not enough recent data to show what was working in the most recent games. So I made no
preparation at all."* The pooled score said *overwhelming*; the rival had improved; the
number could not be decomposed. **Note the wording — "not enough RECENT data", not "not
enough data". The sample was large. It was large about the wrong thing.**

### (D) THE OFFENSIVE READ

**The intuitive play is refuted on our own data.** Freshly-shipped versions are **not**
weaker: demeaned eloDelta by version age runs +0.182 (first match, t = +1.07, null) → +0.462
→ +0.358 → +0.351 → −0.098 at 12+. A matched difference-in-differences (matched on the
pre-ship window, because **teams ship precisely when they are losing** — pre-ship 5-match
mean −0.625) gives **+0.524 ± 0.107, t = +4.89: a ship makes a team BETTER.** The naive
changepoint test says +1.25 and is mostly regression to the mean; **our own instrument nearly
produced a false finding in the opposite direction, by exactly the selection error this sweep
is about.**

**The only true "un-debugged" signature is VARIANCE, not mean** — SD falls monotonically with
version age, 9.030 → 8.084. Which makes a freshly-shipped opponent a *noisier measuring
instrument with no compensating weakness*: an argument for selecting stable cells, not
fragile ones.

**The rollback signature: hypothesis raised externally, killed by our own data.** CodinGame
competitors named and catalogued *"AI hiding"*, whose first listed implementation — *"Submit
of real AI, then replace for a bad one ASAP"* — is exactly our observed A→B→A. 63.9% of
active teams show it; 3.81% of league matches sit inside one. **But excursion versions are
8.23pp WORSE than the incumbent that brackets them (t = −6.61). They are bad ships, not
hidden strength.** The external taxonomy raised a real hypothesis and the corpus refuted it
in one cut.

**Exposure is dominated by shipping, and this is a rules fact rather than an inference.** A
pure policy in a fully observable game transmits its identity through its actions; 98.0% of
our match table is third-party matches and the platform serves replays; a bot holding the
slot publishes at ~6 games/hour while a 5-game unrated leg shows itself five times. **Every
BW and CG author who reasoned about leakage reasoned about ladder presence, never about
private test games.** The counter-moves they attested are **churn** (Steamhammer: *"Steamhammer
should be a moving target"*) and **freeze** (Iron's author: teams "updated often" are targeted,
so "some bot authors may thus decide to update their bots less often") — **two opposite
answers, neither ever measured against the other, and all of them defensive.** Freeze is
directly contradicted by our own −8.00pp; churn costs a submission and submitting is shipping.

**The one live offensive road is scouting the quiet teams.** Battlecode 2020's The High
Ground rebuilt the ladder leader's strategy from public replays (*"we decided to shamelessly
copy Java's strategy"*) — and the target shipped a counter *at the last minute*, so it never
appeared on the ladder to be scouted back. **A rival-specific counter is the class of plank
most damaged by holding the slot; deploy short and late, not resident.** And the target
selection follows from this sweep's own numbers: **scout teams whose version timeline is
quiet, because against a team reshipping hourly the read is stale by construction.**

**Positive controls:** `rating` → 85 in the Glicko flat; `exploiter` → 28 in AlphaStar;
` the ` → 11/12 academic flats, 35/43 ladder flats, 1,234/1,234 Jay Scott posts. **The
academic miss caught a real failure** — `acad_g_gdescrip.flat` is Type-3 bitmap-font garbage,
excluded from every finding; without the positive control its zero hits would have been
reported as a substantive absence.

**Three new method traps for the library:**
1. **Two-column PDFs extracted with `pdftotext -layout` INTERLEAVE THE COLUMNS**, splicing
   sentences from two columns into fluent, plausible English. **This fabricates text rather
   than failing to match** — the opposite failure mode from every trap on file. Caught on the
   Čertický–Churchill ToG paper; re-extracted with plain `pdftotext`.
2. **BSD `grep -o` with `.\{0,N\}` catastrophically backtracks** on large flat files and has
   to be killed. Use Python `str.find` for context extraction.
3. **Curly apostrophes bit twice more**, in two different corpora — now the single most
   frequent near-miss in this library. Always try both `'` and `’`.

## WHAT THE BUILDER SHOULD ACTUALLY CHANGE

1. **Block on `(opponent_team_id, opponent_version)` in every prereg, record their version
   per match on both sides, and discard any arm-pair straddling a ship.** Unrated games are
   free; pay the matches rather than the confound. Interleave arms — the block decays in
   about an hour.
2. **Add a `CELL FRESHNESS` line to the prereg**, beside `TARGET BAND`: opponent id, current
   version and its debut, games we hold *on that version*, share of our archive on retired
   versions. If that n is under the leg's MDE, the cell is not selectable **however large and
   favourable the pooled number is.**
3. **Filter rolled-back excursion matches out of every per-opponent cell** — now a bias
   correction with a measured sign (−8.23pp), not hygiene.
4. **Run the Fishtest selection-bias audit**: sum claimed per-plank gains since an anchor,
   compare to the anchor-to-now delta off per-match `ourver`. No matches required.
5. **Do not spend a leg on:** attacking a fresh version, resubmit-rerolling, reducing our own
   exploitability, or building a hall of fame. All four are closed above.
