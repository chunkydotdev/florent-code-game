## 1. `SIPHON_DENY_ON` is a go-and-come-back errand whose terminal action is already silenced

**SOURCE** — `deleting-a-multi-step-errand-was-the-improvement.md` (BC2025 The Kragle,
BC2023 don't @ me, BC2021 wololo, Overmind; `evidence: documented`, `transfers: yes`):

> *"Our robots would spend about half of their lifetime traveling back to towers and waiting
> around towers for paint."*

> *"Only after we started looking carefully at what other teams were doing did we notice that
> no other team bothered having their bots refill on paint."*

and the file's own summary of the Kragle goal-stack episode:

> **The cheapest solution to an interrupt that wrecks your plans may be to delete the interrupt.**

**FLAG-SIZED DIFF — ONE CONSTANT.** `doctrine.py:913` `SIPHON_DENY_ON = True` → `False`.

The defect, read out of the tree rather than argued: `LOKI_QUIET_ON = True`
(`doctrine.py:1470`) silences the siphon melee at `eco.py:911-912`
(`if LOKI_QUIET_ON: return False   # QUIET: siphon melee silenced`), which is the **only**
terminal action `_siphon_deny` has. The rest of the routine is untouched and still runs from
`eco.py:1017`. So a builder that has acquired a siphon target:

* at manhattan distance > 1 sets `self.tgt = tgt` and navigates to the enemy belt
  (`eco.py:924-925`), consuming its move for the round and returning `True`, which short-circuits
  the whole rest of `_expand`;
* on arrival at distance 1 returns `False`, falls through to the ore picker, and walks off;
* next round it is at distance 2 again and walks back.

That ping-pong runs until `rnd - siphon_since > SIPHON_MAX_RNDS` (24, `doctrine.py:926`),
after which the tile is banned for `SIPHON_BAN_RNDS` (200, `doctrine.py:929`) and
`_find_siphon` rescans every `SIPHON_SCAN_EVERY` (4) rounds for the next one. It applies to
expanders, to the defender's `_expand` fallback, **and to any stood-down raider**, because
`raid.py:149` and `raid.py:163` both hand the body to `_expand`.

**MECHANISM CLAIM (one line).** Deleting the errand hands those rounds back to movement, so
raiders that stand down should re-establish sooner and expanders should stop parking beside
enemy belts. Visible in a head-to-head win rate, and much more cheaply in an event count:
**rounds in which a builder navigates to a siphon target** should go 0 in treatment, and
builder distance-to-enemy-core at r150/r300 should fall.

**PRECONDITION.** That an opponent ever plants a conveyor orthogonally adjacent to one of our
harvesters. **MEASURED, and it is the largest number in the siphon block** —
`doctrine.py:860-887` records the instrumented probe game (49 of 49 stacks banked by the enemy
belt while it was the only acceptor; 99 friendly / 98 enemy once ours existed) and the wild
rate: *"4.41% of every stack we mined was banked by the ENEMY core, 81% of that volume through
exactly this adjacency shape, worst game 58.5%"*. **What is UNMEASURED is how many rounds per
game the dead walk actually burns** — nobody has counted it, because `LOKI_QUIET_ON` and
`SIPHON_DENY_ON` were shipped by different planks and never read together.

**ON-PROGRAMME.** It is a removal that buys arrival, which is the exact mechanism
LOKI-7/LOKI-8 shipped on (`doctrine.py:1456-1462`, `doctrine.py:1474-1481`). It is not
defence, screening or heal-uptime. If the honest verdict turns out to be "the siphon deny
was load-bearing income", the correct fix is the other one constant (`LOKI_QUIET_ON = False`
for the siphon branch only) — but both cannot be right and today neither is.

## 3. Turret targeting pays 24–30 Ti of ammo for a kill that refunds the enemy's cost scale

**SOURCE** — `displace-dont-kill.md` (`evidence: documented (the principle) / inference (the
application to our cost-scale rule)`, `transfers: yes`):

> So killing an enemy builder:
> - **makes their next builder cheaper** (removes its +20% from their scale), and
> - **frees one of their 50 unit slots**, and
> - costs us **24-30 Ti** of converted ammo to do (40 HP = 3 sentinel or 6 gunner shots).

> - attacks that **remove** builders attack a resource they cannot buy — but they
>   also hand back the scale refund;
> - attacks that **waste their builders' turns** cost them the one thing they cannot
>   buy and refund them nothing. **That is the uniquely favourable quadrant.**

Corroborated in the same slice by `deny-production-not-units.md` (Jay Scott, Planet Wars;
Agade, 1st, Ghost in the Cell; `evidence: documented`):

> *"You don’t win by trading existing ships, you win by closing the enemy’s supply of new
> ships by taking all their planets."*

> *"My goal with bombs is to deny production, the killing of units being too uncertain."*

**FLAG-SIZED DIFF — ONE DICT ENTRY.** `main.py:702-708`, the `_turret` priority map. Today
`EntityType.BUILDER_BOT: 3` (`main.py:704`) outranks `LAUNCHER: 4`, `HARVESTER: 5`,
`CONVEYOR/SPLITTER: 6`. Move `BUILDER_BOT` to 6 (below harvester and launcher, above barrier).
One line.

**MECHANISM CLAIM.** Our sentinels stop spending 10-ammo shots on 40 HP mobile targets that
refund +20% of the enemy's scale when they die, and spend them on 20–30 HP immobile production
instead. Visible as an event count long before it is visible in a win rate: **shots fired at
enemy builder bots** should fall and **enemy harvester/conveyor destructions** should rise.

**PRECONDITION — and this is where I would spend the measurement first.** The forward sentinel
is sited by `can_fire_from(..., SENTINEL, core_tile)` (`raid.py:433`), so an enemy core tile is
in its line by construction and `CORE: 0` wins every round the core is targetable. The dict only
decides what the turret does when the core is **not** in the attackable set — home turrets, and
forward turrets whose core tile is occupied. **What fraction of our turret shots are currently
resolved by the non-core branch is UNMEASURED**, and if it is small this plank has no dose.
`displace-dont-kill`'s own builder hook says the same thing about the other half: *"I found
**no sourced case of a team throwing an enemy unit purely to waste its turns and measuring that
against killing it.**"* — the arithmetic is the evidence, not the literature.

**ON-PROGRAMME.** Denial, not defence: it attacks the enemy's ability to make more, which
`deny-production-not-units` frames as a sequencing claim on the kill — *"a way to make the
later kill affordable, not a substitute for it."*
