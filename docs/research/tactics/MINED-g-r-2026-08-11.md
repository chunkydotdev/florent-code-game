## 1. SEAL THE FOUR CORNERS, NOT JUST THE EIGHT SEATS — close the ring the collar already half-builds

**Confidence: highest in the slice.** It is one membership test, the mechanism is
an engine fact rather than an analogy, and three independent files in this slice
converge on it.

**SOURCE — `minimum-cost-blockading-body.md`:**

> **A blockade made of builder bots costs 10× the titanium and 20× the scale
> increment of one made of barriers**

> - A builder attack is **2 damage for 2 Ti**, so a 30 HP barrier costs them
>   **15 builder-turns and 30 Ti** to remove. **10:1 in titanium and 15:1 in
>   turns, against an object we placed in one turn.**

> the spawn-ring seal is a **~36 Ti,
> 12-builder-turn** proposition, not a 360 Ti one — which moves it from "obviously
> unaffordable" into the range of a 500 Ti opening bank.

**Corroborating source — `press-them-onto-their-own-spawn.md`** (this is the part
that makes the seal forced rather than merely cheap):

> **Read that alongside `can_spawn` requiring PASSABLE: the only known defence
> against a spawn-lock is a form of self-suffocation.** To stop us barriering a
> ring tile, they must stand a body on it — and their own body on their own ring
> tile is exactly the object BC2024 says suffocated Cout for Clout. **A defender
> cannot deny us the tile and keep the tile.**

**Corroborating source — `price-the-emplacement-against-what-counters-it.md`:**

> prefer, at equal role, whichever structure has the higher
> removal-cost-to-build-cost ratio — which on the numbers above is the barrier, by an order
> of magnitude

**FLAG-SIZED DIFF.** `raid.py:271`, inside `_raid_act` step 2:

```
for d in CARDINALS:
    t = p.add(d)
    if (t.x, t.y) not in seatkeys:     # <-- raid.py:271-272
        continue
```

`seatkeys` is built at `raid.py:99` (`self.raid_seatkeys = frozenset(...)`) from
`heal_seats` only. Add a `raid_cornerkeys` frozenset beside it from
`self.raid_corners` (already computed, `raid.py:97`) and test membership of the
union. **One new frozenset, one changed test.** A second one-line refinement —
iterate corners before seats — matters because a raider must *stand on a flanking
seat* to reach a corner, so sealing seats first can wall us out of the corners.

**MECHANISM CLAIM.** Barriers standing on the enemy 12-tile ring should rise from
a ceiling of 8 to a ceiling of 12, and the enemy's builder-spawn count should
fall. **Visible as an event count first** (barriers built on ring tiles; enemy
builder population over time), and only then as a head-to-head win rate.

**PRECONDITION.** Partly MEASURED, and the untested link is named in the library
itself. MEASURED: `destroy()` is allied-only and a body on a ring tile makes it
unbuildable — `press-them-onto-their-own-spawn.md` cites the s24 probe
(`bots/_probe_prison`) for `is_tile_empty = True` but `can_build_barrier = False`.
MEASURED (project CLAUDE.md, live, 165 games): we already put a body on the enemy
12-tile ring in 0.586 of rounds game-mean, so raiders do reach the ring.
**UNMEASURED, and it is the whole prize:** the same file says outright *"the probe
measured buildability under a friendly body, not spawnability. Whether
`can_spawn` returns False on a ring tile holding the core's own builder is
untested."* A partial seal is worth something on its own (each sealed tile is one
fewer spawn option and one fewer heal seat); the **lock** is the untested claim.
UNMEASURED: how often the incumbent currently gets all 8 seats sealed.

**WHY ON-PROGRAMME.** Pure denial, at the enemy core, on the tiles that are
simultaneously their spawn ring, their heal seats and their delivery termini
(`eco.py:125-138`). It attacks reinforcement, repair and income with one object.

**HONEST RISK, stated because it is real.** Barriers are bot-impassable, so a
fully sealed ring also locks *our* raiders off the ring — which removes the
"HOLD THE COLLAR" heal at `raid.py:312` that keeps the barriers alive. The code
degrades gracefully by construction (`can_build_barrier` refuses an occupied
tile, and `_raid_station` skips impassable stations at `raid.py:519`), so the
natural steady state is *eleven barriers plus one body*, which is still a
complete ring and keeps one healer adjacent. That is an argument for firing it,
not against, but it should be read off the replay rather than assumed.

## 2. GATE THE RUSH ON CORE-TO-CORE DISTANCE — the map term LOKI-22 does not have

**Confidence: high, and this is the one idea in the slice where our own paired
measurement already supplies the sign.**

**SOURCE — `map-size-decides-whether-the-rush-is-legal.md`** (no banner):

> `get_map_width()` and `get_map_height()`
> are free, available on round 0, need no scouting and no store slot. If Loki
> commits at all, the commit should be conditioned on them.

> **our attacker's
> arrival cost is superlinear in map size while the defender's is zero.** A builder
> bot moves one cardinal step per move-cooldown and cannot move diagonally, so a
> diagonal crossing of a 30x30 map is ~58 moves, not 30. The defender pays nothing
> to be at home.

**⚠ SIGN CONFLICT, stated up front.** The file's own source (wololo, BC2021)
says the rush dies on *large* maps and survives on *small* ones. **Our own paired
360-game measurement says the opposite for our engine** — so take the *principle*
(condition the commit on geometry) from the library and the *direction* from our
own data. Do not carry wololo's sign across.

**FLAG-SIZED DIFF.** Two sites, one condition:
* `raid.py:406` — `rush = LOKI2_RUSH_ON and ct.get_current_round() < LOKI2_RUSH_RND`
* `main.py:323-326` — the same window test in the roster branch

Add `and <core-to-core distance is LONG>` to both, or (cleaner, one place) define
`LOKI2_RUSH_MIN_DIST` in `doctrine.py` beside `LOKI2_RUSH_RND` (`doctrine.py:1392`)
and compute the distance from `self.core` and `enemy_core_for(...)`, which
`eco.py:35` already provides and `raid.py:84` already calls. The map-size idiom
already exists in the tree (`eco.py:610`, `small = self.mw * self.mh <= 220`).

**MECHANISM CLAIM.** On SHORT maps the bot should behave exactly as
`LOKI2_RUSH_ON = False` (bit-for-bit), and on LONG maps exactly as LOKI-22.
Core-kill share should stop losing the ~20–35pp the rush costs on the short band
while keeping whatever LOKI-22 finds on the long band. **Visible in head-to-head
win rate split by map, and confirmable as an event count** (forward-sentinel
plant round, per map).

**PRECONDITION. MEASURED**, `docs/RESULT-rush-map-interaction-2026-08-09.md`,
paired deterministic, 360 games: `orizon_probe` SHORT −35.4pp (p = 0.0005), LONG
+7.1pp (p = 0.51, null); `cad_probe` SHORT −22.9pp (p = 0.0192), LONG −14.3pp
(p = 0.15, null). Bands are defined in `docs/PREREG-rush-map-interaction-2026-08-09.md`
as core-to-core distance **SHORT ≤ 16** (8 maps) and **LONG ≥ 24** (7 maps).
That document already draws this exact conclusion — *"suppress the rush on SHORT
maps, where it measurably costs share, and may leave it on the LONG band, where
both opponents read null"* — and **it was never built**: `_v138loki22` sets
`LOKI2_RUSH_ON = True` with no map term at all. CAVEAT the RESULT doc states about
itself: band is confounded with map identity, not merely distance.
**⚠ Adjacency to a burned item:** "the early rush" is on the spent list. This is
not the rush; it is the map term the rush has never carried.

**WHY ON-PROGRAMME.** It buys the committed opening — three turrets by r22, the
kill — on the population where it is not measured to cost, and stops paying for
it where it is.

## 3. DELETE THE RAID STAND-DOWN PAUSE — the assault is re-derived from the economics that would never have started it

**Confidence: medium-high on mechanism, unmeasured on dose.**

**SOURCE — `it-walked-away-from-a-kill-it-had-won.md`.**
**⚠ THIS FILE CARRIES A BANNER and I am respecting it:**
`⚠ **TIER 2.** Read through a text proxy (the local artifact carries the proxy's`
`Title:` / `URL Source:` / `Markdown Content:` header), **not diffed against Kaggle's
original HTML**.` — i.e. the *source attribution* is tier-2. The two claims I use
below are the file's own reasoning about **our** engine, not the proxied quote.

> Our forward sentinel that stops being serviced **dies**, and its 30 Ti and its
> contribution to the one global cost scale are gone. **Withdrawal is not free here;
> it is the most expensive way to lose the titanium we already spent.**

> The transferable rule is narrow and cheap: **once an assault is under way, the
> decision to continue must not be re-derived from the same per-turn economics that
> would never have started it.**

**FLAG-SIZED DIFF.** `raid.py:232-234`:

```
self.raid_stalls += 1
if self.raid_stalls >= 3:
    self.raid_stalls = 0
    self.raid_pause_until = rnd + 60
```

Both `3` and `60` are bare literals in `raid.py` with no doctrine constant and no
measurement attached to either. **One number** (`60 → 0`, or `3 → a larger
number`) is the whole diff. Note this is the *navigation* stall path — LOKI-6's
`LOKI6_STALL_SEPARATE` fixed the counter that fed it, but left the pause itself
untouched.

**MECHANISM CLAIM.** Rounds spent by raiders inside `_expand` (the stand-down
branch at `raid.py:145-149`) should fall and rounds spent navigating to the ring
should rise. **Visible as an event count** — raiders established at the ring per
round, and `SLOT_RAID_LIVE` heartbeat continuity — before it is visible in win
rate.

**PRECONDITION. UNMEASURED, and this is the honest answer:** nothing in the repo
counts how often `raid_pause_until` is actually armed per game, nor how many
raider-rounds it consumes. **What would be needed:** one replay pass counting
rounds in which a `role == "raid"` builder is executing `_expand` while an enemy
anchor is known. If the pause almost never arms, this is dead weight and the
cheapest possible null. Supporting context that is MEASURED but is *not* this
number: `doctrine.py:1475-1481` records that every winning change on this line
was a removal that handed rounds back to movement (33.3% → 53.3% → 80.0% → 86.7%
core-kill share, one fixture, n=15 each, 3 real opponents).

**WHY ON-PROGRAMME.** It is a removal of a withdrawal. The programme forbids
defence; this clause converts a raider into an economy body for 60 rounds on a
navigation failure.

**WHY ON-PROGRAMME.** The forward sentinel is described in the tree as *"the only
