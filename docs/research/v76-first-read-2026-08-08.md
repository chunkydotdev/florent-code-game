## 2. What "nests" actually is

### 2.1 The nest itself

- **Gunner, never sentinel** — `turret_type = EntityType.GUNNER; ranges = (3, 2)`
  (**v76:2091-2092**). v74 built a **sentinel** first on the seat-0 engineer
  (`PRIMARY_SENTINEL`, v74:1985-1990); v76 deletes that choice. Rationale in his comment
  (v76:2086-2090): 20 Ti / 7 dmg / reload 1 versus 30 Ti / 18 dmg / reload 2 is similar
  sustained DPS, but "the gunner is a third cheaper to rebuild, and rebuilds are the
  doctrine."
- **Placement**: a full cardinal BFS flood from the raider's tile gives true route distance
  (**v76:2101-2122**), then every (Core tile × 8 facings × ray length 3/2 × 4 approach
  tiles) is scored (**v76:2127-2176**). Rays blocked by a wall are rejected
  (**v76:2148-2152**); the builder is penalised for standing *in* its own gun's ray
  (`ray_penalty = 20`, **v76:2170**). Result: nest sits at **d² 4-9** from the Core tile it
  shells.
- **Cap = 3 LIVE guns within d²≤20 of the enemy Core** (`NEST_CAP`/`NEST_GUN_DSQ`,
  **v76:109-110**), counted by `_live_nests` (**v76:2038-2064**). This is the rebuild
  mechanism, stated as such in his docstring: "a nest the defenders clear drops out of this
  count the moment its tile is rubble, so the planner re-arms."
- **Nests 3+ are eco-gated** (`NEST_ECO_FREE = 2`, **v76:111-112, 2075-2076**): the first
  two go down on the opening 500 Ti bank; the third waits for `SLOT_HARVESTERS ≥ ECO_NEED`.

### 2.2 The ferry (catapult line)

- Two launchers on the core-to-core axis at **4 and 9 Chebyshev steps** from x3r0's own Core
  (`LAUNCHER_L1_STEPS`/`L2_STEPS`, **v76:96-97**), sited by `_launcher_axis_site`
  (**v76:2220-2267**) with the site snapped to the nearest legal tile in a radius-2 box,
  **excluding his Core's yard seats and non-empty terrain** (**v76:2252-2273**).
- Built by the raiders on their way out — raider #1 (seat 0) owns L1, raider #2 (seat 3)
  owns L2 (**v76:1331-1340, 1701-1710**). Give-up at **round 60** (`LAUNCHER_GIVEUP_RND`,
  v76:100).
- In transit a raider looks for a friendly launcher **not behind it** (`_ferry_launcher`,
  **v76:2327-2353**), claims the single insertion slot, stands still inside the pickup ring,
  and gets thrown ~5 tiles forward. **Hold is capped at 6 rounds** then the ferry is banned
  for 30 (`FERRY_HOLD_MAX`/`FERRY_BAN_RNDS`, **v76:125-126, 2506-2519**) — his comment cites
  a measured 200-round deadlock on saga. **The ferry is an accelerant, not a dependency**:
  a banned raider simply walks.
- The `_launcher` side gained a **midfield anchor backfill** (**v76:4569-4577**): an L2
  launcher 9 tiles out cannot see its own Core within r²=26, so the M1 ancestor returned
  early and *never threw a single bot*. v76 recovers its own Core by reflecting the enemy
  anchor through the symmetric map table. **This is the fix that makes the whole ferry
  work** — without it the subsystem is inert.

### 2.3 Nest persistence (the repair half)

- `_heal_battle_adjacent` (**v76:2355-2382**): a raider adjacent to a friendly
  **GUNNER / LAUNCHER / BARRIER** heals it, 1 Ti for +4 HP. Economy buildings are
  deliberately excluded. This out-paces the 2 dmg builder pecks that clear most nests.
- The primary nest (`SLOT_SIEGE`, published by seat 0) is repaired **first**, ahead of any
  other work (**v76:2443-2450**).
- Action order inside `_raider` (**v76:2437-2461**): build ferry launcher → repair primary
  nest → repair any adjacent combat structure → **build a nest (only inside the nest zone)**
  → melee → plug a mine.

### 2.4 Mine plugging

`_try_mine_plug` (**v76:2384-2416**): a **3 Ti barrier on an empty ore tile in the enemy
half**. Gated on round ≥ 25 and a 12 Ti reserve (**v76:116-117**). The intended sequence is
melee kills their harvester (harvesters already rank right after guns/Core in
`_sabotage_prio`), the tile is bare ore next round, and the barrier plugs it before rebuild.
3 Ti deletes a 2.5 Ti/round income stream. **This is the cheapest new mechanism in the file
and the one most likely to actually pay.**

### 2.5 Heal-war retarget

`_turret` (**v76:4461-4484**): any turret that can see the enemy Core publishes its HP to
`SLOT_ENEMY_HP`, packed as `((last_drop_round + 1) << 9) | hp`. If the published HP has not
**dropped** for 15 rounds (`HEALWAR_STALL_RNDS`, v76:130) while a turret sits within d²≤32
of the Core, target priority flips to **enemy BUILDERS first** — kill the medics. See §5.4:
**as wired this is close to inert on the nest guns.**

### 2.6 Core-grind gate

`_sabotage_prio` (**v76:1981-1986**): melee pecks on the enemy **Core** are now allowed only
as a SUPPLEMENT (≥2 guns standing) or a FINISHER (Core ≤ 150 HP). His own forensics in the
comment: "two raiders sank ~800 Ti of pecks r125-325 into a Core their medics held at ~400
HP." See §5.1 — **the gate is defeated by the counter it reads.**

### 2.7 Jam handling (three separate fixes)

SLINGSHOT masses raiders down one axis, which produced pile-ups v74 never saw:
- `_bfs_direction` now treats **visible builder bots as blocked** in the plan
  (**v76:4244-4253**) — his measured failure was a four-bot gridlock standing 250 rounds in
  a one-exit ore pocket.
- `_nav` gained a **jam-breaker** (**v76:4337-4348**): past `stuck ≥ 8`, step to *any* free
  cardinal, id-rotated so two jammed bots do not mirror forever.
- `_move` gained a **traceback fix** (**v76:4380-4392**): a bot displaced by a launcher
  between its own turns held a stale `pave_prev` many tiles away; `is_tile_empty` on it
  raised "Position out of vision range". His note says this was the frame behind **6 of 204**
  tracebacks in his own h2h battery and in the M1 mirror sweep. A non-adjacent `pave_prev`
  is now proof of displacement and dropped. **Note this covers the ENEMY-throw case too** —
  the v72-era "108 enemy throws, 15 builders lost, 0 adaptation" gap is now at least
  crash-safe, though still not tactically answered.

### 2.8 The kill pattern — what a 59% core-kill share should look like

The measured 59% core-kill share (verdict) is the doctrine working. From source, the kill is
**ranged, cumulative, and never melee-led**, and it should be recognisable in replay:

1. **The damage is gunner ray fire, not pecks.** Nests are gunners at **7 dmg / reload 1**
   (v76:2091). Three live nests sustain **~10.5 HP/round** on a 500 HP Core — about 48 rounds
   of uninterrupted fire, or realistically 150-300 rounds with clears and rebuilds. Melee is
   a *supplement*, explicitly gated (§2.6).
2. **It is a persistence race, not a burst.** There is no coordinated alpha strike anywhere
   in the file — no burst timer, no synchronised volley, no forward *production*. The whole
   mechanism is: stand a gun, repair it at +4 HP/round, rebuild it when it dies, repeat. The
   expected replay signature is a **long shallow HP slope on the defender's Core with
   repeated resets**, not a cliff.
3. **The economic half is untouched v74.** No forward harvesters, no siege train, no second
   production base. Everything forward is 3 raider bodies, 2 launchers, ≤3 gunners, and 3 Ti
   barriers. **This is a cheap offense** — roughly 20 Ti per nest plus 1 Ti/round upkeep —
   which is why it can run all game without collapsing the economy behind it.
4. **Two accelerants** decide how early it lands: the ferry (raiders arrive in ~2-3 hops
   instead of walking 15-25 tiles, §2.2) and the r0 ammo prime (§2.3 / v76:1063-1067) so the
   first nest never dry-fires.
5. **Tiebreak fallback is weak by construction.** With no forward economy and half its
   replacement hands redirected to raiding (§5.8), the 41% of decided games that *don't* end
   in a core kill are where v76 should be softest — and tiebreak 1 is titanium delivered.

**Matching this against the tape**: hooks P3 (rebuild persistence), P1 (Core-directed melee
volume), and P7 (forward commitment) together decide whether the 59% comes from nests working
as designed or from something else. If P3 shows rebuild counts ≈1 while the core-kill share
stays at 59%, the doctrine is **not** the cause and the read needs to look elsewhere — most
likely at our own heal line (Law 2) rather than at his offense.

## 5. Risk scan (ranked)

**Structural note first — the things that are NOT wrong.** `run()` wraps `_dispatch` in a
blanket `try/except Exception` with a once-per-unit stderr traceback (**v76:898-909**), so
**no path in this file can produce the unit-kill class**; a bug costs one round's action.
There are **zero bare `except:`** clauses (45 `except Exception`, all typed). CPU discipline
is genuinely good: an 8,000 µs self-bail of the 10,000 µs budget (`CPU_BUDGET_US`, v76:592)
checked at **14 sites**, including *inside* every BFS loop every 64 steps (:1445, :2107,
:4050, :4086, :4303). Every new mutating call site is `can_*`-gated. **This file is hard to
kill.** The risks below are economic and logical, not crash risks.

### 5.1 WORST — the CORE-GRIND GATE reads a counter his own file documents as broken

The gate (**v76:1981-1986**) is specified in its own comment as "two or more guns already
**standing**". It is implemented as:

```python
if ct.read_store(SLOT_HOME_GUN) < 2 and not (_ev and (_ev & 511) <= 150):
    continue
```

`SLOT_HOME_GUN` is a **monotonic build-ever high-water counter**. It is incremented at four
sites (:2213 nest build, :3072/:3076 harvester turret, :3192 counterbattery) and
**decremented nowhere**. v76 line **1222** says so in as many words:

> `SLOT_HOME_GUN` cannot answer this: it is never decremented, so rubble and distant
> artillery both read as a live gun.

He wrote `_live_nests` (:2038) specifically because builds-ever is the wrong semantics for a
rebuild doctrine — and then, 60 lines away, wired the new gate to builds-ever. Consequences:

1. The counter is fed by **home** turrets too (counterbattery, mine-guard), so it crosses 2
   on most maps by roughly r30-80 **without a single nest ever standing**.
2. From that round on, the gate is permanently open and raiders melee-peck the enemy Core
   unconditionally — precisely the 2 Ti/2 dmg vs 1 Ti/+4 HP exchange (**8:1 against**) the
   gate exists to prevent, and precisely the ~800 Ti his own comment measured.
3. The same counter drives the ammo engine's `weapons` term (:1060, :1096-1105), so
   `ammo_target` saturates at 48-72 for guns that are rubble.

This is the single highest-value finding in the read and it is **falsifiable from replay**:
count `builderAttack` events targeting enemy Core tiles after `SLOT_HOME_GUN` would have
crossed 2 (see hook P1).

### 5.3 HIGH — the E1 peacetime ammo floor is disabled two different ways

E1 (the plank our line took *from him*) raised the weapons-but-peaceful titanium floor from
12 to 46 so ammo top-up could not pin the bank below the ~23 Ti harvester rebuild price. Its
comment survives verbatim at **v76:1106-1118**, including the claim at :1102-1104 that "the
peacetime ti_floor of 46 below is untouched." **The code contradicts the comment twice:**

1. **`race_lead` override** (**v76:1119**): `ti_floor = 12 if (under or race_lead) else (46 if weapons else 52)`.
   `race_lead` is true whenever the published enemy Core HP is below his own
   (**v76:1062**). The moment a nest lands one shot on an undamaged Core, the floor
   collapses from 46 to 12 and stays there for the rest of the game.
2. **Dry-fire block** (**v76:1068-1073**): with any weapon standing and ammo < 16, it
   converts down to a hardcoded 12 Ti floor **before** the E1 gate is ever reached, every
   round.

Combined with §5.1's inflated `weapons`, the ammo engine spends against a floor of 12 for
turrets that no longer exist. This is a direct regression against the plank whose own
forensics measured 522-739 Ti of drain, and it is measurable: `coreConvertAmmo` totals
against live-turret counts (hook P2).

### 5.4 HIGH — the heal-war retarget is close to inert on the nest guns

The retarget (§2.5) only reorders targets inside the `get_attackable_tiles()` scan at
**v76:4500-4533**. But:

- Nests are **GUNNER-only** by construction (:2091).
- A gunner's path through `_turret` hits the `get_gunner_target()` early-return first
  (**v76:4485-4496**) and fires-and-returns whenever the nearest targetable tile in its
  facing line is hostile — the healer_focus table is never consulted.
- A gunner's attack pattern *is* its single facing line, and its shots are obstacle-blocked,
  so on the rare fall-through the reordering has almost nothing new to choose from: a
  farther builder behind the Core fails `can_fire`.
- The rule can therefore only really bite on a **SENTINEL within d²≤32 of the enemy Core**
  (:4476-4478) — and M2 builds **no forward sentinels at all**. Home sentinels never get
  within d²≤32 of the enemy Core.

Net: a headline mechanism that is likely to produce ~zero observable behaviour change. Note
the *incidental* version still works — a medic standing in a nest gunner's ray is already the
nearest target and gets shot — but that is geometry, not the rule. Stated as a prediction so
it can be falsified (hook P4).

### 5.5 MODERATE — `SLOT_LAUNCHER` is a max-of-ordinals, not a count

Writers use `write_store(SLOT_LAUNCHER, max(read, self.launcher_no))` (**v76:2304-2305,
2321-2322**), and readers gate on `>= 2` (**:2281, :2477**). If **L2 is built before L1** —
raider #1 dead, or its site blocked, or a reinforcement raider gets `launcher_no = 2` first —
the slot reads 2 and **the entire L1 build is cancelled for the rest of the game** even
though only one launcher exists. Nothing decrements it either, so a **destroyed** launcher is
never rebuilt (he accepts this explicitly at :1354-1357). Self-limiting because the ferry is
an accelerant, not a dependency, but it silently halves the throw line.

Secondary: the reinforcement-raider launcher rebuild (**:1355-1361**) is effectively dead
code — reinforcements arrive at role_n ≥ 5, typically past `LAUNCHER_GIVEUP_RND = 60`, so
`_try_build_ferry_launcher` latches `launcher_done` on its first call.

### 5.6 MODERATE — the ferry ack can be lost to a same-round store collision

The raider consumes its ack with `write_store(SLOT_LAUNCHED_ID, 0)` (**v76:1634**) while the
launcher writes the same slot on every throw (**:4660, :4666, :4688**). Writes are buffered
and land next round; two writers to one slot in one round means **last writer wins**. Under a
ferry that throws the same raider many times per game, an ack will occasionally be dropped,
leaving a raider with stale walking state. Mitigated — not fixed — by the §2.7 traceback
guard, which drops a non-adjacent `pave_prev` regardless of ack.

### 5.7 MODERATE — `SLOT_ENEMY_HP` latches stale when the raiders die

Only units that can *see* the enemy Core publish to slot 5 (**v76:1286-1316**). Home gunners
(r²=13) and home sentinels (r²=32) never can; in practice **only raiders** (builder r²=20,
standing at the Core) publish. If every raider dies, slot 5 freezes at its last value — and
with it `race_lead` (§5.3) and the core-grind FINISHER branch (`≤ 150`, §5.1/:1984) stay
latched open on stale data, indefinitely.

### 5.8 LOW-MODERATE — economy loses half its replacement hands

v74 made **every** replacement builder (role_n ≥ 5) a generic expander, explicitly because
"the measured shortfall was ... not enough hands laying harvesters and conveyors"
(v74:1224-1232). v76 alternates: odd seats become raiders (**v76:1352-1366**). With
`REPLACEMENT_MAX = 8` that is up to 4 bodies redirected from an economy his own v74 comment
says was already short-handed. The base five seats are unchanged.

### 5.9 LOW — CPU pressure in the nest zone, but well guarded

A raider inside the nest zone with no valid plan can call `_plan_siege` (full cardinal
map flood + a 4×8×3×4 candidate search) up to **three times per round**: once via
`_try_siege_build` in the action phase (**:2452**), once in the move phase (**:2489**), once
more on the `stuck >= 3` replan (**:2491-2494**). This is real but **well mitigated**: the
`_cpu_exhausted` guard sits between the phases (**:2463**), `_plan_siege` re-checks on entry
(**:2083**) and every 64 BFS steps (**:2107**), and all state writes happen last so a bail is
a clean no-op. Caveat worth recording: **the guard cannot be tested locally** —
`get_cpu_time_elapsed()` reads 0 under `fcode run` even with `--tle` (his note at
v76:588-591, matching `docs/tooling.md`). So its calibration is unverified on both sides.

### 5.10 LOW — home defense is thinner by design

The v74 saboteur melee recall is deleted (diff `@@ -1685,33 +1793,12 @@`); raiders never
return. He is explicit that this is doctrine ("L3 spec 8: the nest persistence IS the
mechanism; the ferry line only runs forward") and that the interceptor (role_n 1), the
defender (role_n 4), counterbattery, and the universal adjacent heal all keep their hands.
Genuine exposure, deliberately taken — flagged for the production read rather than as a
defect.

**Law 1 — ray coverage (INTO, double-edged).** The law: across 30 games, **0 of 147
uncovered enemy turrets took a single shot from us** (combined corpora **0/216**), and in the
nine CAD v117 losses **uncovered turrets did 11,935 of the 12,349 turret-damage points that
killed our core — 96.6%** (`v72-bleed-cad-family-2026-08-08.md:33-49`). Lifetime separation
reproduces at n=405: **covered median 8 rounds (wins) / 11 (losses) vs uncovered 81 / 105**
(`kcm-win-c1-validation-2026-08-07.md:365-368`). v76's nests at d²4-9 are precisely this
establishment class, and **persistent same-tile rebuild is the strongest possible play into
it** — against an uncovered defender they are lethal. Against a tile-aimed fixed-facing
sentinel ring the same loop inverts into a kill farm: `v72-bleed-cad-family:56-61` records
sentinel #68's ray covering a tile CAD re-planted 13 times, "every one of them dead within
2-4 rounds". **This is the single sharpest lever we have against v76** — and note the law's
own caveat that coverage is *necessary but not sufficient* (covered→killed is only 19/30 in
the wild, `kcm-wild-establishment-rates-2026-08-07.md:64-75`); it "predicts our capability,
not our conduct" (`tipping-point-decoder-2026-08-08.md:377-390`).

**Law 2 — bimodal heal (INTO, hard).** heal/dmg **≥0.94 → 13/13 survived; ≤0.86 → 16/16
died** (`v72-bleed-cad-family:114-121`), replicating 10/11 in production
(`v73-production-read-2026-08-08.md:508-511`). The shortfall is **1-3 bodies, not titanium**
— one loss died with 9,557 Ti banked — and the re-read settled that **seat availability
explains ~0 of it: the binding constraint was BODIES in 101/101 sampled siege rounds**
(`graft-brief-2026-08-08.md:55-66`). v76's heal-war retarget (§2.5) is aimed at *exactly*
that constraint: kill the medics, not the Core. **On paper this is the most dangerous plank
in the file.** The mitigation is §5.4 — as wired it is close to inert on gunner nests, which
is precisely why hook P4 is worth its own row. If x3r0 ever fixes the wiring, this plank
gets much more dangerous, so P4 is also an early-warning tripwire for the *next* version.

**Law 3 — CAD-family counterbattery (AGAINST, degraded not collapsed).** The measured rule:

**d²≤13 it is 84% killed / median life 8 rounds** (`cad-probe-refreeze-spec-2026-08-08.md:354-385`).
The answer is a **gunner counter-plant in 75 of 76 measured pairs** (95.6% of damage). v76's
nests sit inside that band. **But v76 arrives with CAD's own mitigations**: persistent
rebuild (CAD re-plants on the exact tile of a dead turret **281 times over 40 games**) and
heal upkeep under siege (CAD's builder heals go **4.27 → 19.99 per 100 rounds, a 4.7×
response**). So v76 is running the CAD playbook rather than the naive snipe, and against
CAD-family opponents this should look like a grind rather than a wipe. The counter's *siting*
still fires 100% of the time.

**Law 4 — Ouroboros attrition-from-screen (AGAINST, twice).** Ouroboros keeps ~a quarter of
its gunners at d>144 as a home screen whose fire profile is **44.3% conveyor / 22.0% builder
bot** — "it suppresses our forward economy and our raiders; it is not aimed at our core at
all" — and that screen "is what keeps their builder losses at a median of 1/game against our
median of 12" (`ouro-probe-refreeze-spec-2026-08-08.md:155-173`). The kill then lands as 2-3
gunners inside d≤9 in a burst, **median 174 rounds later**, against a core with nobody left
to heal it (`:207-214`). **v76 deleted its home melee recall entirely (§5.10) — it has no
screen**, the one structure that produces that 12:1 attrition, and its forward-only gun cap
answers no d>13 standoff sentinel. Carry the caveat that the probe reproduced the attrition
and still won, so **D1 (no answer to the standoff sentinel), not attrition, is the causal
lever** (`ouro-v2-gap-decomposition-2026-08-08.md:43-79`) — v76 fails D1 too.

**Law 5 — forward snipe is hard-countered by three teams (AGAINST, but better-armed).**
KCM: median forward-sentinel lifetime **15 rounds**, answered by a gunner planted 1-3 tiles
away within 0-5 rounds, 7/7 cases; forward *gunners* die in **3-7 rounds**; "established ≥3:

v76 is the archetype these counters farm — with the three mitigations they were *not* tested
against (rebuild, ferry delivery, adjacent repair). **The sharpest thing to check is L4, the
map-conditional posture law**: `v72-bleed-cad-family:146-165` — **all 6 wins at core
separation d²≤81, 8 of 9 losses at d²≥144**, medians 64 (wins) vs 144 (losses), seat
irrelevant. If v76 runs nests on d²≥144 maps it inherits the loss side of that step function,
and **map separation should be a split variable in every v76 production read**.

**Law 6 — heal-war 4:1 (INTO).** "Heal is +4 HP for 1 Ti; a builder attack is 2 dmg for
2 Ti. A single healer beats two attackers on both HP and cost"
(`clankers-classification-2026-08-07.md:168-171`) — measured as a **4:1 exchange in the
healer's favour**, with 368 attacks killing only 3 conveyors
(`clankers-noconfound-2026-08-07.md:341-346`). v76's `_heal_battle_adjacent` (§2.3) puts this
law on *his* side of the nest: a defender who answers nests with **builder melee** loses the
exchange 4:1. **The counter is turret fire, not melee** — one gunner is 7 HP/round against a
repairer's +4, so a single adjacent repairer loses to a single counter-gunner and he needs
two bodies parked to hold a nest. That is the arithmetic our defensive answer should be built
on, and it converges with Law 1: **shoot the nests, don't peck them.**

**Law 7 — enemy launcher throws (conditional).** The standing gap: "**108 enemy throws, 15
builders lost, 0 adaptation**" (`orekeeper-v69-production-read-2026-08-07.md:382`), because

moves its own builders — which is benign to us. But `_launcher` also retains the v74 **exile

it runs **ahead of** the friendly throw. If his midfield launchers exile our builders at any
volume, we lose bodies with zero adaptation, which feeds Law 2's BODIES constraint directly.
**Worth counting in the first window** — it is the one place laws 2 and 7 compound.

swings, 8:1" — which v76 quotes back at us in its own `MELEE_FUTILE_HITS` comment
(v76:163-175) — does not appear as that triple in our corpus. The **865-swing tail is the

## 7. Production hooks for v76's first ladder window

Each hook: what to count, which `Update` events (per `tools/replay_schema.md`), and the
expected value if the mechanism works as written. Standing decode gotchas apply — dedupe
`placeEntity` by entity id (gunner rotations re-emit it), recompute per-turret damage from
`fireTurret` keyed by shooter, and remember **launcher throws emit no `fireTurret`** (they
appear as a `moveBuilderBot` whose `to` is >1 tile from the previous tile; attribute to a
launcher at d²≤2 of the pre-throw tile, diagonals included).

**P1 — Core-grind gate: does §5.1 fire?** *(highest value; tests the worst risk)*
Count `builderAttack` events whose `target` is an enemy Core tile, bucketed by round, and
overlay the round at which his cumulative turret `placeEntity` count (deduped, all turret
types, both nest and home) first reaches 2.
*If the gate works as intended*: Core pecks are ~0 until ≥2 nests stand simultaneously, then
resume. *If §5.1 is right*: Core pecks run continuously from the crossing round onward,
including in games where zero nests are alive — and total Core-directed `builderAttack`
count should be in the high hundreds, matching his own ~800 Ti / 400-swing forensic.

**P2 — E1 floor collapse.** Sum `coreConvertAmmo.amount` per team per game and plot the
running `Player.titanium` floor. Cross against live turret count (`placeEntity` minus
`removeEntity` per turret id).
*If §5.3 is right*: titanium repeatedly pinned near **12** rather than 46-52 in games where
his Core is undamaged and ours is not, and ammo conversion continuing while live turret count
is 0. Reference magnitude from the E1 forensics: 522-739 Ti of ammo over a game.

**P3 — Nest establishment and rebuild persistence.** *(the doctrine's own claim)*
For each gunner `placeEntity` at d²≤20 of our Core: record round, tile, and its
`removeEntity` round. Then count **distinct rebuilds on the same or adjacent tile**.
*If the doctrine works*: ≥2 nests standing simultaneously by ~r60-100, per-nest lifetime
short (his model expects them cleared), and **rebuild count per tile ≥ 3** — his cited
precedent is "Landers rebuilt one tile six times". *If it fails*: rebuild count ≈ 1 and the
`NEST_CAP` of 3 live is never reached.

**P4 — Heal-war retarget: does it ever fire?** *(tests §5.4)*
Identify rounds where his Core-adjacent turret fire (from `fireTurret`, shooter at d²≤32 of
our Core) targets a **builder bot** tile while our Core HP is above 150 and has been flat for
≥15 rounds (from `updateHp` on our Core id).
*Prediction from the read*: **near zero** distinct retarget episodes, because nests are
gunners and the rule only reorders the sentinel scan. Any non-trivial count falsifies §5.4 —
which is exactly why this is worth counting.

**P5 — Ferry throughput.** Count `moveBuilderBot` events with `|to − prev| > 1` for his team,
attribute to the launcher at d²≤2 of the pre-throw tile, and split by launcher id.
*If the ferry works*: **2 distinct launcher ids** with throws, both built before ~r30,
throws-per-game in the tens, and each thrown bot landing monotonically closer to our Core.
*If §5.5 bites*: only **one** launcher id ever throws. *If the midfield backfill (§2.2)
failed*: **zero** throws from the L2 launcher despite it standing — the exact M1-ancestor
symptom he fixed.

**P6 — Mine plugging.** Count `placeEntity` of `barrier` on tiles the map declares
`ENV_ORE_TITANIUM` in **our** half, by round. Then count how many of those ore tiles carried
one of our harvesters that was removed in the preceding ~3 rounds.
*If it works*: a handful per game from r25 on, with a visible drop in our
`titaniumCollected` slope. **This is the cheapest mechanism in the file (3 Ti) and the most
likely to actually pay** — worth its own row even if the count is small.

**P7 — Raider commitment / home exposure.** Track his builder bot positions per round; count
the fraction of his living builders at d²≤36 of **our** Core, and separately how many of his
builders ever return to within d²≤25 of **his own** Core after leaving.
*If the doctrine holds*: forward fraction rises monotonically and **returns ≈ 0**
(raiders never recall, §5.10). This is also the direct read on whether our own forward
economy or a home-side counterattack has an open lane.

**P8 — Jam handling.** Count his builder bots whose position is unchanged for ≥8 consecutive
rounds while not adjacent to a build/heal target, and the number of `moveBuilderBot` events
immediately following such a stall.
*If §2.7's three fixes work*: stalls resolve within ~2 rounds of crossing 8 and there are no
multi-bot piles persisting >20 rounds. *If not*: his own measured failure mode (a four-bot
gridlock standing 250 rounds) reappears — a free win condition for us on corridor maps.

**P9 — Lighthouse stack leak, both sides.** *(runs on existing replays, not the ladder
window — see §5.2 for the full method)* Decode `distributeResources` / `ResourceMove` on the
lighthouse rows of the existing hsd-vs-v76 bar leg and close the stack identity per side.
*Expected*: hsd reproduces ~27% (460/1,734); v76 leaks **non-zero but lower**, because
`_chain_dead` exists in his file and not in ours. A v76 figure at or above 27% would mean
`_chain_dead` does not touch this pathology; a figure near zero would make porting it a
priority for our line rather than a nice-to-have.

**Priority order for a first window**, given the 61.7% / 59%-core-kill tape: **P3 → P1 → P7**
decide whether the nests are actually the cause; **P4** is the tripwire for x3r0's next
version; **P9** is fix-worker input available today. P2, P5, P6, P8 are attribution detail.
