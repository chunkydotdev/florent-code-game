# v3 — What are the slot-swap rule's actual error rates?

**Date:** 2026-08-08 · **Author:** third session (main dir, not an arm)
**Data:** `elo_history.tsv` (560 rows → 295 distinct match-stamps, matches 127-429)
**Surfaces touched:** none. Read-only. Zero new matches.
**Method:** `v3_swap_rule.py` (observed + Monte Carlo) · raw: `v3-raw-output.txt`
**Prompted by:** the research arm's reframing — ask the *answerable* version
("do triggered windows recover on their own?") instead of the counterfactual one.

---

## Answer

**The slot-swap rule is not a safety control. It is a timer.**

`ship-gate.md` states the rule is "now the entire control on shipping fast" and
"must not drift." Measured: a **truly neutral** holder trips it **50.4% of the
time at match 8** and **96.6% by match 20**. A holder that is genuinely **+60
Elo better over 20 matches still trips it 78.6% of the time.** The rule cannot
distinguish a good bot from a bad one, and it fires on essentially everything
if you wait.

Observationally the trigger is **anti-predictive**: after it first fires, the
holder goes on to **gain** a mean **+6.5 Elo**, positive in 6 of 8 cases.

---

## 1. The rule, and why it is a coin flip by construction

Rule: rolling last-5 net Elo, arms at holder-match ≥ 8, **≤ 0 frees the slot.**

Measured per-match ladder Elo increment across 302 reconstructed matches:
**mean +0.82, sd 9.25** (min −19, max +19). So the rolling-5 sum has
**sd ≈ 20.7 Elo**.

At the first arming point the rule asks whether a roughly zero-mean 5-match sum
is ≤ 0. **That is a coin flip, analytically, before any data.** A real edge of
+2 Elo/match contributes +10 to a sum whose sd is 20.7 — under half a standard
deviation.

Worse, the rule **re-tests at every subsequent match with no multiplicity
correction**, so P(fire) → 1 with tenure length. This is the whole result.

## 2. Simulated false-positive rate (bootstrap, mean removed → true edge = 0)

| tenure length | P(rule frees the slot) |
|---|---|
| 8 | **50.4%** |
| 12 | 79.2% |
| 20 | **96.6%** |
| 30 | 99.6% |
| 50 | 100.0% |

## 3. Discrimination — the number that condemns it

P(fires) at a 20-match tenure, by the holder's **true** per-match edge:

| true edge | ≈ Elo over 20 | P(fires) |
|---|---|---|
| −3.0 | −60 | 99.8% |
| −1.0 | −20 | 98.5% |
| 0.0 | 0 | 96.7% |
| +1.0 | +20 | 93.0% |
| +2.0 | +40 | 87.1% |
| **+3.0** | **+60** | **78.6%** |

**A 120-Elo swing in true quality moves the fire rate by 21 points.** A control
that fires on 99.8% of terrible bots and 78.6% of excellent ones is not
selecting on quality.

## 4. Observed behaviour on the real tape

15 of 37 holders reached 8 matches (the rule can arm). **11 of those 15 crossed
the trigger.**

| holder | n | 1st trigger | # triggers | recovered | Elo after trigger | matches after |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| v53 | 45 | 15 | 18 | yes | −5.0 | 30 |
| v61 | 9 | 8 | 2 | no | +4.0 | 1 |
| v63 | 8 | 8 | 1 | no | +0.0 | 0 |
| v64 | 12 | 8 | 1 | yes | **+18.0** | 4 |
| v67 | 8 | 8 | 1 | no | +0.0 | 0 |
| v68 | 19 | 8 | 9 | yes | −16.0 | 11 |
| v72 | 26 | 8 | 8 | yes | **+21.0** | 18 |
| v74 | 14 | 8 | 4 | yes | +6.0 | 6 |
| v75 | 14 | 8 | 7 | no | +5.0 | 6 |
| v76 | 8 | 8 | 1 | no | +0.0 | 0 |
| v80 | 23 | 11 | 4 | yes | **+19.0** | 12 |

- **Spontaneous recovery: 6/11 = 55%** (rolling-5 returns positive under the
  same holder, no swap).
- **Net Elo from first trigger to end of tenure: mean +6.5, median +5.5,
  6 of 8 positive.**

**Honest caveat, and it matters:** three of the eleven (v63, v67, v76) had zero
matches after the trigger, so "did not recover" is true by construction — they
were replaced. The recovery question genuinely applies to 8 tenures, and 6 of
those 8 went positive. That is the number to quote, not 6/11.

Tonight supplied a live instance the builder already logged: v80's armed window
crossed to −12, recovered on its own within the hour, then crossed again — no
swap either time.

## 5. Recalibration, if the rule is kept in its current shape

Rolling-5 sum sd = 20.7 Elo. Thresholds, by false-positive rate:

| threshold | FPR @ m=8 | FPR @ m=20 | FPR @ m=40 | P(fire) on a genuinely +2/match bot |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **0 (current)** | **50.4%** | **96.6%** | **100.0%** | **87.1%** |
| −20 | 17.3% | 67.7% | 93.1% | 42.0% |
| −30 | 7.7% | 41.8% | 72.8% | 19.6% |
| −50 | 0.8% | 6.6% | 15.3% | 1.5% |

**But read the FPR@40 column before adopting any of these.** Even at −40 the
rule fires on 41% of neutral long tenures, because the defect is not the
threshold — it is **re-testing every match with no multiplicity correction.**
A fixed threshold only slows the inevitable.

**The real fix is the domain-native one we already use locally: a sequential
test with explicit bounds and error rates.** Fishtest runs SPRT on Elo bounds
[elo0, elo1] with α=β=0.05, which controls error across the whole sequence
rather than per-look. `tools/sprt.py` already implements the machinery for
local legs; a ladder-side version with bounds like [−10, 0] Elo would give the
rule an actual, statable false-positive rate for the first time.

Interim, zero-code option: **raise the arming point.** The rule's damage is
concentrated at m=8, where it is a literal coin flip.

## 6. What this means next to v1

These two entries interlock, and together they describe the current regime:

- **v1:** the local gate has 19% power — it cannot resolve a plank.
- **v3:** the ladder control fires on ~everything — it cannot catch a bad ship.

`ship-gate.md` traded local rigor for ladder safety on 2026-08-08. That trade
was reasonable on the information available and it broke a real deadlock. **The
measurement says both sides of the trade are underpowered**: shipping moved
from an instrument that could not resolve +5pp onto a control that cannot tell
+60 Elo from −60. The safety was never where the ledger said it was.

This is not an argument to stop shipping fast. It is an argument that **the
rollback trigger is currently firing at random**, and that the ~1-hour ladder
window it consumes is being spent on coin flips.

---

## Falsifiable predictions

1. Applying the current rule to any future holder will trigger by match 20 in
   ≈97% of tenures, regardless of that version's quality.
2. Of triggers that are not acted on, ≈55% will show the rolling-5 return
   positive under the same holder.
3. Raising the threshold to −30 will cut trigger frequency at m=8 by roughly
   6× while still catching a genuinely −3/match holder ~1 time in 2 by m=20.

## Caveats

- Ratings on the tape are integers and multi-match gaps are spread evenly
  across the missing matches, which **understates** per-match variance. A
  higher true sd makes every false-positive number above **worse**, not better.
- 302 increments across 37 versions; the increment distribution is pooled
  across opponents and eras, so it mixes regimes.
- Section 4's sample is 11 tenures. Section 2-3 and 5 do not depend on it.
