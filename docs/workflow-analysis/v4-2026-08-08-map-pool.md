**Date:** 2026-08-08 · **Measured by: the research arm** (session `284161ab`),
commissioned from this series' queue. Recorded here because it closes a v2/v5
hypothesis and revises v1's item 1; the primary deliverable is theirs.
**Data:** 2,891 archived ladder games fingerprinted on (dims, cores, ore count,
wall count) against the 15 local maps. Zero downloads.

## Consequences

1. **Closed as a null; do NOT merge with the Lunds zero-cell structure.** The
   research arm's conditional — *if* the pools differ, their per-(map, seat)
   zero-cells are one symptom of measuring the wrong distribution — resolves to
   *they don't differ*. The zero-cells are a genuine performance fact, and
   merging the two would blur separate claims.
2. **Removes a hypothesis from v2/v5 for free.** Local↔ladder distribution
   mismatch is not a candidate explanation for gate/ladder divergence. Whatever
   makes local gates unpredictive, it is not that we play the wrong maps.
3. **`program.md`'s standing warning is discharged.** It says "the maps are
   ours, not the organisers' — treat map-specific tuning as untrustworthy until
   `fcode maps sync` has run." Measured: the pools match. Map-specific tuning is
   trustworthy, and v1's item 7 (hold out maps before racing) becomes *more*
   defensible, not less — a held-out local map is a genuine held-out ladder map.
4. **Operational, and worth acting on tonight:** hive and fjordgate are the two
   **rarest** draws at 6.0%. Work targeted at hive defects is aimed at the
   least-drawn map in the pool — a downward correction to its expected value
   that is much cheaper to apply before a ship than to discover after one.

## A6 — what this does to v1's item 1 (and it resolves an apparent contradiction)

v1 item 1 says "raise the standard leg to n≈800." A5 says "seeds buy almost
nothing; the efficient axes are maps and opponents." v4 now says maps are
capped at 15 and already fully exploited by a standard battery. Read together
those look like they collapse the recommendation. **They do not, because the
two leg types have different ceilings and the distinction has not been stated
anywhere:**

| leg type | what one extra match buys | effective n ceiling |
|---|---|---|
| **NOISE_ON (stochastic confirm)** | an **independent draw** — `spawn_salt` comes from live entropy, so the seed does not even control the game | **none.** nominal n = effective n. Raise it freely. |
| **NOISE_OFF (deterministic screen)** | a new configuration only if (map, seat, opponent) is new | **≈ 15 maps × 2 seats × n_opponents = 30 per opponent.** Seeds buy nothing. |

So:

- **v1 item 1 stands unmodified.** n≈800 on a NOISE_ON confirm leg is 800
  genuine observations at 22 minutes. There is no shape ceiling there, and A5
  does not bind it.
- **v1 item 3 (det as screening gate) is more constrained than I wrote**, and
  the arms were right to defer it. A deterministic screen against one opponent
  tops out near **30 effective observations** no matter how many matches are
  run. That is a hard ceiling, not a budget question.
- **Therefore the binding constraint on deterministic screening is opponent
  diversity** — and that is the axis we are poorest in, since every local
  opponent is a teammate lineage or a hand-extracted probe, and `tooling.md`
  records that pulling opponents off the platform is structurally unobtainable.

**The one-line version: raising n works for stochastic confirms and does not
work for deterministic screens.** Anyone who reads A5 as "stop raising n" has
over-generalised a deterministic-leg fact onto the stochastic leg where it is
simply false.

## A7 — the distinct-shape counter detects precondition violations, and the reading is inverted

Raised by the research arm, **verified here against `bots/` directly.**

`det.py` states as an *unenforced* precondition that all sides must be
deterministic. Census of every opponent reference:

| opponent references | `NOISE_ON` |
|---|---|
| `opp_v67, v68, v69, v72, v74, v76, v78` — **every modern one** | **`= True`** |
| `opp_v39, v44, v45, v49, v50, v56, v58, v63`, `starter`, all 15 probes | *(no symbol)* |

This resolves an anomaly that was sitting in plain sight. The builder's HG
effect leg vs `opp_v78` reported **120/120 distinct shapes** — impossible for a
genuinely deterministic leg, whose ceiling is 15 maps × 2 seats = **30**. It was
never a well-powered det leg. **It was a stochastic leg wearing `det.py`'s
clothes**, because the opponent reseeds `spawn_salt` from live entropy every
match. The precondition was violated and nothing enforced it.

> **The reading of the shape counter is the opposite of the natural one.** A
> shape count near 1.0 on a "det" leg is evidence the leg is **stochastic**,
> not evidence it is well-powered. 44/120 vs `opp_v63` is what a genuine det
> leg looks like — seeds collapsing, exactly as A5 predicts.

This also means the research arm's 15%-power reading of the HG refutation
stands **for the right reason**: stochastic sampling variance was the correct
model, because the leg was in fact stochastic.

### A7b — flagless is not deterministic. Measure it.

`starter` carries **no `NOISE_ON` symbol** and `det.py`'s own docstring records
it as **NOT deterministic**. So absence of the flag proves nothing, and the
eight flagless `opp_*` dirs are *candidates*, not a verified pool.

Which gives a cheap, concrete way to widen the only axis that matters for
deterministic screening:

- **Verified deterministic opponents today: one** (`opp_v63`, the measured
  reference). Det screening therefore caps near **30 effective observations
  total** — not per opponent we might add.
- **Eight flagless pre-v67 opponents are unverified candidates.** Verifying one
  costs **two matches**: run the same (map, seed, seat) twice and check for an
  identical end state. The whole set is ~16 matches, single-digit minutes.
- If even half verify, the det ceiling rises from ~30 to ~120 — **the cheapest
  available increase in deterministic screening power**, and it needs no new
  code.

> **Series rule 4: absence of a noise flag is not evidence of determinism.
> Determinism is a measured property, and `starter` is the standing
> counterexample.**

## Method note

The research arm's first run of this reported **"0% of archived games are on
local-pool maps"** — a null that would have been explosive and was entirely an
artefact: field 1 is `width` in a `.map26` (where the top-level message *is*
`Map`) but a `Map` **submessage** in a `.replay26`, so a fingerprinter written
for map files and fed replay files read a nested message as a width. Third
silent decoder fault of the evening. It was caught by contradiction against an
earlier census that had successfully identified maps *by name* — if 100% were
non-pool, that census could not have worked.

This is the evidence behind the series' rule 3 (see README): **state what a
wrong parse would look like**, and note that all three catches came from having
an independently-derived number to contradict, not from care.
