What each part of the game does

Two teams, one grid, one resource. You write a single Player.run(ct) which the engine calls once per round for every living unit you own — the core, every builder bot, and every turret.

There is no shared game object. Everything is read through the unit-scoped Controller. Its methods act on this unit unless you pass an explicit entity id. Branch on ct.get_entity_type() at the top of run() — that is the whole architecture.

How you win

Primary Destroy the enemy core. That is the only win this programme counts. R1000_IS_DEFEAT: yes — a round-1000 tiebreak win is recorded as a defeat even when the ladder pays for it.
If r1000 hits Tiebreakers in order: titanium collected → harvesters alive → titanium stored → coinflip. Key 1 decides ~94% of them.
The trap “Collected” means DELIVERED TO THE CORE, not mined and not emitted. Engine-probed: a harvester alive all game with no conveyor route home scores 0. Passive income also counts 0. A harvester with no route home is worth nothing on the deciding key, forever.

The board

Grid8×8 to 30×30, symmetric by reflection or rotation.
TilesEMPTY, WALL (blocks building), ORE_TITANIUM (the only place a harvester may be built).
Compass(0,0) is the north-west corner; x grows east, y grows south — so NORTH is (0, −1). Builder bots may only move in the 4 cardinal directions; all 8 are legal for facing and orientation.

The two economies

Titanium
500 to start
+10 passive every 4 rounds. Pays for every build.
Ammunition
0 to start
No passive income at all. The core converts Ti → ammo 1:1.
Two separate things share the word “titanium”. There is the global pool that pays build costs, and there are physical stacks of 10 that move through the map on conveyors. Turrets never touch either — they fire from the global ammo balance, so they never need feeding.

convert_ammo(amount): at most once per team per turn, usable the same turn, and it does not consume the core's action cooldown — so converting never costs you a spawn.

Cost scaling is ONE GLOBAL ADDITIVE FACTOR, not per-category. Engine-confirmed. It starts at 1.0 and every build adds to the same number, which then inflates the price of every entity type. Spawning only builder bots drove the factor 100%→200% and raised the price of conveyors and harvesters that were never built. Destroying your own building removes its contribution.

Consequences: the gunner:sentinel price ratio is pinned 2:3 at every scale, and each live builder bot has already added +20% before you buy a single turret. Always use the get_*_cost() getters — never a hardcoded base cost.

What a builder bot can do

One action per turn, cooldown-gated. Acting and moving are mutually exclusive — doing one blocks the other until next turn.

buildAny building type on an orthogonally adjacent empty tile.
attack2 Ti → 2 damage to the building on an adjacent tile.
heal1 Ti → +4 HP to all friendly entities on an adjacent tile.
destroyAny allied building on an adjacent tile. Free, no cooldown, unlimited per turn.
self_destructDestroys the unit. Deals no explosion damage — it is not a weapon.

The delivery pipeline

Conveyors, splitters and harvesters form a purely economic chain into the core. Resource distribution happens once at end of round, after every unit has acted. Turrets never participate and never hold resources.

The round-robin is team-blind. An enemy conveyor adjacent to your harvester is a full-rank acceptor — an unwired harvester beside an enemy belt gives roughly half its output away, and titanium is credited to whoever owns the destination core. Pushing resources into an enemy core is a gift, not a poison.

Limits that bite

Unit cap
50
Per team, including the core. get_unit_count().
CPU per unit / turn
10 ms
Overrun interrupts that turn only. The unit survives.
Comms store
16
Integer slots, per team. Writes are buffered — visible next round.
An uncaught exception is permanent death. If anything other than a CPU timeout escapes run(), the engine prints the traceback and destroys that unit for the rest of the match. A CPU timeout does not. SystemExit and KeyboardInterrupt are the only exemptions.

This is not just a hazard — it is a weapon. It is the mechanism behind crash induction: throw an enemy builder somewhere its own code cannot handle and it kills itself with its own error handling. See the Lingo page.

Guard asymmetries worth remembering

can_launch No team check and no vision guard. You may pick up an enemy builder.
is_in_vision(pos) Returns False off-map — it does not raise. The safe pre-check.
get_tile_*(pos) Raises off-map. This is the crash surface.
can_fire Returns True at 0 ammo. The real check lives in the firing path and raises — which destroys your own turret. A hazard to us, not a lever on them.
Gunner vs sentinel They do not share a targeting predicate. Gunner can_fire_from is False on every empty tile; sentinel returns True on the same tiles. Any code or analysis treating them as one predicate is wrong for one of them.
