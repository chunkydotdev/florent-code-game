Lingo

The words we use in commits, flags and read-outs. Type to filter.

Running an experiment

arm
One variant in a comparison. Usually the parent bot plus one flag flipped, so any difference is attributable to that flag.
shard
One arm's local battery process, filling a .tsv row per game. What the Cores page counts.
battery
A block of local self-play games between two arms. Free and unlimited — nothing rations local cores but attention.
leg
A run of live games against a real opponent, via fcode match unrated. Costs rated exposure, because unrated matches play whatever submission is currently active.
cell
One (opponent × map × seat) combination in a leg or panel. A cell that cannot express the treatment measures nothing.
panel
A fixed set of opponents a leg is fired at.
ablation
Turning off one part of a shipped plank to find out which part was doing the work. “We shipped a 61% we cannot attribute.”
plank
A single idea being tested — one behaviour change with a metric attached.

Pre-registration

prereg
A committed file, written before the run, stating the bar, the estimator, the planned n and what would falsify the idea. Its whole value is that it predates the data.
two-clock standard
The proof a prereg really came first: its git author time must precede the platform's createdAt for the first match of the leg. Two independent clocks, neither of which we control.
ADD-only amendment
An amendment may only add a constraint, or fix a rule whose inputs do not exist yet. Anything that loosens, retargets or reinterprets an existing bar is a new prereg and must say so.
falsifier
The result that would kill the idea, written down before it can be argued with.
look schedule
Pre-committed points at which a filling arm may be scored. Progress can be watched any time; letting the moment you happened to look decide the verdict is the fault.

Measurement and its traps

dose
How often the mechanism actually fired. A precondition, not a result — a rate read on an arm that never fired is not a null, it is a non-experiment.
null cell
An arm byte-identical to its control. It must read ~50%. If it does not, the fixture is broken and no other number on it means anything.
NEG cell
A known-bad arm. It must read clearly below 50%. Together with the null it proves the fixture can move in both directions.
positive control
A case where the answer is already known, run to prove the instrument works. A check that has never produced the other verdict has not been seen to check.
band
The noise interval at a given n. At n=5,408 it is ±1.33pp; at n=2,704, ±1.88pp. Simply 1.96 × √(0.25/n).
inside-band
A result too close to 50% to mean anything ⇒ no ship. Unbiased per decision and biased in aggregate: a weak fixture lands inside-band systematically, and inside-band always resolves for whoever already holds the slot.
MDE
Minimum detectable effect — the smallest difference a given n could ever resolve. A null below the MDE means “no power”, never “no effect”, and those imply opposite actions.
optional stopping
Peeking repeatedly at a filling arm and stopping when it looks good. Inflates the false-positive rate, is invisible in a fixed-n z, and cannot be corrected afterwards unless the look schedule was recorded.
multiplicity
Many arms tested at once, so some look good by chance. A different fault from optional stopping, which is many looks at one arm — the corrections are not interchangeable.
O'Brien-Fleming
An interim boundary that is deliberately much stricter than the final one, so an early look only stops on a large effect and the overall error rate stays at 5%.
paired / unpaired
Paired = both arms play the identical (map, seed, seat) cell, so only the treatment differs. Strictly more powerful, and free on a deterministic engine — but it needs NOISE_ON = False or the cells are not reproducible.
echo loop
Testing against bots we wrote ourselves. They lie in a known direction, which is why live games are the fixture of record.
fixture of record
live_unrated — real opponents. Local batteries may prioritise a road; only live games may retire one.

The ladder and shipping

game share
Games won ÷ 5. The thing the ladder actually pays, so it is the primary currency. A 3–2 win can lose rating and a 2–3 loss can gain it.
Elo delta
32 × (S − E) where S is game share and E the logistic expectation from the rating gap. Verified exactly, max residual 0.000000.
holder
The submission currently active on the ladder. Read it from the live Active bot: line — a document naming it is a cache.
incumbent
The bot tree the holder was built from. PROGRAMME.md names it, and every new arm is controlled against it.
ourver / oppver
Per-match version tags. The ground truth for “which bot actually played this match” — the rating tape tags rows by poll time and is structurally blind to it.
leak
A rated match played by something other than the intended holder — usually because a prototype was activated for an unrated leg and the ladder paired us mid-window. Budgeted at roughly −8 Elo per leaked match.
submitting IS shipping
fcode submit auto-activates what it uploads. There is no “upload now, activate later”. The upload belongs inside the firing window or not at all.
slot rule
The stop-loss on a live holder: while armed at k≥8 matches, net5 ≤ −21 frees the slot. It frees, it never forces.
net5
Rating now minus rating five matches ago — a rolling slope, not a level. It relaxes as a bad result ages out, so a bleed slower than −4.2/match never trips it, forever.
drawdown
Peak-to-current rating. Sees level where net5 only sees slope — which is why both are printed.
p_null
The chance the slot rule fires on a holder that is doing nothing at all: 14% by k=8, 75% by k=27, 97% by k=60. A fire is close to uninformative; “never fired” is the informative half.
converge
A rating needs ~100 matches to settle. With ~420 rated matches left in the game, that is about four ship-and-converge cycles total.
rc naming
Ships are Loki vN; unrated legs are Loki rcX.Y. The name says which kind of thing is on the slot.

Bot behaviour

salt
Cut an enemy conveyor with builder melee, then barrier the dead tile so the cut sticks. The field repairs 40% of bare cuts within ~4 rounds, so the barrier is the point.
raid
The forward-offence module — everything we do in enemy territory.
ferry / insert
Using our own launcher to throw our own builder forward, past the walk. Exile is the same throw aimed at removing something.
kidnap
Throwing an enemy builder — legal because can_launch has no team check.
crash induction
Manoeuvring an enemy unit somewhere its own code raises — a map border, or off a cached plan. Their exception handling destroys the unit permanently. Costs us zero ammo and one throw. Asked of the organisers and approved.
seat
A tile adjacent to a core — where builders stand to heal it, and what a raider denies by standing there.
ring
The 12 tiles around a 2×2 core. The core needs exactly one free ring tile to spawn.
trunk / belt
The main conveyor line from a harvester back to the core. A gap anywhere in it delivers nothing.
collar
Buildings ringing our own core — protective, and they block our own line of sight.
forward vs home
Which half of the map a turret was built in. Pooling the two inverts the sign of our biggest gap — home we are fine, forward we are eight rounds late and half the count.
LOKI-N
Numbered ideas on the Loki line. Loki is the trickster bot: cheap tricks, manipulation, poisoning, exploits.
bodyaware (plank)
The builder pathfinder's BFS treats other builder bots ("bodies", either team) as soft obstacles distinct from walls, buildings and turrets: a first pass routes around bodies too, and only if that finds no path does a second pass ignore bodies and route through them, since a body can move out of the way and a wall cannot. Exists to break two-tile navigation locks where builders freeze pathing around each other. No master toggle — it is a permanent change inside eco.py's _bfs_direction, not a flag. Solo screen: DONE at 53.70% (±0.94pp, n=10,801). (bots/_v242bodyaware, eco.py:809)
rnd1 (plank)
Lifts the round-gate on building the HOME launcher. The parent's LAUNCHER_MIN_RND=160 was priced only on cost (a launcher adds +10% to the global scale factor), never against the opportunity it excludes — the comments measure 53.7% of games seeing an enemy builder plant a turret within d²≤8 of our core, 73.3% of those before round 160, against our own median actual launcher build of round 318. LOKI_HOMEEARLY_RND (set to 1 in this tree) replaces the r160 floor, and the comments flag a known non-zero price for doing so: roughly 2.8pp measured on a different chassis. Solo screen: DONE at 51.29% (±1.33pp, n=5,401). (bots/_v259rnd1, doctrine.py:1538)
spawnlock (plank)
Sites a home launcher directly on one of the enemy core's 12 spawn tiles (CORE_SPAWNING_RADIUS_SQ=2), combining two mechanisms: it evicts every enemy builder that walks up to heal or rebuild (0 ammo, fires every round), and — verified by disassembly, can_spawn calls is_bot_passable — the building itself is then permanent spawn denial on that tile. Bounded by LOKI_SPAWNLOCK_NEAR_DSQ=8 (the build tile must sit this close to a core tile) and gated on LOKI_SPAWNLOCK_TI_FLOOR=40 bank left after paying for it. Solo screen: CANCELLED at 49.68% (±1.62pp, n=3,647). (bots/_v252spawnlock, doctrine.py:1267)
bodyblock (plank)
Gives an idle-and-free builder — measured rising from 3.31% of builder-rounds at r10 to 29.01% in the last 20 rounds before our core dies — a terminal fallback instead of doing nothing: it melees an adjacent enemy conveyor/splitter (2 Ti for 2 dmg), or relocates toward a named destination on the enemy's half via exact BFS, refusing to re-tread a recently visited tile and standing still rather than take a stale step back into a lock. Exists because is_bot_passable is team-blind, so an idle body can physically block enemy movement too. Solo screen: CANCELLED at 47.24% (±1.64pp, n=3,575). (bots/_v262bodyblock, doctrine.py:1690)
catapult (plank)
A launcher sited close to home (LOKI_CATAPULT_D2_HOME=8 from our own core) that a spawning builder deliberately waits to be picked up by, instead of the parent ferry's opportunistic "only throw if the walk happens to cross the pickup ring." The wait is bounded per body by LAUNCH_STALL_RNDS=36 rounds, after which it gives up and walks. One throw moves a builder ~5 tiles instantly at 0 ammo, against 1 tile per round walking, and the launcher fires every round. Solo screen: CANCELLED at 47.21% (±2.92pp, n=1,129) — the comments themselves flag this as a coverage question, since the round gate (LAUNCHER_MIN_RND=160) still leaves most launchers built well after the median r318, deliberately left to the rnd1 plank to fix. (bots/_v253catapult, doctrine.py:1278)
collarseal (plank)
Changes what an arrived raider does at the enemy core. Measured over 157,816 throws: at landing d²≤8, 61.4% of raiders reach it but only 1.55% ever land a hit on the core itself, median life 5 rounds — not enough to matter against 500 HP. So instead of attacking, the raider builds barriers on the core's 12-tile spawn ring, which is permanent spawn denial (can_spawn gated on tile passability, and destroy is team-checked so the enemy cannot clear their own ring). A two-line change to the parent's existing barrier-seal step: lifted above the core-peck branch and widened from 8 heal seats to all 12 spawn tiles. (bots/_v254collarseal, doctrine.py:1231)
pinaim (plank)
Changes where the launcher's EXILE throw sends an intruding enemy builder. The parent aims for the site farthest from our own core, which is self-defeating at a home launcher — it hands the victim safety and removes it from the pickup ring. This plank aims at a tile diagonal to the launcher instead (d²=2), which a builder bot cannot attack back from since builder melee is orthogonal-only, so the intruder gets picked up again next round and every round after, for free. The comments note the victim still escapes the landing tile 96.4% of the time within one round, so this is priced as repeated displacement, not a kill, and falls back to the parent's farthest-aim when no diagonal pin site is legal. (bots/_v251pinaim, doctrine.py:1289)
sentshell (plank)
Surrounds a forward sentinel with 3 barriers on its orthogonal neighbours, aimed at a measured 6x lifetime gap between our forward sentinels (median 17 rounds) and the field's (104). By disassembly, a sentinel's own ray-walk is obstacle-blind so the shell costs the sentinel nothing to fire through, but it blocks the gunner fire responsible for 67.1% of our sentinels' final blows, blocks builder melee (attack is Manhattan-1), and blocks launcher kidnap (can_launch checks destination passability, and only conveyors/splitters pass — barriers do not). Costs 9 Ti / +3pp scale on top of the sentinel's own 30 Ti / +20pp, roughly 30% overhead. Solo screen: CANCELLED at 48.70% (±2.98pp, n=1,078). (bots/_v273sentshell, doctrine.py:1689)

Infrastructure

corefill
The runner that keeps local cores busy from a worklist. It launches in file order, which has silently made priority decisions before.
keeper
The background process that pulls new matches, archives replays and decodes them into the corpus.
ladder_games.tsv
Our rated record, one row per game. The right surface for any population or denominator question.
meta_join.tsv
Per-replay attribution across all archived games. Missing ~38% of ladder matches and full of sparse columns — never use it for a win-rate denominator.
league_matches.tsv
The whole league's match history, including opponents' version timelines. The only place to pin what bot the other side was actually running.
events.tsv
Decoded per-event wire data — the only surface that can see barriers. builds.tsv is a turret census and reports zero barriers for everyone.
cores_idle
The alarm that fires when nothing is running locally. It names the next queue item, so the alarm carries its own remedy.
ship_watch
Polls the live holder and prints the slot rule's state, the drawdown and the age of every source it read.

How we work

lanes
Parallel sessions: builder (writes bots, runs batteries, ships), research (analysis, keeps the queue stocked), side lane (drift watch and prereg discipline — no bot edits, no verdicts), and occasionally a meta lane reviewing the structure itself.
drift watch
Every commit from every lane audited against a numbered checklist (D1…D32). A flag carries its anchor; it is never a veto.
D-number / Q-number
D = a drift-watch rule. Q = a question in a lane's own retro. Both are numbered so they can be cited rather than re-argued.
QUEUE.md
The build queue, in fire order. A row counts only if it carries a GREP: naming what was checked in the incumbent and what was found.
GREP gate
The admission rule above. It proves a check was run — not that its result is still true, which is a different and unguarded thing.
ALWAYS_BE_RUNNING
Idle local cores are a defect. Games are free and unlimited; only the platform is rate-limited.
IN-FLIGHT
The registry in coordination.md where a lane announces a subagent before spawning it, so nobody duplicates work.
